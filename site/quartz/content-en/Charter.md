*“Absolute truth above all”*

The Charter of the community “Faith in Wisdom” answers the questions: **what we carry and by what rules we live.**

## What the Charter Is

**Definition.** A charter is the written constitution of a community: it **forms the system**. Our Charter is the rules of life for the community of those who have faith in wisdom and follow the path of wisdom. The generating order: the goal—from the goal, the rules—the rules specify the roles; the charter sets this road down so that it stands without depending on memory or arbitrariness ([[Theory of Systems]]).

**The goal.** Every charter begins with a goal. **The goal of one who has faith in wisdom is the capacity to find absolute truth and to understand it (to follow). The goal of the path of wisdom is to carry wisdom: to help others see absolute truth and understand it.** Hence the two names are two sides of one community: “Faith in Wisdom” is who we are, and “the Path of Wisdom” is how we walk; hence also the two tasks of the roles of the [[Code of Ethics|Code]]: the follower’s, to find truth rightly; the philosopher’s, to carry wisdom.

**Components of the goal**—each stands on a derived base:

- **Capacity** is a graded scale; the sufficient reason for starting on the path is an act of will, a desire ([[Metaphysics|will]]); and will is never zero—entry onto the path is open to everyone ([[What Is Not and Cannot Be|What Is Not, and Cannot Be]]);
- **To find** is to determine: to draw the boundary *A* | not-*A* ([[Logic|the act of distinction]]);
- **Absolute truth** is objective truth: how exactly things are ([[Truth and Absolute Truth]]);
- **To understand** is to see the structure and its interrelations ([[Truth and Absolute Truth#The Rungs of Representation|the rungs of representation]]; [[Theory of Systems|the analysis of a system]]);
- **To help see** is to show, not to impose ([[Ethics|virtue toward another]]);
- **To help understand** is to explain, not to persuade: persuasion produces opinion, explanation produces understanding ([[Truth and Absolute Truth#The Rungs of Representation|the rungs of representation]]).

**The task of the Charter** is to set out the rules that guide one on the path of wisdom—for those who are ready to carry wisdom and to be united in a community. A follower is bound by nothing but an inner attunement: the whole corpus is given as help, [[Ethics|ethics]] included. One becomes a member of the **Guild of Philosophers** through initiation—the Oath of Maat; initiation gives the role of a member of the Guild, and thereby of a philosopher (Article 2.3). The rules of the Charter outline the field that corresponds to the role of a philosopher and of a member of the Guild: to leave the field is to leave the role, and this leads to the loss of status.

**“Faith” in the name.** The Russian word for faith, *vera*, shares a root with Latin *verus* “true” and *veritas* “truth”: a holding to the true, fidelity. The faith of the community is fidelity to the ground—an attunement to absolute truth—not opinion without grounds; belief as a status and fidelity are told apart in [[Truth and Absolute Truth#The Rungs of Representation|the rungs of representation]].

## Article 1. Founding

The community “Faith in Wisdom” is hereby established. It is established by its founder, who is also its first Leader, by virtue of founding (Article 4.1). The task of the community is **to carry wisdom**: to help others see absolute truth and understand it—in accordance with the [[Code of Ethics]].

## Article 2. Membership

- **2.1.** Levels: listener (not a member) · follower · philosopher (one who carries wisdom).
- **2.2.** Follower: a promise to oneself to follow Truth—an inner attunement, the only thing that binds the one who walks; the condition of membership in the community is a quarterly donation (of any amount) to the Treasury. Entry is immediate.
- **2.3.** A philosopher is one who is able to analyze order and who carries wisdom; a role of capacity ([[Philosophy]]). A philosopher need not be a member of the Guild of Philosophers, but every member of the Guild must be a philosopher. Entry into the Guild is **initiation**: the **Oath of Maat**—to carry wisdom (Maat is the Egyptian name for truth, justice, and cosmic order); initiation gives the role of a member of the Guild of Philosophers, and thereby of a philosopher. Admission to initiation follows a probationary period (ready to carry, not only to follow); the oath is taken before all the members of the Guild who can be present. The philosopher serves Wisdom.
- **2.4.** Other affiliations are permitted as long as nothing is placed above absolute truth (the Law of Non-Contradiction).
- **2.5.** Withdrawal is free, at one’s own wish—without return to the former status; re-entry is at the first step: follower. For one who has broken the Oath, the way into the Guild is closed.
- **2.6.** Expulsion is for a proven violation of the Charter (Article 6); at the first stage, also by decision of the Leader (6.5).

## Article 3. Structure

The structure of the community rests on places of three kinds—all are specified by the rules and differ in what they hold and how they are filled: a **role** holds service and is filled by a calling, from within; a **function** holds work and is filled by appointment or by a mechanism; a **position** holds coordination and is filled by appointment and, as the community grows, by nomination and election (Article 4).

- **3.1.** Roles (filled by a calling): **Teacher**—group meetings; **Mentor**—personal guidance. A calling is the inner call of the will to a role of service: it is heard, not appointed; it is tested by doing—fitness is the outcome of the path.
- **3.2.** Functions (appointment or mechanism): **Judge**, **Steward**; each is headed by a Counselor; each lives by its own **rulebook** within the Charter.
- **3.3.** Positions: **Senior Philosopher** (about one for every twelve; organizes a group and coordinates) · **Counselor** (head of a function) · **Leader** (Head of the Guild of Philosophers and of the whole community).
- **3.4.** The duty of every member of the Guild: to carry wisdom · to keep the Oath · the discipline of grounds · to show the logic · to protect the one who is pressured.
- **3.5.** The inner structure of those who carry—the initiated members—is the **Guild of Philosophers**. Absolute truth above all.
- **3.6.** Secondary affiliation: a function is a secondary calling (the primary one is to Truth); functions are the seed of future Guilds, at the core of whose governance stand members of the Guild of Philosophers.

## Article 4. Governance

- **4.1.** The Leader: the first is the founder, by virtue of founding (Article 1). At the first stage decisions are made by the Leader—including the formation of new internal systems and the organization of decision-making processes; mechanisms of election and voting are relative to the systems they serve and are introduced as the community grows. The Leader is a philosopher like any other, follows the same rules, and is subject to judgment (Article 6).
- **4.2.** Counselors—heads of functions—are appointed by the Leader; an unfilled function is performed by the Leader until someone responsible is appointed.
- **4.3.** The format of a decision follows the nature of the question: a question of truth is decided by analysis as demonstration (**a vote does not decide truth**); matters of work and coordination are decided by the word of a position, always with an explanation by sufficient reason.
- **4.4.** Openness by default; closure only by sufficient reason; the grounds for closure and the outcome are open.
- **4.5.** Changes to the Charter: **correction**—upon a proven logical violation; a change of free forms—by the Leader. There is no interpretation (“revision”): logic is explained and demonstrated.
- **4.6.** The channel for followers is to turn to a philosopher for consultation.

## Article 5. Community Life

The forms of community life—lectures, analyses, debates, group formats—take shape organically and are written down as the community grows; at the first stage they are set by the Leader (Article 4.1). Listeners come to open events. Obligations are borne only by one who has taken a role: a member of the Guild, by the Oath; one who has taken a function, by its rulebook; a follower is bound only by an inner attunement (Article 2).

## Article 6. Analysis of Failures

- **6.1.** An accusation is brought by the one affected. Until proof—the presumption of innocence.
- **6.2.** Proof is by analysis; the court is a single judge; a panel sits by sufficient reason (the seriousness of the case).
- **6.3.** Measures follow the kind of failure: harm → restitution (the judge sets the amount) · imposition → suspension from functions until correction, with a public explanation of why it is wrong · a proven lie → expulsion from the community (loss of membership).
- **6.4.** Acknowledgment is made to the judge and to the one affected: it removes punishment; restitution remains.
- **6.5.** At the first stage the Leader may suspend or expel a member from the community on the Leader’s own judgment.

## Article 7. Funds

- **7.1.** The Treasury of the community: members’ quarterly donations · outside gifts · income from activities.
- **7.2.** Funds go only to the functions of the community; profit remains in the Treasury.
- **7.3.** At the first stage the disposal of funds rests with the Leader (Article 4.1); accounts are open (4.4). Details: the rulebook of the funds function, as it is formed.

## Article 8. The Community in the World

- **8.1.** One center; cities and countries—local chapters.
- **8.2.** The Leader and those the Leader appoints speak on behalf of the community.
- **8.3.** Termination of activity is not provided for.
