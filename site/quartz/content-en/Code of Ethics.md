**The Code is the derived mechanism of right choice, set down and applied to our system.** Every ethical system exists in relation to a system and roles: an operator occupies a role, and the operator’s obligations are derived from the role by the rules of the system. The prescriptions here are derived: the general chain is on the [[Ethics]] page, and each step of the Code rests on the steps of that chain.

## The Ground

**Step 1** *(the derivation of ethics: steps 14 (the Law of Judgment by Role), 17, 18, 81)*. Obligations are derived from the role: an act is judged by its conformity to its role according to the rules of the system in which the operator acts.

Once the system and the role are named, the obligations follow. Hence the Code is brief: its strength lies in each rule’s being derived rather than in their number.

**Step 2** *(the definition of the system; the derivation of ethics: step 12)*. The system is the [[Path of Wisdom]]. Its task unfolds in the two tasks of the roles: the follower’s, **to find truth rightly**; the philosopher’s, **to carry wisdom**.

“To find truth rightly” is wisdom in action: the unity of will and technique around truth ([[Ethics|the derivation of ethics]], steps 75–76). “To carry wisdom” is to help others see absolute truth and understand it ([[Charter#What the Charter Is|the goals of the path]]); the one who carries it holds an intention toward the true order—the best, not just any (step 37).

**Step 3** *(the definition of the roles; the derivation of ethics: step 12)*. The roles of the system: the **Follower**—one who walks the path; the follower’s task is to find truth rightly. The **Philosopher**—one who is able to analyze order ([[Philosophy]]); the philosopher’s task is to carry wisdom.

The philosopher is a role of capacity. The roles are open: fitness is the outcome of the path, and capacity unfolds in doing.

## The Obligations of the Follower

**Step 4—the duty to know** *(the derivation of ethics: steps 17, 65, 83)*. To take up one’s role: to learn one’s role · to learn the rules of the role · to act in accord with them. The rules of one’s role are knowable, and the striving to know them is an obligation.

**Step 5—the mechanism of right choice** *(the derivation of ethics: steps 13–22)*. Before acting:

1. Is there a choice with a status here—is [[Order|order]] at stake (steps 10, 13, 43)?
2. What is my role, and what are its rules (step 17)?
3. What is my [[Ethics#Intention and the Lie|intention]]—and is it an action against the will of another for my own ends (step 21)?
4. Act in accord with the role and the rules (step 12).
5. Check by the result—and by the addressee and the time of your own action (steps 15, 21).

**Step 6—the path and correction** *(the derivation of ethics: steps 59, 60, 63, 75–79)*. To fill the structure with truth—along both axes: technique (how) and will (with what). Upon an error—acknowledgment: the turn toward truth is possible at any step; a lie addressed to another turns the error into evil, and a new lie hides the previous one. For the harm from a wrong choice—restitution to the one affected, whether a miss or intent.

**Step 7—consciousness and conscience** *(the derivation of ethics: steps 21, 84; [[Metaphysics#The Manifestations of Logic|Metaphysics]])*. To keep consciousness manifest: reflection makes the doer an observer of the doer’s own acts. Conscience—an intuitive response to the status “right—wrong”—is fallible and can be formed; to form it is an obligation.

## The Obligations of the Philosopher

The obligations of the Follower remain; capacity adds its own.

**Step 8—to carry wisdom** *(the derivation of ethics: steps 37, 73)*. One who is able to analyze order is obliged to carry wisdom—to enter where there is no order and to help others see and understand. The obligation is derived from the ground: order requires analysis, and analysis requires capacity; the capable are obliged. To carry wisdom is to hold an intention toward the true order—the best, not just any.

**Step 9—demonstration, not imposition** *(the derivation of ethics: steps 52, 53)*. The way of logic is to show itself by being itself; its bearer shows the rules, and the will of the other is preserved. Imposition is an action against the will of another for one’s own ends (step 21): the path of the aggressor.

**Step 10—protection** *(the derivation of ethics: steps 52, 54)*. To protect those whose will is under pressure; to give knowledge of logic—it is itself the defense against the counterfeit of logic, sophistry.

## Reciprocity

**Step 11** *(the derivation of ethics: steps 72, 73)*. The network of mutual provision: each role gives from its capacity or resource what the function of another role needs—the capable carry order, the holders of a resource give the capable their conditions. Injustice is non-reciprocity, the breaking of the network.

## Boundaries

- The Code is assembled from the derivation and changes only together with it: the Code is corrected by the derivation, not by opinion.
- “Why one is obliged” is answered in the derivation of ethics: obligation is a condition of one’s own path, not an external law with a sanction (step 86). The Code sets down the structure of obligations; the whole chain stands on the will, and its derivation is given in [[Metaphysics#The Operator and the Will|Metaphysics]].
- The Code does not promise good: to live by the Code is to act rightly; it is the observer who will call an act good (the derivation of ethics: step 21).
