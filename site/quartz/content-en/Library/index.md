A building of knowledge on one foundation.

## One Beginning

Everything here begins with one fact—**something exists**: it presents itself and is confirmed in every act of thought, perception, and doubt. Its first consequence is the distinction *A* | not-*A*; in the structure of this act the five laws of [[Logic]] are recognized—not rules of thinking but properties of being itself, given together with it. Then come being and the witness ([[Metaphysics]]), the structure of every system—Rules, Roles, and Elements ([[Theory of Systems]])—knowing, choice, building together, reasoning, and number. Nothing in the building is postulated: each step is derived from the previous one, and where the derivation has been brought to a formal record, a machine checks its model; the assumptions of the instrument and the price of each result are named on [[Formalization#The Ground|“Formalization”]]. The Library is the record of this derivation, laid out by branches.

## Eight Entrances

The branches are entrances to one building; their order is the order of derivation.

**[[Logic]]—the foundation.** The five laws as conditions of existence are the ground of all the branches. Here too: [[Order]], set out as the fifth law of logic, and what [[Philosophy|philosophy]] is.

**[[Metaphysics]]—what being is.** How the laws of Logic are given in being: Logic and the Source, Light; the witness and the observer; consciousness, the Intellect, and the Mental—three manifestations of Logic; the will and the mental field.

**[[Theory of Systems]]—how a whole is arranged.** The structure of every system is Rules, Roles, and Elements; from this triad the arrangement of any whole is derived—from an atom to a piece of reasoning: the generating order, constitution, interaction, dynamics, emergence, the boundary of the theory.

**[[Epistemology]]—how we know.** Everything that exists is knowable. Epistemology does not postulate its positions or the boundaries of knowledge; it reads them off: knowledge is taken as a system—rules, roles, elements; the ladder leads from data through reading and information to the record. Themes: [[Truth and Absolute Truth]], [[Objectivity and Subjectivity]], [[Intuition]].

**[[Ethics]]—how to choose.** Ethics is an action, a process whose goal is the maintenance of order through the right arrangement of roles; as a system it is the evaluation of choice. The right (the rightful) is order, and order is an aspect of logic; evil is an action against the will of another for one’s own ends.

**[[Social Systems]]—how to build together.** A branch in development: its pages are a draft still to be expanded; the meanings are being unfolded in a separate strand of work.

**[[Architecture of Reasoning]]—how to reason without failures.** The six domains are regions of homogeneous work of thought; each has a guiding question and a guiding principle. A fallacy is a failure in the architecture of reasoning, and every failure has an address; a well-formed statement makes a paradox impossible. Attached to the branch are the [[Fallacy Catalog]] and the [[Paradox Catalog]].

**[[Mathematics]]—how to count.** Mathematics is the language for describing logical laws: the laws exist, and they are discovered, not invented. A number is the result of an act of observation, information about a distinction made; the natural series is a process of generation, a real number a process of approximation; infinity is a process, finite at every step. The finitization boundary coincides with the boundary of constructivity—a line running through the whole volume, proved in the decidable cases. Attached to the branch is the volume “Mathematics.”

## Collections

Attached to the branches are their large collections, which can also be read on their own.

- **The volume “Mathematics”**—from the first distinction and the laws of logic to the final synthesis, by parts and chapters ([[Mathematics#The Volume “Mathematics”|“Mathematics”]]); each part rests on its own code files, and the points of support are on [[Formalization#The Volume “Mathematics”: An Extended Reading|“Formalization”]].
- **The derivation of ethics** is the page [[Ethics]] itself: the numbered chain of its derivation from “*A* exists”; its application is the [[Code of Ethics]].
- **The [[Fallacy Catalog]]**—fallacies by type of violation of the architecture; **the [[Paradox Catalog]]**—analyses by category; **the [[Domains]]**—the six regions of the work of thought ([[Architecture of Reasoning]]).

## Verification

The word “proved” with a reference to “Formalization” is not a figure of speech: behind it stands a theorem whose proof is accepted by the kernel of the Rocq system (formerly Coq), and anyone can check this. What is proved are statements of models: the page [[Formalization]] shows what exactly is proved, at what price, and what remains an argument of the prose; it also gives the numbers and the map by branches, and explains what formalization is and how it differs from the reader’s trust.

## What the Building Does Not Contain

The fourth corner of the modal square—“is not, and cannot be”—does not enter the field: the impossible does not exist; it is built—by category substitution. The registry [[What Is Not and Cannot Be]] gathers such constructions with the exact names of what really stands behind the word: nothing as an object, the ego as an agent, actual infinity, the return of a past state.

## How to Read

**The pages of the branches share one frame:** an opening sentence, the etymology, the definition—and a “Summary” at the end; the pages of the laws follow a single form; many pages analyze their subject as a system—Rules, Roles, Elements; cases are set in collapsible blocks. The relation of the [[Theory of Systems]] to the other branches is a derivation, not a reference: they take their architecture from it. “Proved” means a theorem of a model on the page [[Formalization]]; “the limit of the derivation” marks an exact boundary: what has been established and what remains open.

**Two routes.** From the foundation—in the order of the entrances: [[Logic]] → [[Metaphysics]] → [[Theory of Systems]] → [[Epistemology]] → [[Ethics]] → [[Social Systems]] → [[Architecture of Reasoning]] → [[Mathematics]]. From a question: what is?—[[Metaphysics]] · how is a whole arranged?—[[Theory of Systems]] · how do we know?—[[Epistemology]] · how to choose?—[[Ethics]] · how to build together?—[[Social Systems]] · how to reason without failures?—[[Architecture of Reasoning]] · how to count?—[[Mathematics]].

**Unhurriedly.** The pages are dense: each sentence is a step of derivation, and the next step stands on the previous one. They are best read slowly, and after reading one should give oneself time for reflection: return to the question of what has been understood and what it rests on. Ideally—meditation on what has been read: to hold what has been understood at the center of attention until it becomes one’s own. Understanding is more than knowledge: knowledge requires only holding information—remembering; understanding requires holding the structure itself as well: why it is so.

## Summary

1. **The derivation begins with one fact**—“something exists”: in the structure of the act of distinction the five laws of Logic are recognized; this is the foundation of the building.
2. **The branches are entrances to one building;** their order is the order of derivation: Logic, Metaphysics, Theory of Systems, Epistemology, Ethics, Social Systems (in development), Architecture of Reasoning, Mathematics.
3. **Attached to the branches are large collections:** the volume “Mathematics,” the derivation of ethics, the catalogs of fallacies and paradoxes, the domains.
4. **“Proved” means a theorem of a model** accepted by the Rocq kernel; its status and price are on “Formalization.”
5. **The impossible does not exist—it is built;** the registry names the substitution and the exact name.
6. **Read slowly,** with reflection on what has been read.
