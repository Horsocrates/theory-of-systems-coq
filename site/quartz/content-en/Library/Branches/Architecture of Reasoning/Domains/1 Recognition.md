Recognition is the first of the six [[Domains|domains]] of reasoning, the point of entry of information: in it, what is gets fixed. Here: how perception, attention, and the filter are arranged, why errors of recognition are invisible from within, and what discipline makes them at least partly visible.

## Etymology

**Recognition**—in Russian *raspoznavanie* ← *ras-* + *poznat’* ← **znat’** “to know” (the Indo-European root *ǵneh₃-*, the same as in Greek *γιγνώσκω* (gignōskō), Latin *noscere*, English *know*). The prefix *po-* makes knowing a directed act; *ras-* carries distinguishing and analysis—the same prefix as in *ras-suzhdenie* “reasoning” itself. To *raspoznat’* is **to distinguish and identify**: to see in what is present what it is. For contrast: English *recognition* ← Latin *re-cognoscere*—“to know *again*,” a repeated meeting with what is already familiar. The Russian word holds the act of distinguishing, the Latin line holds re-cognition, knowing again; both senses work in the domain, and the danger lies precisely in substituting the second for the first—“re-cognizing” instead of distinguishing.

## Definition

**Recognition is the fixing of what is.**

It answers the question *what is here?*—and only that question: it fixes the difference *A* / not-*A*. Not “what is this?” (that is [[2 Clarification|clarification]]), not “through which framework do I look?” ([[3 Framework Selection|the framework]]), but *what is given*. Its work is to take what is present precisely, adding nothing and taking nothing away, so that the other domains have something to work with. Within, the domain moves from the general to the particular: first it is noticed that something is—data; then what exactly this is—information; then which categories apply to it—qualities; and only then what its concrete values are—characteristics. This material will become a concept further on, in clarification.

Recognition works with the channels through which information is perceived: attention turns to the very process of perception and does not pass at once to the question *what exactly am I perceiving?* Holding attention within the domain of recognition, the witness focuses on incoming information and leaves its interpretation outside the bounds of the domain. This makes it possible to restrain a hasty determination of what has been met, so as to avoid projections and illusions arising from expectations and from built-in cognitive mechanisms for filling in the unknown. It also makes it possible to separate into layers the data (what is), the incoming information (what the witness’s channels have transmitted), and the witness’s work with the information received.

## Origin: A Function of the Mental Field

Recognition is a function of the **mental field**: the reception of differences through the channels of perception and their fixing. Data lie in the objective field; taken by the witness, they become information, and information settles as a record into the witness’s subjective field, its slice of the objective field. The domain of recognition is the place where the slice is replenished with records; a thought is a record of inner recognition ([[Metaphysics#Thought|“Metaphysics,” “Thought”]]).

Links with [[Metaphysics|metaphysics]]: the witness is the role of the reader; attention is a manifestation of the will; consciousness is gradual—hence the discipline of the domain is possible; the Mental is the medium of perception. From here, too, comes the measure of the domain in the operator’s life: the rules of a system give a **pool** of available moves, but the operator chooses from its own **list**—a part of the pool that has passed through perception; the list is composed here, in recognition—and an oversight, a legal move outside the list, is born in this domain ([[Theory of Systems#A System in Time: Dynamics|dynamics]]).

## The Domain as a System

Analysis by the generating order—from the goal ([[Theory of Systems]]):

- **Goal**—a true record of what is: to fix *A* as *A*, adding nothing and taking nothing away—and thereby to give the subsequent domains true material.
- **Rules**—from the goal: the [[#The Principle of Presence|Principle of Presence]] (the Law of Identity applied to perception); the movement from the general to the particular; the channels of perception carry differences, attention takes them one at a time, the filter lets through and completes—invisible from within; the transition onward—by sufficient reason, when there is enough material for the next domain.
- **Roles**—the witness at the entrance (the reader) and the object—what is: it presents differences.
- **Elements**—the differences presented by the meeting; information taken by the witness; at the output—record-fixations in the witness’s subjective field.

## The Principle of Presence

Recognition is guided by the **Principle of Presence**: to see *A* as *A*, adding nothing and taking nothing away. This is the [[Identity|Law of Identity]] applied to perception: when the expectation *B* is projected onto *A*, Identity is violated in thinking even before reasoning begins.

And here is the most insidious feature of the domain: **errors of recognition are invisible to the one who commits them.** An incorrect comparison can be checked, an incorrect inference reconsidered. But an incorrect recognition leaves no trace: the witness works with distorted material and does not know it. There is no direct access to “raw data” even at the level of basic color—what is experienced as obvious givenness is already the result of unconscious processing; all the more so at the level of meaning, and most subtly of all when another person is being recognized.

> [!example]- Case: “The Dress”—even color is a construction

>
> In February 2015 a photograph of a dress split the internet: some saw it as blue and black, others as white and gold—literally *saw* different colors; friends looked at one screen and could not believe they were seeing different things. >

>
> The critical point here: no one *chose* how to see. The correction ran automatically, before awareness, and it was impossible to switch by an effort of will (for some people the perception changed over time—on its own): the filter chose for the viewer. Even at the level of basic color the witness does not always know what exactly “is”: the brain applies an assumption about illumination before any awareness.

## Perception

**Perception is the act by which what is reaches the witness:** the passage from the objective field into the subjective one, an active taking; its mechanism is the channels ([[Metaphysics#The Observer as a System|“Metaphysics”]]). Along a channel run **differences**—boundaries between objects; on the witness’s side stands the **filter**: not everything presented is taken. The arrangement of perception explains why seeing *A* as *A* is a discipline and not a given.

**Difference comes before the thing.** To perceive a table, the witness separates it from the floor, the wall, the air: before “what is this,” “not the same as” is taken. Hence a subtlety about identity: in being, a thing is determinate in itself, prior to any gaze; in cognition, however, distinguishing precedes identity—a single *A*, without a background, merges with non-being for the knower. This is visible at the limit: complete darkness distinguishes one thing—only “I,” and nothing else can be told apart in it; light appears—and the visible distinguishes two: “I” and the other ([[Metaphysics#Ontological Principles|“Metaphysics”]]). But light and darkness define each other: light is light only as not-darkness, darkness only as not-light. What separates them is a third—not a thing between the sides but the very structure of distinguishing, the boundary as such: the eye sees light and darkness, the mind grasps what separates them. This is the very boundary “*A* | not-*A*” from the page [[Logic]]. Recognition begins at the boundary.

**Data are not yet information.** Data are objective: the object presents differences itself, before any witness. A difference becomes information only when the witness actively takes it in—the word itself preserves this: Russian *vos-prinyat’* means “to receive, to take into oneself,” Latin *perceptio* ← *percipere* (*per-* + *capere*)—“to seize entirely”; in both roots there is an active taking: consciousness is a hand that takes, not a mirror in which the world imprints itself on its own. And the witness does not take directly: the path runs through the channels, and on the witness’s side stands the filter, deciding what will pass. A channel has its own limitations, which can distort: a color-blind person does not distinguish red and green—the data are the same, the wavelength has not changed, the distortion occurs at the level of the channel ([[Intuition]]); what is indubitable for one may not exist for another—by a difference of channels with the same data. The second dimension of reading is the **threshold**: what complexity will fit into a whole image. What has not passed the filter and has not fit within the threshold does not settle as a record—and in the witness’s world, assembled from what has been read, it is as if it were not there.

The witness can grasp what is in two ways: contemplatively—by the senses, which carry impressions—and mentally, working with information itself: as a whole, intuitively, or in parts, discursively; these are already the instruments of cognition—[[Intuition|intuition]] and discursive thought.

## Attention

Between the channel and the filter stands what takes—attention.

**Attention is a manifestation of the will.** Free will is two choices: of direction—where to direct attention—and of response—how to answer experience ([[Metaphysics]]; [[Ethics|the derivation of ethics]]); what will be taken from what is presented is decided by the direction of attention.

**Attention is selective.** It singles out one part of the field per step and thereby not only amplifies it but also selects: directed at one thing, it is blind to another, and the blindness is invisible from within—the witness does not know what it has not noticed until someone points it out.

**Consciousness is gradual**—it is the degree of perception ([[Metaphysics]]): practice—meditation, for instance—leads from distraction to collectedness. The center of attention is always occupied by something, so every act has an orientation; holding truth at the center of attention is attunement ([[Truth and Absolute Truth#Honesty|“Truth and Absolute Truth”]]): recognition is correct when the center is occupied by what is, not by expectation.

> [!example]- Case: “The Invisible Gorilla”—inattentional blindness
> In 1999 Daniel Simons and Christopher Chabris showed viewers a short video: two teams pass basketballs, and the task is to count the passes of one of the teams. In the middle of the video a person in a gorilla suit crosses the court: stops among the players, turns to the camera, thumps their chest, and leaves—about nine seconds on screen in all. About half of the viewers do not notice the gorilla: attention occupied with counting cuts off the rest—and what is cut off leaves no trace.
>
> The effect does not spare the experienced: when a gorilla was inserted into a lung CT scan, 83% of radiologists missed it—people whose profession is to notice; moreover, eye tracking showed that most of those who missed it looked right at it. And here is the second half of the lesson: those who missed it had no idea they had—on a second viewing they were consistently surprised, some even exclaiming, “I missed that?!”; and 78% of Americans believe that people generally notice the unexpected. Inattentional blindness is indistinguishable from within from fullness of sight.
>
> Sources: Simons, D. J., and Chabris, C. F. “Gorillas in Our Midst: Sustained Inattentional Blindness for Dynamic Events,” *Perception* 28, no. 9 (1999): 1059–1074; Drew, T., Võ, M. L.-H., and Wolfe, J. M. “The Invisible Gorilla Strikes Again: Sustained Inattentional Blindness in Expert Observers,” *Psychological Science* 24, no. 9 (2013): 1848–1853; Simons, D. J., and Chabris, C. F. “What People Believe about How Memory Works: A Representative Survey of the U.S. Population,” *PLoS ONE* 6, no. 8 (2011): e22757.

## The Filter

The witness has no dealings with “raw” reality—only with its reading, and what is misread is passed on as reality itself. Therefore what stands between the world and the witness deserves separate analysis. **The filter has several layers**: *biological*—the limitations of the sense organs; *cultural*—the categories of language and tradition; *philosophical*—assumptions about the nature of reality; *personal*—history, traumas, habits of attention. Over them act the emotions—not a fifth layer but a modulator that changes the “lighting” of the whole structure.

**Layers and threshold are two dimensions of one reading.** The layers are the horizontal dimension: which information exactly will pass selection, which will be cut off or distorted. The threshold of complexity is the vertical one: what level of complexity consciousness is able to assemble into a whole image. A child fails to understand a philosophical text not because filters interfere—the threshold has not yet been reached; a scientist understands the most complex theories—and does not notice the obvious in their own relationships: the area is closed by the personal layer. Both dimensions work simultaneously: information must pass the filter *and* not exceed the threshold—only then does it become available.

What is cut off does not vanish: it is present but not unfolded for the witness. The filter is not a malicious censor: it protects consciousness from overload, for to perceive everything at once would be to perceive nothing. The price of protection is an incomplete picture.

**Both limitations are mobile.** The layers can be rebuilt, the threshold shifts: practice, learning, contemplation cleanse the filter and widen the capacity. When consciousness develops, what was previously invisible becomes visible—not because it has appeared, but because the perceiver has changed. On this the discipline of the domain rests: the filter is open to retuning, attention grows with practice.

**The filter itself is invisible.** Like the eye, which sees everything except itself, it shapes perception while remaining outside the field of view: the witness takes the filtered picture for the full reality. Overcoming begins with the awareness that the filter exists—and continues with questions: what might I have failed to notice? which of my biases could have distorted the perception? what would a person with a different experience, a different culture, different convictions see? These questions take one’s own position into account: objectivity is the witness’s orientation toward what does not depend on its position ([[Objectivity and Subjectivity]]), and the questions make the filter at least partly visible.

> [!example]- Case: “The Bias Blind Spot”—the filter does not see itself
> The Princeton psychologist Emily Pronin, with coauthors, described in 2002 a stable effect—the “bias blind spot”: people notice their own biases less readily than those of others. In a 2015 study, 85% of 661 surveyed Americans considered themselves less susceptible to biases than the average American—a statistically implausible picture; only one considered themselves more biased.
>
> The cause is an asymmetry of access, the “introspection illusion”: assessing others, a person observes their behavior; assessing oneself, a person looks inward, into one’s own intentions. But biases work *before* conscious processing—they cannot be found in the inner report: there everything looks like honest weighing. The witness sees through its filter—and does not see the filter itself: it is that *with which* one looks, not that at which one looks.
>
> Sources: Pronin, E., Lin, D. Y., and Ross, L. “The Bias Blind Spot: Perceptions of Bias in Self Versus Others,” *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 28, no. 3 (2002): 369–381; Pronin, E., and Kugler, M. B. “Valuing Thoughts, Ignoring Behavior: The Introspection Illusion as a Source of the Bias Blind Spot,” *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 43, no. 4 (2007): 565–578; Scopelliti, I., Morewedge, C. K., McCormick, E., et al. “Bias Blind Spot: Structure, Measurement, and Consequences,” *Management Science* 61, no. 10 (2015): 2468–2486.

## The Discipline of Attention

Since distortion is invisible from within, what is needed against it is not keen sight but *discipline*. Discipline is possible because consciousness is gradual: practice leads from distraction to collectedness. It consists of four movements. **Slowing down**: before interpreting—see; the enemy of accurate perception is an unchecked automatic reaction.[^1] **Questioning**: what do I actually see? what of this is data, and what is my completion? **A multiplicity of angles**: one look—one vantage point; a new angle reveals that the previous one was partial. **Accepting uncertainty**: an honest “I do not yet know what this is” instead of “this looks like *X*, so it is *X*”; dwelling in not-knowing is also a discipline. The philosopher Simone Weil called attention “the rarest and purest form of generosity.”[^2] To turn it wholly upon something is to give that thing existence in one’s consciousness.

There remains a question that the domain deliberately leaves open: who looks through these filters? This is the question of who reasons—and it belongs to [[Metaphysics|metaphysics]]. The architecture needs only three facts, and all three are already on the table: the witness exists—perception presupposes a perceiver; its consciousness is gradual—so attention can be trained; and its blind spot is real—so discipline is not a luxury but a condition.

## Record-Fixation

The outcome of the domain is **fixation**: information settles as a record into the witness’s subjective field, its slice of the objective field—and from that moment the witness works with it. Here the road of recognition comes to a fork—the only one in the domain: to fix **what is**—or to fix **what is not**: a projection, an illusion. A record of what is not is outwardly indistinguishable from a true one: it lies in the slice in the same way and is read in the same way; the difference is in the root: a true record has its root in what was presented, an illusory one in expectation and completion. Projection itself is legitimate—as a thought-form of the layer “can be, but is not”; it becomes an illusion when it is fixed with the status “is” ([[Truth and Absolute Truth#The Potential and Projection|“Truth and Absolute Truth”]]; on the witness’s records about itself—[[Metaphysics#The Ego Zone|the Ego zone]]). Both paths to a wrong fixation—to add and to subtract—are analyzed below.

## Addition and Subtraction

Errors of recognition are of two kinds: some **add** what is not there; others **subtract** what is there.

Addition is completion. The brain does not tolerate uncertainty: where data are scarce, it fills the gap from past experience, expectations, context—automatically, before awareness. This is not a defect but the chief merit of perception—the ability to extract meaning from the incomplete; the same ability fails when expectation fills the gap incorrectly. This is subtlest where meaning is recognized, and above all another person: having barely seen a person, the witness already “knows” who is there, and from then on sees its own projection. With those long known, the material of completion is the whole accumulated image; hence one’s own picture of a person is seen more often than the person.

Subtraction is the reverse: attention directed at one thing makes one blind to another; what is recognized is not all that is, but what attention is directed at.

What completion feeds on—the biology of the senses, the categories of language, personal history—are the layers of the filter analyzed above; the filter is invisible from within. Therefore what is needed against errors of recognition is discipline—to ask: what of the perceived is data, and what is completion?

This discipline is hardest where completion hardens into **prejudice**. The Russian word *predubezhdenie* says that the conviction took shape *beforehand* (cf. Latin *praejudicium*—“preliminary judgment,” whence English *prejudice*): it was not drawn from the meeting but brought to it ready-made. A whole category brought in advance—“one of those,” “they are all the same”—shifts perception to its foundation: from then on everything the person does is read as confirmation—warmth is interpreted as pretense, sharpness is met with “there you go, I told you so.”

> [!example]- Case: “Warm—Cold”—a word brought before the meeting
> In the second half of the 1940s the psychologist Harold Kelley conducted an experiment at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (published in 1950). Students of three class sections were told that the class would be taught by an unfamiliar instructor, and before the instructor’s arrival they were given a short biographical note, supposedly from someone who knew the instructor: a graduate student, twenty-six years old, married, three semesters of teaching. The notes were identical in everything except one characteristic: in half of them the instructor appeared as a “rather cold” person—industrious, critical, practical, and determined; in the other half “cold” was replaced by “very warm,” the rest word for word. The versions were distributed at random within one room: neighbors at the desk read different things without suspecting it—and then for twenty minutes watched the same person leading the same discussion.
>
> After the class the ratings diverged. Those who had read “warm” rated the instructor as more considerate of others, informal, sociable, good-natured, humane, with a sense of humor; those who had read “cold” rated the same behavior notably lower on these traits. One word, received before the meeting, worked as a ready category: from then on the behavior was not observed but read through it.
>
> The behavior of the observers themselves changed as well: fifty-six percent of the “warm” readers entered into discussion with the instructor—and only thirty-two percent of the “cold” ones; Kelley describes this relationship as low. Here the mechanism of prejudice is visible in miniature: the category arrived before the person and decided what would be seen—and then preserved itself: the “cold” instructor was spoken to less, and the students thereby deprived themselves of the material that could have corrected the impression. The word from the note became observation, observation—proof of the word.
>
> Sources: Kelley, H. H. “The Warm-Cold Variable in First Impressions of Persons,” *Journal of Personality* 18, no. 4 (1950): 431–439; the root of the design: Asch, S. E. “Forming Impressions of Personality,” *Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology* 41, no. 3 (1946): 258–290. A replication in a real lecture with an evaluation of the teaching: Widmeyer, W. N., and Loy, J. W. “When You’re Hot, You’re Hot! Warm-Cold Effects in First Impressions of Persons and Teaching Effectiveness,” *Journal of Educational Psychology* 80, no. 1 (1988): 118–121.

Recognition here is distorted and, beyond that, closed upon itself: expectation itself selects evidence in its own favor—[[Confirmation Bias|confirmation bias]]. In the catalog it is a syndrome that affects reasoning as a whole; in the first domain its mechanism is data filtering, analyzed below. In prejudice it is built in still deeper—into perception itself, before any argument. And, like every error of recognition, prejudice is indistinguishable from within from sight: the prejudiced person does not feel that they *judge*—they are sure that they *see* what the other person is really like. That is why prejudice holds firmer than arguments: an argument disputes a conclusion, while prejudice lies lower—in the material itself, at the entrance—and quietly poisons the whole chain that grows out of it.

Once the data have been noticed, it makes sense, before analyzing, to let [[Intuition|intuition]] show itself—it suggests where to look; its place in the architecture and the rule “intuition begins, discursive thought checks” are in the section [[Architecture of Reasoning#Intuition and Reasoning|“Intuition and Reasoning.”]]

## Grounds for Transition

The transition to the next domain takes place by [[Sufficient Reason|the Law of Sufficient Reason]][^3]: when there is enough material for a unit of work. The subjective sign of the same is a shift of attention: while recognition is under way, attention asks “what else is here?”; when the ground has been gathered, it begins of itself to ask differently: “what is this?” The danger lies at the edges ([[Architecture of Reasoning#Movement through the Domains|forms of order failure]]): a threshold too low—premature transition; too high—stalling, an endless gathering that never reaches analysis.

**The first and second domains are connected more deeply than “step by step.”** The first fixes—the second clarifies *what exactly* was fixed; therefore [[2 Clarification|clarification]] often sends attention back into recognition—for additional information, observation. And the process works not with the whole stream but with **separately taken units of information**: a unit is singled out and recognized—“*A*”—and this is enough to pass it on to clarification: “what is *A*?” Clarification, in turn, can send it back again—to determine the manifestations of *A*, its qualities and characteristics, and what stands next to *A* but is not *A*—starting a cycle in which the other domains also work. A return to recognition takes place every time incoming information is requested: **recognition is the point of entry of information for all subsequent work in the mental field**.

## Fallacies of the Domain of Recognition

When the Principle of Presence is not observed, recognition breaks down—and always in the same way: the distortion enters *before* conscious reasoning and is therefore invisible from within. In the [[Fallacy Catalog|catalog]] the failures of the first domain are assigned the group [[2.1 Domain 1 Recognition|2.1 “Domain 1: Recognition”]]—**26 fallacies**, the largest of the domain groups: distortion of what is perceived as the object of reasoning. Within it are five mechanisms: [[2.1 Domain 1 Recognition#Object Deformation|object deformation]] (*A* turns into *A′*—2 fallacies), [[2.1 Domain 1 Recognition#Object Substitution|object substitution]] (instead of *A*, *B* is recognized—16, the most populated mechanism of the catalog), [[2.1 Domain 1 Recognition#Data Filtering|data filtering]] (selection of material to fit expectation—5), [[2.1 Domain 1 Recognition#Projection|projection]] (adding what is not there—2), and [[2.1 Domain 1 Recognition#Source Distortion|source distortion]] (1). Three frequent representatives are analyzed below—one each from the mechanisms of deformation, filtering, and substitution. Errors of posing the question itself strike earlier than the domain—at the condition of entry; they are analyzed on the page [[Question]].

**[[Straw Man]]**—object deformation. The opponent’s position is distorted, and it is the distorted version that is refuted: *A* turns into *A′*. More often this is not calculation but self-deception: one hears what one expects or fears instead of what was said. Check: would the opponent recognize themselves in my retelling?

**[[Half Truth]]** (card stacking)—data filtering. What confirms is let into the field of attention, what contradicts is sifted out, and the criterion of selection is correspondence to the desired conclusion, not relevance. The conclusion follows logically from the *presented* data, but the data themselves are the result of selection. Check: what data could refute the conclusion—and were they considered?

**[[Ad Hominem Argument]]**—object substitution. Instead of the argument, its bearer is recognized and attacked: *A* is replaced by *B*. Facts about a person are relevant for assessing the reliability of a source—but they *supplement* the consideration of the argument and do not replace it. Check: what am I considering—the argument or the person?

> [!example]- Case: “Polywater”—collective blindness
> In 1961 the Soviet chemist Nikolai Fedyakin discovered that water condensed in thin quartz capillaries behaves anomalously: viscous, boiling only above 150 °C, solidifying at about −40 °C. It seemed a new form of water. The influential physical chemist Boris Derjaguin called it “anomalous” and presented it at international conferences; by 1970 dozens of laboratories in the United States and Britain were studying it—in that year alone about a hundred papers appeared, including in *Nature* and *Science*. “Polywater” became a sensation—there was even a panic that it could “infect” ordinary water, and the Pentagon provided funding.
>
> The scientists saw anomalous properties, reproducibility, confirmations from independent laboratories. What they did not see were the sources of contamination. Thin capillaries could not be cleaned completely: sweat from hands, silicon from the glass, organic impurities from the air got into the samples, and the “anomalous” properties were explained precisely by them. Denis Rousseau of Bell Labs showed that the infrared spectrum of “polywater” practically coincides with the spectrum of human sweat; under perfectly clean conditions no anomalous water formed.
>
> The contamination was *before the eyes* of the researchers—but the filter was aimed at “a new form of water,” and the simple explanation seemed dull. The Cold War added cultural pressure, professional pride added personal pressure: to admit contamination meant admitting that the laboratory was not clean enough. The debunking took years because the problem was not in logic and not in method—it was at the level of perception: to see the dirt, one had to stop looking for new water. Derjaguin admitted the error in 1973, publishing in *Nature*, together with N. V. Churaev, the conclusion that the anomalous properties are explained by impurities.
>
> Sources: Franks, F. *Polywater* (Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 1981); Rousseau, D. L. “‘Polywater’ and Sweat: Similarities between the Infrared Spectra,” *Science* 171, no. 3967 (1971): 170–172; Derjaguin, B. V., and Churaev, N. V. “Nature of ‘Anomalous Water’,” *Nature* 244 (1973): 430–431.

Three fallacies—three ways of violation in the first domain: to deform the object, to sift it, to substitute it. One thing unites them: the distortion enters before conscious reasoning, and everything that follows, however impeccable, only develops distorted material.

Recognition provides material—but material is not yet understanding. One can gather all the details, knowing what *is here*, and not know *what this is*. Between gathering material and analyzing it lies a step that is often skipped—[[2 Clarification|clarification]]. If recognition answers “what is here?”, clarification answers “what is this?”.

## Questions

The guiding question—**“what is here?”**—along the axes of the [[Question#The Question and the Domains|question]]: a completing goal of the category of the present (what? which?); the direction stands at the boundary of the known, in the second zone, and the material arrives by **inflow**—through the channels of perception; on the path of cognition this is the stage of **construction**: the domain gathers a pool of material; the step is closed by the witness’s ascertainment: “this is.”

The working questions of the domain—in the order of work:

- **Presence**—“what is here?”—the guiding question itself; then “what else is here?”, until the gathering of material is complete;
- **Unit**—“has a unit been singled out—what exactly am I passing on to clarification?”—unit-by-unit processing ([[#Grounds for Transition|grounds for transition]]);
- **Completion**—“what of the perceived is data, and what is my completion?”—the discipline of the filter, against addition;
- **Subtraction**—“what have I not looked at?”—against the blindness of attention;
- **Distinguishing**—“have I distinguished the object—or re-cognized what I expected?”—against substitution by re-cognition;
- **Fixation**—“am I fixing what is—or what is not?”—the fork of the record ([[#Record-Fixation|record-fixation]]).

The transition is a conscious step, not a spontaneous jump of attention: attention can in fact leap too early. A witness who knows the architecture consciously works through the first domain and only then goes on to [[2 Clarification|clarification]], when enough material has been recognized for its work.

## Summary

1. **Recognition is the fixing of what is:** it fixes the difference *A* / not-*A* and answers the question “what is here?”; it is the point of entry of information for all subsequent work.
2. **Within, the domain moves from the general to the particular:** data, information, qualities, characteristics; data lie in the objective field, information settles as a record into the witness’s subjective field, its slice of the objective field.
3. **The Principle of Presence is the Law of Identity applied to perception:** to see *A* as *A*, adding nothing and taking nothing away.
4. **Perception is an act, the channels are its mechanism; between the channel and the filter stands attention**, a manifestation of the will; a channel can distort, the filter selects and completes, and the filter itself is invisible from within.
5. **Errors of recognition are invisible to the one who commits them:** they add what is not there or subtract what is; against them stands the discipline of attention: slowing down, questioning, a multiplicity of angles, accepting uncertainty.
6. **The fallacies of the domain—group 2.1 of the catalog, 26 records:** object deformation, object substitution, data filtering, projection, source distortion; all enter before conscious reasoning.
7. **The outcome of the domain is record-fixation with a fork:** to fix what is—or what is not; the transition to clarification is made by sufficient reason, when a unit has been singled out.
