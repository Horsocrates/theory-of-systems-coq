The domains are the six regions of the work of thought of which reasoning is composed. Here: what a domain is, how the domains depend on one another, which laws stand behind their principles, and when a domain has been passed. Movement through the domains as a whole—free transitions under an order of dependence, eight forms of order failure, returns, diagnosis from end to beginning—is treated on the branch index: [[Architecture of Reasoning#Movement through the Domains|“Movement through the Domains.”]]

## Etymology

**Domain** ← Fr. *domaine* “estate” ← Lat. *dominium* “ownership, property” ← *dominus* “master” ← *domus* “house.” The axis of meaning is **holding**: a domain is a region held by a distinct kind of work. The word was chosen instead of “stage”: a stage is a temporal notion, a domain a logical one; the domains describe *what* must be done, not *when*.

## Definition

**A domain of reasoning is a region of homogeneous work of thought with its own function and its own principle.** To [[1 Recognition|recognize]], to [[2 Clarification|clarify]], to [[3 Framework Selection|select a framework]], to [[4 Comparison|compare]], to [[5 Inference|infer]], to [[6 Reflection|look back]]—six different functions, six distinct kinds of work; in the system of reasoning the domains are its roles ([[Architecture of Reasoning#Reasoning as a System|“Reasoning as a System”]]). The domains are always at thought’s disposal: as a craftsman possesses the whole set of tools even when working with one, so thought always has all six at hand—a domain is neither opened nor closed; in it one either works or does not.

**What is ordered is dependence:** the work of each domain rests on the material obtained by the preceding ones—and the horizontal of the [[Order|Law of Order]] consists in this dependence. From the dependence follows the natural path—recognition, clarification, framework, comparison, inference, reflection; this is the order of grounds.

**The necessity of the sequence is logical, not psychological.** One cannot compare what has not been recognized, infer from what has not been compared, examine a conclusion that does not yet exist—this is impossible by its very sense, like “a roof without walls.” Reflection as a process requires a path already passed; as a position of observation it is present at all domains.

A domain can be passed badly—recognized through a projection, clarified insufficiently. Every error has an address: the conditions of reasoning, a domain, order, or the process as a whole; that is why errors can be diagnosed ([[Fallacy Catalog]]; failures within the domains are [[02 Domain Violations|type 2]]).

**The logical sequence is rigid, the temporal one is flexible.** Living thought is interwoven: a future conclusion may be glimpsed already in recognition. The question is this: by the moment of the conclusion, were the preceding domains adequately completed? An expert’s hypothesis is not a conclusion but a preliminary recognition—“I see a pattern”; in checking it, the expert passes the same domains as the novice—faster, compressing the order and skipping what is obvious. Errors arise precisely where compression discards what is necessary.

## The Six Domains

1. **[[1 Recognition|Recognition]]—*what is here?*** The point of entry: the witness meets what is; the work of the domain is to take what is present precisely, adding nothing and taking nothing away, so that the others have something to work with. There are two ways to fail—addition (projection) and subtraction (selective blindness), and both errors are invisible to the one who makes them: what is distorted at the entrance passes unnoticed through all the subsequent domains. **The Principle of Presence**: to see *A* as *A*—to register what is, not what is expected or desired.

2. **[[2 Clarification|Clarification]]—*what is this?*** Movement from name to essence: a name is not yet understanding. Its questions are: what is this; what makes it what it is; how does it differ from what is similar; what does the word mean *here*. The main enemy is the illusion of understanding: the word is familiar, so it seems understood. **The Principle of the Root**: dig down to a support; stopping is by sufficient reason relative to the goal: the concept is clarified to the extent that reasoning can rest on it.

3. **[[3 Framework Selection|Framework Selection]]—*through which framework do I look?*** The determination of the system of coordinates in which comparison is possible: one always looks *through* something; the only question is whether the choice is conscious. The same data are compatible with many frameworks; frameworks form a hierarchy, and one should begin with the highest level. **The Principle of Objectivity**: the framework is chosen by the nature of the object and the goal; an orientation toward truth excludes an orientation toward confirmation—the two cannot be combined logically.

4. **[[4 Comparison|Comparison]]—*how does this relate to that?*** The revealing of relations through a single criterion: comparison discovers relations rather than creating them; an apple and a pear are comparable by sweetness, an apple and justice are incomparable: there is no measure applicable to both. **The nodal domain—all three principles at once**: Presence (only attributes that are there), Objectivity (a single criterion without exceptions), the Root (the ground of the measure itself); violate any of them, and comparison is either impossible or unreliable.

5. **[[5 Inference|Inference]]—*what follows from this?*** The extraction of a consequence from established grounds: the material of inference is the processed result of the four preceding domains. Where grounds are lacking, the honest move is to withhold. **The Principle of Rationalism**: a conclusion must be *earned* by the preceding work—a conclusion beyond the grounds is speculation; confidence is formulated according to the strength of the grounds.

6. **[[6 Reflection|Reflection]]—*how did I reason about this?*** The comprehension of the reasoning that has been passed and of its limits—a rise to the level of observation above the slice: the first five domains look at the object, reflection at the reasoning itself. Three nested layers: on the object (is the result right), on the process (how I came to it), on oneself (what in me led the reasoning); returns to the domains already passed are the norm. Its role is special: the position of observation above the slice, present at all domains; as a process it is a new pass of the same domains over the records of the path: a domain by its place in the path, a recursion by its mechanism. **The Principle of Limitation**: every answer has a range of application, assumptions, and new questions.

## Laws and Grounds for Transition

The principle of a domain is the application of one or several laws of logic to its work: the principle is derived from the laws. The ground for transition is the criterion of completion: a domain has been passed when there is a sufficient reason for moving on to the next one ([[Sufficient Reason]]).

| Domain                | Guiding question                   | Principle                                      | Grounding law                                                  | Ground for transition |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| 1. Recognition        | What is here?                      | Presence                                       | Identity                                                       | There is enough material for a unit of work; the unit is singled out |
| 2. Clarification      | What is this?                      | the Root                                       | Sufficient Reason; the stability of a term is held by Identity | Terms are defined explicitly and do not change their meaning; hidden assumptions are named; the goal of the question is formed |
| 3. Framework Selection | Through which framework do I look? | Objectivity                                   | Non-Contradiction; the ground of the choice is Sufficient Reason | The framework is taken with a ground (“why this one”); what it leaves outside its boundary is named |
| 4. Comparison         | How does this relate to that?      | nodal: Presence, Objectivity, the Root         | Identity, Non-Contradiction, Sufficient Reason, Order          | The comparison list is filled: the measure is applied to all the given material |
| 5. Inference          | What follows from this?            | Rationalism                                    | Sufficient Reason; with it Order and Excluded Middle           | The consequence is recorded and marked—or a suspension of judgment is recorded with the lack named |
| 6. Reflection         | How did I reason about this?       | Limitation                                     | Identity, taken from the side of the boundary                  | The answer is closed by a check against the goal; the limits are drawn; open questions are named |

Six questions are the minimal architecture: remove any one—and an impassable gap opens; a seventh necessary question has not been found. The structural core of this is proved ([[Formalization#Domains|“Formalization,” “Domains”]]).

## The Cycle of the Domains and the Path of a Question

The cycle that a [[Question#The Ladder of Questions|process question]] opens unfolds through the domains along the stages of the path to an answer: recognition and clarification **construct**, framework, comparison, and inference **choose**, reflection **ascertains**; a decision of the will enters reasoning exactly once—as the framework—while inference takes the root step and closes with the extraction of the consequence. The marking of the guiding questions along the axes is on the page “Question” ([[Question#The Question and the Domains|“The Question and the Domains”]]) and in the section “Questions” of each domain page.

## Correspondence to Reality

The domains work because they track the structure of reality itself: it is *given* (Recognition), *determinate* (Clarification), *many-sided* (Framework Selection), *connected* (Comparison), *necessary* (Inference), *inexhaustible* (Reflection). The architecture of the domains is discovered, not established—like the laws themselves.

## In Ethics

Passing through the domains is a matter of honesty. Rationalization—picking grounds to fit a ready conclusion—is reverse movement: it passes off untruth as truth; refusing reflection is refusing to acknowledge the limits of one’s own confidence. The *Challenger* disaster: the knowledge was there, the comparison was made incompletely, and still the engineers drew a limit for the launch; management rejected this conclusion, and the engineers were required to prove that the launch was dangerous—it was reflection that was rejected (the case is on the page [[4 Comparison#Fallacies of the Domain of Comparison|“Comparison”]]).[^1] “Nature cannot be fooled” (Feynman).[^2] The order of reasoning is an ethical demand ([[Order#In Ethics|“Order,” “In Ethics”]]).

## Summary

1. **A domain of reasoning is a region of homogeneous work of thought with its own function and its own principle;** in the system of reasoning the domains are roles.
2. **What is ordered is dependence:** each domain works on the material of the preceding ones; the logical sequence is rigid, the temporal one flexible.
3. **Six domains**—recognition, clarification, framework selection, comparison, inference, reflection; each has a guiding question, a principle, a grounding law, and a ground for transition.
4. **The principles are derived from the laws of logic;** a domain has been passed when there is a sufficient reason for moving on to the next one.
5. **The cycle of the domains is the path of a question in miniature:** construction, choice, ascertainment; a decision of the will enters once—as the framework.
6. **The domains track the structure of reality:** it is given, determinate, many-sided, connected, necessary, inexhaustible.
7. **Passing through the domains is a matter of honesty:** the order of reasoning is an ethical demand.
