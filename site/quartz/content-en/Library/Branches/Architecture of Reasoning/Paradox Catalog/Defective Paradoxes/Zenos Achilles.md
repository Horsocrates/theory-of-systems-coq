**D.13** · [[Defective Paradoxes|defective paradox]] · subtype: False Premise

**Essence of the error.** An infinite number of steps ≠ an infinite time

## Exact Formulation

Achilles runs ten times faster than the tortoise and gives it a head start of a hundred meters. While he runs those hundred, the tortoise crawls on by ten; while he runs ten, it is a meter ahead; while he runs a meter, a decimeter… Every time Achilles reaches the place where the tortoise was, it is already ahead. There are infinitely many steps in this description, and at each of them the tortoise leads. Zeno’s conclusion: the fastest will never catch the slowest.

## Origin

Zeno of Elea (fifth century BCE), a pupil of Parmenides, built his aporias as weapons: not to show that motion cannot be seen, but to show that the thought of motion is contradictory, and thereby to defend the doctrine of the one motionless being. The four aporias of motion have come down to us through Aristotle’s *Physics*: the [[Zenos Dichotomy|dichotomy]], Achilles, the [[Zenos Arrow|arrow]], and the stadium. Aristotle gave the answer himself, and it remains load-bearing to this day: the distinction between actual and potential infinity; the path is infinitely *divisible*, but not infinitely *divided*. An anecdote credits Diogenes with an answer given by the body: he stood up and walked—solvitur ambulando, “it is solved by walking.” Strict reckoning came with the theory of limits: the sum of an infinite geometric series is defined and finite; the twentieth century continued the dispute on new material—Bergson (motion is indivisible; it is the intellect that cuts it up), Russell (mathematics has solved it), and then the discussion of supertasks (Black, Thomson, Grünbaum), which grew directly out of this aporia.

## Consequence

The aporias are the first thought experiments about infinity, and their progeny is twofold. To mathematics they bequeathed the need for a strict theory of series and limits: “infinitely many terms” and “an infinite sum” are different things, and this difference had to be forged. To philosophy they bequeathed the very watershed between the actual and the potential infinite, on which our formal base also stands (P4: infinity is a process, not a completed object). The direct heirs are the supertasks: [[Thomsons Lamp|Thomson’s Lamp]] and [[Ross Littlewood|Ross–Littlewood]] ask what comes “after” an infinite sequence of acts—a sharpened form of the same confusion of description with performance. The name of the aporia has also migrated into physics: the quantum Zeno effect concerns the influence of frequent measurements; the subject is a different one, and the kinship is in the name only.

## Domain Analysis

**Domain 1—Recognition: what is given?** Components: Achilles, the tortoise, the head start, the ratio of speeds, the sequence of positions “where the tortoise was.” Structure: one motion is described by an infinite series of catching-up steps. No signal yet.

**Domain 2—Clarification: what do the terms mean?** Here is the defect. “Will never catch up”—“never” speaks of time. But Zeno’s series enumerates events, not durations: step n is the reaching of place n. The tacit premise of the transition—“infinitely many steps take an infinite time”—is false: the durations of the steps decrease tenfold at each step, and their sum is bounded. “Never” has taken the place of “at no step of this description”—and the description enumerates exactly the moments *before* the meeting and not a single one after it. Signal: a property of the description has been transferred to the motion.

**Domain 3—Framework Selection:** the second layer of the defect. The instrument—an infinite list of acts—is applied to a continuous process: Achilles does not perform an infinite multitude of deeds; he performs one motion, which the description cuts into as many parts as it likes. Division is a potential process: divisible does not mean divided. Signal: the instrument does not fit the object.

**Domain 4—Comparison:** not engaged.

**Domain 5—Inference:** within the description everything is honest: at each step the tortoise is ahead—true. Only the transfer “at each step” → “always” is false. The problem is not in the inference.

**Domain 6—Reflection:** the meeting is observed in every race—signal: the description does not cover the event; return to domain 2. The converse is also worth noticing: Diogenes’ walking does not lay the defect open—it only signals it; laying it open is the work of logic.

## Diagnosis

**Domain of the error:** Domain 2 (clarification)—primary; Domain 3 (framework selection)—secondary. **Type:** defective—false premise: “infinitely many steps require an infinite time.” **Violated laws:** [[Identity]]—“never” (a quantifier over time) has been replaced by “at no step” (a quantifier over the members of the description); a property of the instrument has been ascribed to the object. **Essence of the defect:** the boundary of what the description covers has been passed off as the boundary of the motion. On the E/R/R triad: the Rule of the description (the list of catching-up steps) covers only part of the Element (the motion)—and a property of the Rule has been passed off as a property of the Element.

## Why There Is No Paradox

Reckoning, diagnosis, ground. Reckoning: with a head start of a hundred meters and a tenfold speed, the meeting takes place at the mark of 1000/9 ≈ 111.1 m; the times of the steps form a geometric series, and its sum is finite—“infinitely many terms” does not mean “an infinite sum.” Diagnosis: Zeno’s series enumerates all the moments before the meeting—within it there is no meeting by construction, just as there is no one in the list of numbers less than one; “absent from the list” does not mean “does not exist”—the list simply ends before the event, and the aporia passed off its boundary as the boundary of the world. The ground is a process one: the infinity of division is potential; the path is divisible without end, but nothing presents the motion with the demand to “perform an infinite multitude of acts”—there are exactly as many acts as the description has cut. The sum of the series is not a completed infinity but a process with a bounded limit; the meeting is an event of the motion, not the last member of the list, and that is why it is not among the members. Aristotle said this first, and only the strict reckoning had to be added. The honest boundary: analysis answers *where and when* the meeting takes place; laying the aporia open answers *why “never” seemed to be a conclusion*—both answers are needed, and the second does not reduce to the first.

## Lesson

**On the instrument and the object.** The properties of a description do not transfer to what is described: the infinity of the members of a series is a fact about the series, not about the race. **On the quantifier “never.”** On hearing “never,” ask: what does the quantifier range over—time, or the members of someone’s list? **On potential infinity.** Divisibility is not dividedness; a process is not a completed object: half the paradoxes of infinity are a forgetting of this difference. **On observation.** Experience (solvitur ambulando) signals that the description is defective—but only analysis shows where exactly.
