**D.24** · [[Defective Paradoxes|defective paradox]] · subtype: Indeterminacy of a Concept

**Essence of the error.** “After infinity” is meaningless

## Exact Formulation

A lamp with a button. For half a minute it is on, for a quarter of a minute off, for an eighth on, for a sixteenth off… The durations halve, and their sum is a minute: by the end of the minute infinitely many switchings have been performed. The question: is the lamp on at the moment of exactly one minute? On—impossible: every switching on was followed by a switching off. Off—impossible: every switching off was followed by a switching on. Neither answer can be derived.

## Origin

James Thomson, “Tasks and Super-Tasks” (*Analysis*, 1954)—the article that gave philosophy the very term “supertask”: an infinite sequence of acts performed in a finite time. Thomson built the lamp as a weapon: since the state at the end of the minute can be derived neither as “on” nor as “off,” a supertask is impossible. Benacerraf (“Tasks, Super-Tasks, and the Modern Eleatics,” 1962) disarmed the conclusion with a single move: the description sets the states of the lamp at all moments *before* the minute—and says nothing about the minute itself; “not derivable from the description” does not entail “impossible”—it entails “the description is silent.” Thomson agreed. The discussion moved on: Grünbaum on the kinematics of supertasks, Earman and Norton on supertasks in general relativity, the literature on “Zeno machines” that solve the uncomputable in two minutes—at the price of the same old fiction of the completed infinite. The root of the genre is obvious: the lamp is the [[Zenos Dichotomy|dichotomy]] read from the other end.

## Consequence

The lamp is the standard case of underdetermination dressed up as contradiction, and its arithmetical shadow is famous: the sequence of states 1, 0, 1, 0, … is Grandi’s series 1 − 1 + 1 − 1 + …, which has no sum; the “half-sum 1/2” of Abel summation is a redefinition of the word “sum,” and the joke “the lamp is half on” honestly shows the price of such an answer. The second legacy is a watershed within the supertask itself: the convergence of the *durations* (a geometric series—honest reckoning, the same as in Zeno) and the “completion” of the *series of acts* are different things: there is a sum of the times; there is no “last switching.” The third is the boundary of hypercomputation: a machine that has “performed infinitely many steps” inherits the lamp’s question—what does its “final” state mean?

## Domain Analysis

**Domain 1—Recognition: what is given?** Components: the lamp, two states, a geometric series of switchings, the moment t = 1, the question about the state at that moment. Signal: a question about “after all the members” of a series that has no last member.

**Domain 2—Clarification: what do the terms mean?** Here is the defect—a double one. “All the switchings have been performed”—the completion of the uncompletable: the [[Hilberts Hotel|hotel]] in dynamic form; “the completed uncompletable” is an oxymoron of the same grade as “a full infinity.” “The state at t = 1”—what determines it? The state of the lamp at the moment t is determined by the last switching before t; the series has no last member—the definition does not reach as far as the minute. The sequence of states does not converge—there is no limit value, not because it is mysterious, but by construction. Signal: the question demands a value where the definition is silent.

**Domain 3—Framework Selection:** the instrument “value in the limit” is applied to a non-converging process—kin to the [[Zenos Arrow|arrow]]: a demand for a property where it is not defined.

**Domain 4—Comparison:** not engaged.

**Domain 5—Inference:** both branches are honest—but all they prove is a mutual non-derivability: “on” is not derivable, and “off” is not derivable. From this Thomson concluded “impossible,” whereas what followed was “underdetermined” (Benacerraf). The problem is not in the branches but in the reading of their outcome.

**Domain 6—Reflection:** “neither on nor off”—signal: check whether the condition determines an answer; return to domain 2.

## Diagnosis

**Domain of the error:** Domain 2 (clarification). **Type:** defective—indeterminacy: the state “after infinity” is not set by the condition. **Violated laws:** [[Sufficient Reason]]—no answer has a ground in the condition: the description does not determine the value at t = 1; [[Excluded Middle]] meanwhile remains intact—a physical lamp would be in some state; what is silent is not being but the description. **Essence of the defect:** “after all the steps” is not a step; the question asks for an Element where the Rule of the process assigns none. On the E/R/R triad, exactly this: the Rule sets the state at each step of the series, and t = 1 does not belong to the series.

## Why There Is No Paradox

Benacerraf’s move is the core: the states at all moments before the minute impose no constraint whatever on the minute; both “refutations” refute derivability, not possibility. The condition is underdetermined—it can be consistently completed in any way: the lamp is on, off, or has evaporated; no completion conflicts with the series of switchings, because the series is silent about the minute. The process layer gives this a precise form: the sequence 1, 0, 1, 0, … is not Cauchy—it does not converge; “the value in the limit” is a predicate of a converging process, and to ask it of a non-converging one is like asking for the sum of Grandi’s series: there is no answer, not from any weakness of reckoning, but from the absence of a bearer. And an honest separation from Zeno, important for the whole block: [[Zenos Achilles|Achilles]] has a whole—the motion—and the description only cuts it, which is why the meeting exists, though it is not on the list; the lamp has no whole—the lamp *is* its series of switchings; the description is not partial, it is exhaustive—and exhaustively silent about the minute. There, the boundary of the description was passed off as the boundary of the world; here, the world is not even given beyond the boundary of the description. The convergence of the durations (the minute does add up—an honest geometric series) does not save the question: the sum of the times is the limit of the process of times, while “the state after all the acts” is not the limit of the process of states, for there is no limit.

## Lesson

**On underdetermination.** “Neither yes nor no” is not always a contradiction—often it means “the condition is silent”; distinguish non-derivability from impossibility. **On the limit.** A value in the limit is the privilege of convergence; a non-converging process has nothing to present at t = 1. **On the two series.** In a supertask the durations converge, but the series of acts does not “complete”: there is no last act, and “after all” is not a moment of the series. **On Zeno’s mirror.** A partial description of a whole (Zeno) and a complete description without a whole (the lamp) are opposite defects of one family: do not confuse which one is before you.
