**N.6** · [[Non Paradoxes|non-paradox]] · subtype: Epistemic Peculiarity

**Essence of the error.** It is rational to acknowledge the probability of error

## Exact Formulation

An author has written a conscientious book: every claim has been checked, and he believes each one. In the preface he writes, as is customary: “responsibility for the errors that surely remain is mine.” In sum: he rationally believes every claim of the book—and rationally believes that at least one of them is false. Together these beliefs are unsatisfiable: it cannot be that all the claims are true and at least one is false. A rational author, it seems, is bound to be inconsistent.

## Origin

David Makinson, “The Paradox of the Preface” (*Analysis*, 1965)—a twin of Kyburg’s [[Lottery Paradox|lottery]], found not in a problem book but in living practice: a modest phrase from a preface turned out to be a logical exhibit. The pair “lottery—preface” has walked together ever since, but the twins have different roles: the lottery builds beliefs by counting, homogeneously and a priori; the preface does so empirically and honestly: all previous books of all authors have contained errors, and induction gives the belief “mine has some too” a firm ground. That is why the preface stings theories of rationality more sharply: the modest meta-belief cannot be discarded as artificial—it is itself a model of rationality. The knot became the banner of fallibilism: fallibility is not a vice of the agent but the agent’s honest condition.

## Why This Is Not a Paradox

The world and the degrees of confidence are free of contradiction. Let the author be 0.99 confident in each of three hundred pages—excellent reliability; then confidence in the faultlessness of the *whole* book—the product of the factors—is about 0.05, and the belief “somewhere there is an error” weighs 0.95: both beliefs are coherent and both are rational; the conflict appears only when degrees are rounded to “yes/no.” The second separation is by level: “there is an error in the book” is a belief *about one’s own beliefs*, an assessment of their joint reliability; it disputes no single page—it disputes only the conjunction of all the pages, and the author nowhere asserted that conjunction: the book asserts the pages, not their conjunction. The defect lies in the expectation that a rational agent is bound to be consistent under conjunction; for a finite, fallible knower this ideal is unattainable—and unnecessary.

## Domain Analysis

**Domain 1—Recognition: what is given?** Components: the set of the book’s claims, belief in each, the meta-belief of the preface, the demand of joint consistency. Signal: one of the beliefs is about the beliefs themselves: the storeys are different.

**Domain 2—Clarification: what do the terms mean?** Here lies the defect of expectation. “I believe each” and “I believe that not all are true” seem to be beliefs of one row—but the first series lives on the pages, the second above the corpus: an assessment of one’s own reliability, inferred by induction from experience. Signal: a gluing of levels—belief and belief-about-beliefs.

**Domain 3—Framework Selection:** the demand of conjunctive consistency is the framework of an ideal omniscient being; applied to a finite agent with an honest statistics of errors, it is a framework that does not fit the object.

**Domain 4—Comparison:** an instructive pair with the [[Lottery Paradox|lottery]]: there the beliefs are homogeneous and countable, here the meta-belief is empirical; the mechanism is one—the aggregate does not inherit the properties of single beliefs.

**Domain 5—Inference:** every step is honest; what is unsatisfiable is only the conjunction, which no one asserted.

**Domain 6—Reflection:** the knot itself is an act of reflection: the preface is the author’s view of the corpus of his own reasoning from a level above; by the canon of the domains this is precisely the work of the sixth domain—and here it is done correctly.

## Diagnosis

**Category:** non-paradox—an epistemic peculiarity. **Location of the defect:** the expectation of conjunctive consistency from a fallible agent. **No law is violated:** the degrees are coherent, the levels are separated. On the E/R/R triad: beliefs in the pages are Roles on the Elements of the corpus; the meta-belief of the preface is a Role above the corpus, a reflective assessment of reliability; the conflict is illusory—the relations live on different levels, and only rounding glues them together.

## Resolution

The computational layer: 0.99 to the power of three hundred is about 0.05; “surely there are errors” is a belief of weight 0.95; in the degrees of confidence everything is smooth, and there is no irrationality in any of the beliefs. The level layer: the meta-belief denies no claim of the book—it assesses the corpus; to get a contradiction, one must ascribe to the author a belief in the conjunction of all the pages, and he does not hold it and is not obliged to: to assert separately is not to assert together. The practical layer is about virtue: the modesty of the preface is not a gesture of politeness but a rational conclusion from induction; to strike it out would mean believing “my book is the first faultless one in history,” and that would be irrational. Fallibility, explicitly acknowledged, is the working condition of an honest knower: absolute truth above all—and about one’s own limits first of all. A degenerate case, for sharpness: if the book consisted of the single sentence “there is a false statement in this book,” the preface would close up with the [[Liar Paradox|liar]]—but that is a different, structural trouble: in Makinson’s case the book has content, and the meta-belief stands on it, not on itself.

## Lesson

**On the levels of belief.** A belief about beliefs stands a storey above the beliefs; adding it to them into one conjunction is a categorial gluing. **On the limits of the ideal.** Conjunctive consistency is the framework of the omniscient; for a finite agent, rationality is calibrated degrees plus an honest meta-assessment, not a faultless list of “yes.” **On modesty.** “Surely I have erred somewhere” is not a weakness of the position but its strength: acknowledged fallibility is the only honest framework for a corpus of any size.
