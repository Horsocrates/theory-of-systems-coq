The “Architecture of Reasoning” concerns the structure of right reasoning: how thought goes from a question to a grounded answer. Its ground is the [[Logic|laws of logic]], properties of being itself, given together with it; its subject is the path of thought from the encounter with what is to the conclusion and its limits. There are three entrances: the [[Domains|domains]]—how thought works, [[Fallacy Catalog|fallacies]]—how it goes astray, [[Paradox Catalog|paradoxes]]—how the construction of a statement breaks.

## Etymology

**Reasoning** ← *reason* ← Old French *raison* ← Lat. **ratio** ← *reor* “to reckon, to suppose”: counting, calculation, proportion; hence also rationality. To reason is to find grounds and to establish relations between things. The axis is **counting**.

For contrast—in other languages:

- Russian ***rassuzhdenie*** ← *rassuzhdat’*, *rassudit’* (*ras-* + *sudit’* “to judge”) ← *sud* “court, judgment”; Proto-Slavic *sǫdъ* is composed of *som-* “together” and the Indo-European root *dʰeh₁-* “to put, to set” (compare *det’* “to put,” *delo* “deed”): by its root, “what is put together.”[^1] The prefix *ras-* carries analysis, division into sides: *rassudit’* means to examine a case and pass a decision; reasoning is judgment unfolded into a process, in which what is unclear is laid out until understanding is reached. Cognates: *sud* (court), *suzhdenie* (judgment), *rassudok* (the faculty of judgment), *pravosudie* (justice). The axis is **judgment**: the weighing of sides and a grounded decision.
- Gk. **λογισμός** (logismos) ← λογίζομαι (logizomai) “to reckon; to consider” ← λόγος (logos) ← λέγειν (legein) “to gather; to say.” The first meaning of the word is “reckoning,” then “reasoning”; beneath reckoning lies gathering. The axis is **gathering**.
- Lat. **discursus** ← *discurrere* “to run to and fro”; in later authors, the passage of thought from one point to another, hence “discursive,” step-by-step thinking.
- Skt. **vicāra** ← *vi-* “apart, in different directions” + *car* “to move”: going to and fro, then deliberation, investigation. A twin of the Latin *discursus*.

The axes converge: reasoning is movement through grounds toward a grounded result, the path from the unclear to the understood. The Russian word adds its own: the result of reasoning is a *judgment*—a verdict weighed on all sides.

## Definition

**Reasoning is the process of searching for an answer to a [[Question|question]]: the directed movement of thought from a question to a grounded answer.** As a system it is held by its goal—a grounded answer; its rules are the laws of logic, its roles the six domains, its elements the contents of thought.

From within thinking, the same function is named in “[[Metaphysics#Three Operations|Metaphysics]]”: reasoning is consecutive deliberation within the frame of a question; its core is clarification and reflection, and it makes use of all six domains.

The definition is normative: it takes reasoning in its genuine function—the search for understanding through a question. This keeps the distinction between movement toward absolute truth and movement away from it. Rationalization of ready conclusions, sophistry for the sake of victory, self-deception for the sake of comfort take on the appearance of reasoning, but the substance of searching is absent from them: they are imitations. To study the structure of successful reasoning on examples of unsuccessful reasoning is like studying the architecture of a building from its ruins.

What unites all cases of genuine reasoning—whatever the subject, the method, the epoch? A question. One can think, recall, fantasize, state; one reasons only in answer to a question.

## Origin

The oldest of the imitations is sophistry. In fifth-century BCE Athens the sophists, for the most part non-Athenians, took fees for teaching eloquence—the skill of persuading and of winning a dispute. To Protagoras belongs the thesis “man is the measure of all things”; Plato in the *Theaetetus* read it as a rejection of absolute truth, common to all.[^2] If each has their own measure, the eloquent prevails, not the one who is right. Socrates’ answer is a question. It is enough to ask what exactly is meant—and a sophism built on ambiguity falls apart. Hence the defense: a sophism is a deliberate violation of the architecture of reasoning; whoever understands that architecture sees the trick, and a sophism seen loses its force. The architecture of reasoning is both a map of one’s own errors and an optics against the manipulations of others.

> [!example]- Case: the *Meno*—six domains twenty-four centuries before their name
> In the *Meno* Socrates, by questions alone, leads a slave to the solution of the problem of doubling a square. At first the slave is sure of knowing the answer: double the side; the construction shows a square four times as large. Then—one and a half times the side; wrong again. The slave admits that he does not know—and from this honest not-knowing, through new questions, he himself finds the solution: the square built on the diagonal has double the area. The path passes through all six steps: recognition (what is meant), clarification (what this is), framework selection (the problem is geometrical), comparison (side or diagonal), inference (the square on the diagonal), reflection (whether it is now seen). Descartes would have broken the same problem into clear steps, Leibniz would have written it in symbols, a formal logician would have checked the inference; but where the premises came from, and how the slave learned that one must build on the diagonal, only the path of questions explains. The steps are taken, although they do not yet have a name.
>
> Source: Plato, *Meno*, 82b–85b.

The definition also clarifies what is not in it: words. Inner experience is wider than speech—it holds images, sensations, unsymbolized thoughts; some people have almost no inner speech, and this is called *anendophasia*.[^3] If reasoning *were* inner speech, such people could not reason; but they do reason. The search for an answer proceeds in whatever modality there is: the architecture of reasoning is one in every modality.

There remains a question that reasoning about reasoning cannot avoid: why does a rightly built chain of thoughts lead to absolute truth *about the world*? The answer here: the laws of thinking and the laws of reality are the same laws. [[Logic]] is the structure of reality itself, which is discovered from within; the laws of physics derive from logic: they are the manifestation of the laws of logic with respect to the interaction of physical systems.

## Reasoning as a System

An analysis by the triad of the [[Theory of Systems]], starting from the goal:

- **Goal**—a grounded answer to the question. It is the ground of the whole system: by it one measures whether each domain has been passed sufficiently.
- **Rules**—the laws of logic. Each domain has a guiding principle derived from the laws; the dependence of the domains on material is ordered by the horizontal of the [[Order|Law of Order]]. Transitions between domains are free.
- **Roles**—the six domains. A domain is a region of homogeneous work of thought with its own function and its own principle. A special role is reflection: the position of observation above the slice, present at all domains; as a process it is a new pass of the same domains over the records of the path: a domain by its place in the path, a recursion by its mechanism ([[6 Reflection|“Reflection”]]).
- **Elements**—the contents of thought on the path from question to answer: what has been recognized, concepts, the framework taken, established relations, the conclusion, the limits of what has been passed. Each domain works on the material of the preceding ones.

## Foundations

Before the domains, foundations are laid—the path from the primary fact to the very ability to reason:

1. **[[Logic]]**—the laws of logic, derived from the fact “something exists”; the common ground of all branches.
2. **[[Epistemology]]**—knowledge: the ladder from data through information to record; the field and the slice; knowledge-about and the paths to it.
3. **[[Intuition]]**—a channel of knowledge-about: the transmission of information to consciousness in response to its request; the rule of alliance “intuition begins, discursive thought checks.”
4. **[[Philosophy]]** (in the branch “Logic”)—the discipline of bringing knowledge into agreement with what is: the cognition of reality through reasoning; the philosopher and the sophist; absolute truth above all.
5. **[[Question]]**—the beginning of reasoning: a directed act, a request given form, which turns attention to a region of the field of possible knowledge; it opens the domains.

Perception—the act by which differences are taken (its mechanism is the channels)—is treated in the domain “[[1 Recognition|Recognition]].”

## Domains

The anatomy of reasoning is six domains. Each has a guiding question and a guiding principle:

1. [[1 Recognition|Recognition]]—*what is here?* The Principle of Presence: to see *A* as *A*, adding nothing and taking nothing away.
2. [[2 Clarification|Clarification]]—*what is this?* The Principle of the Root: dig down to a support; stopping is by sufficient reason relative to the goal.
3. [[3 Framework Selection|Framework Selection]]—*through which framework do I look?* The Principle of Objectivity: the framework is chosen by the nature of the object and the goal.
4. [[4 Comparison|Comparison]]—*how does this relate to that?* The nodal domain: Presence, Objectivity, and the Root together.
5. [[5 Inference|Inference]]—*what follows from this?* The Principle of Rationalism: the conclusion is earned by the preceding work, and its strength is marked by its grounds.
6. [[6 Reflection|Reflection]]—*how did I reason about this?* The Principle of Limitation: every answer has a range of application, assumptions, and new questions.

What a domain is, how the domains depend on one another by material, and which laws stand behind the principles is treated on the page [[Domains|“Domains.”]]

## Movement through the Domains

The domains are described separately, but living reasoning does not line them up in a row: thought jumps, returns, runs ahead. One distinction helps to reconcile this with order: **what is ordered is dependence**—a conclusion drawn without clarification is defective, at whatever moment this happened. The sequence of domains is like a score: the structure is given, the performance is alive; the logical sequence is rigid, the temporal one is flexible. The transitions themselves are free; one thing is required of thought—to make them consciously. The practical rule is therefore *to check*: in drawing a conclusion, are the domains on which it rests complete?

The order of reasoning has its own failures: they undermine the structure in which the domains work. A domain may be passed flawlessly and still lead to error if it is passed out of its place. There are eight forms:

- *Premature transition*—a domain is not completed, and the work is already in the next one: the comparison is not finished, and the conclusion is being drawn.
- *Reverse movement*—reasoning goes from the conclusion to the premises: the conclusion determines which data will be noticed and how they will be interpreted. The building is built from the roof.
- *The skipped stage*—a domain is passed emptily, and reasoning goes on as if it had been passed; between premises and conclusion there is a void under the appearance of connectedness.
- *Mixing*—operations of different domains go on at once: a fact is already seen through a framework before the framework has been chosen; recognition merges with evaluation.
- *Object substitution in the course*—reasoning begins about one thing and ends about another; the Law of Identity requires that *A* remain *A* along the whole path.
- *Burden inversion*—a ground is demanded from one who made no claim.
- *Stalling*—there is a ground for the transition, and there is no transition.
- *Blocked return*—a defect has been found, and the return to it is rejected.

All eight look like reasoning: an eye that seeks the error in the content of the steps does not find it. The defect is structural—it lies in the order of the steps and in the transitions between them. In the [[Fallacy Catalog|catalog]], failures of order are given type [[03 Sequence Violations|3 “Violations of Sequence”]]; it has records for reverse movement (rationalization, the vicious circle) and for burden inversion (shifting the burden of proof).

**Returns** are a regular move of the architecture; they are not counted among the failures. Reflection, having found a defect, consciously returns thought to the domain where the failure occurred. Returns are of three kinds: *corrective*—an error has been found, and the return removes it; *deepening*—there is no error, but the understanding is too little for the task; *expanding*—an aspect has opened that was not visible before. Complex problems are solved by iterations: pass, check, return, refine, pass again—this is a spiral into depth. There is one danger—endless return, that is, stalling: a return is justified where a concrete defect or a concrete possibility of going deeper has been found.

**Diagnosis** goes from the end to the beginning. When a conclusion has turned out to be wrong, the error in it is more often a symptom than a cause: the conclusion inherited the defect of a preceding domain. There is one rule: **look for the error in the earliest violated domain.** If the defect is in clarification, improving the comparison is pointless: first clarify, then revise everything that was built on the unclarified.

**Reflection along the way.** In the architecture reflection stands last, but it works not only at the end: background observation catches an error earlier—to notice a missed clarification during framework selection is easier than after the conclusion. There is also the opposite extreme: if reflection interrupts every step, thinking is paralyzed. The balance is checkpoints at the junctions of domains: before framework selection—has enough been recognized and clarified; before the conclusion—is the comparison complete; before action—have the limits been taken into account.

The six domains are a way of moving thought: once mastered, it acts in everything a person henceforth considers seriously. When the principles become reflexes, navigation ceases to be a technique and becomes **a form of attention**. The architecture does not prescribe a result; it prescribes *movement*: to see what is; to clarify what thought touches; to choose a framework consciously; to compare on the merits; to infer from sufficient reason; to return to one’s own limits.

## Intuition and Reasoning

Before analyzing what has been noticed, it makes sense to let [[Intuition|intuition]] show itself. Intuition is the transmission of information to consciousness in response to its request, bypassing discursive mediation: the whole is grasped at once. There are two modes: *direction*—the answer to “what is to be done?”, its conductor is the body; *knowledge*—the answer to “what is?”, its conductor is consciousness. By its material the intuitive relies on what is built in or on what has ripened—settled experience that has become tacit. In reasoning intuition runs through all six domains: it suggests where to look, what is essential, which framework fits the object; an experienced physician recognizes a disease before naming its signs. Intuition does not pass a verdict—it begins; discursive thought unfolds the whole into checkable steps.

To recognize a signal and to check what has come are different matters. A signal is recognized by its sign: whoever has a good connection with their own body recognizes intuition, for example, by gooseflesh ([[Intuition#Reception, Errors, Criterion|criterion]]). The sign says only that this is intuition; whether what has come is understood rightly, checking decides: both the channel and the reception can distort, and expectation and emotional background settle on the signal. Ripened intuition is reliable where the environment is stable and feedback is fast: the chess player and the firefighter “feel” rightly, the stock trader does not.[^4] The rule is therefore strict, and strictest at the entrance, in recognition: **intuition begins, discursive thought checks.**

## Fallacies of Reasoning

The same architecture also explains how thought goes astray. A logical [[Fallacies|fallacy]] is a failure in the architecture of reasoning, and every failure has an address: the conditions of reasoning, a domain, order, or the process as a whole. The primary address is one—the place where the violation begins; in other domains the same error is called a mechanism of that domain. Hence every error has an address, a mechanism, and a diagnostic question. The [[Fallacy Catalog|catalog]] has one hundred fifty-two records in five types—according to what exactly they violate:

1. **[[01 Violations of Conditions|Violations of Conditions]]—38: reasoning does not begin.** Its place is taken by manipulation, coercion, or a defective question. Two groups: [[1.A Question Framing Errors|errors of posing a question]] (the loaded question, merged questions, the taboo, the [[Impossible Goal|impossible goal]]) and substitutions for reasoning—force, pity, emotion, gain, false authority, disinformation, or pressure in place of an argument.

2. **[[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations]]—105: failure within a domain.** Reasoning proceeds, but one of the six functions is performed defectively; this is the largest type of the catalog—one group per domain: [[2.1 Domain 1 Recognition|Recognition (26)]], [[2.2 Domain 2 Clarification|Clarification (13)]], [[2.3 Domain 3 Framework Selection|Framework Selection (16)]], [[2.4 Domain 4 Comparison|Comparison (8)]], [[2.5 Domain 5 Inference|Inference (20)]], [[2.6 Domain 6 Reflection|Reflection (22)]]. Here live the classical fallacies—from projection and selective blindness at the entrance to equivocation and inference beyond the grounds.

3. **[[03 Sequence Violations|Violations of Sequence]]—3: the order of transitions is broken.** Two forms of order failure have records: reverse movement—rationalization and the vicious circle—and burden inversion—shifting the burden of proof. All eight forms are treated above, in “Movement through the Domains.”

4. **[[04 Syndromes|Syndromes]]—6: a systemic distortion of the whole process.** Pervasive, self-reinforcing, and invisible to the one affected: confirmation bias (the will serves comfort, not truth), motivated reasoning (the whole course is led by the desired conclusion), groupthink. Their address is the process as a whole.

5. **[[05 Context Methods|Context-Dependent Methods]]—6: two-faced.** Valid under their conditions and erroneous outside them: [[Trust Your Gut|trust in intuition]], [[Affective Fallacy|affective evaluation]], [[Appeal to Tradition|the appeal to tradition]] and [[Appeal to Nature|to nature]], [[Argumentum Ex Silentio|the argument from silence]] and [[Argument from Consequences|from consequences]]. The task of the type is the discernment of conditions. Each of the six methods has a twin among the domain violations: it is one record with two faces—an error with an address in a domain and a method with conditions of validity. That is why the five types count one hundred fifty-eight faces and one hundred fifty-two records.

The pages of the types treat the most frequent representatives—those on which the mechanism is seen most clearly.

## Paradoxes

A well-formed statement makes a paradox impossible: a paradox is a diagnostic signal, an indication of where the architecture of reasoning is violated or where the premises carry an unrecognized defect. In reality there are no paradoxes.

A paradox is therefore laid open: one finds where the defect lies—in the construction, in the premises, or in the expectation itself—and then it is seen that the paradox had nowhere to form. The method is domain analysis: the paradox is led through the six [[Domains of Reasoning|domains]], and the point of failure becomes visible. The [[Paradox Catalog|forty-six classical paradoxes]] fall into three categories—by the place of the defect:

1. **[[Structural Paradoxes]]—13: level confusion.** The construction itself confuses levels: an operation takes itself as an object, an element acts as its own rule. This is an error of construction along the vertical of the [[Order|Law of Order]]: in a well-formed statement it is impossible. The [[Liar Paradox|“Liar”]]—a statement evaluates its own truth-value; [[Russell's Paradox|Russell]]—the set of all sets that do not contain themselves contains itself if and only if it does not contain itself; [[Cantor's Paradox|Cantor]]—the set of all sets would have to be both larger and not larger than the set of its subsets; at the root is the totality taken as a finished element of itself. The “Liar” stands on both axes of Order: levels are confused and Criterion Precedence is violated. To lay such a paradox open is to see the violation of the hierarchy of levels: the construction is ill-formed.

2. **[[Defective Paradoxes]]—25: a flaw in the premises.** The construction is correct, the defect is in the premises: an unclarified concept ([[Sorites Paradox|the sorites]]—“heap” is not clarified as a graded concept; the [[Ship of Theseus]]—“the same” without a criterion of identity), a hidden contradiction ([[Unexpected Hanging|the unexpected hanging]]—“an announced surprise”), a false assumption ([[Zenos Achilles|Achilles and the tortoise]]—an infinite number of steps does not mean an infinite time), or an error of reasoning. Each flaw has an address in the domains of reasoning; to lay the paradox open is to find the defect of the premise.

3. **[[Non Paradoxes|Non-Paradoxes]]—8: lagging intuition.** The result is correct—intuition lags behind: [[Monty Hall]]—under the usual rules the host knows where the prize is and opens an empty door, so switching pays: the chance is two thirds; [[Birthday Paradox|birthdays]]—the number of pairs grows quadratically. It is intuition that needs correcting; the result needs no correction.

The cards of the [[Paradox Catalog|catalog]] analyze the mechanism; structural and defective paradoxes have a section “Why There Is No Paradox,” non-paradoxes a section “Why This Is Not a Paradox.”

## Summary

1. **Reasoning is the process of searching for an answer to a question:** the directed movement of thought from a question to a grounded answer. As a system: the goal is a grounded answer; the rules are the laws of logic; the roles are the six domains; the elements are the contents of thought.
2. **The laws of thinking and the laws of reality are the same laws:** that is why a rightly built chain of thoughts leads to absolute truth about the world.
3. **Six domains**—recognition, clarification, framework selection, comparison, inference, reflection; each has a guiding question and a principle. Reflection is a special role: the position of observation above the slice, present at all domains.
4. **Dependence is ordered, transitions are free.** There are eight forms of order failure; returns are a regular move; the error is sought in the earliest violated domain.
5. **Intuition begins, discursive thought checks:** the sign says that intuition has come; whether what has come is understood rightly, checking decides.
6. **An error has an address:** conditions, a domain, order, or the process as a whole; the catalog has one hundred fifty-two records in five types.
7. **A well-formed statement makes a paradox impossible;** in reality there are no paradoxes.
