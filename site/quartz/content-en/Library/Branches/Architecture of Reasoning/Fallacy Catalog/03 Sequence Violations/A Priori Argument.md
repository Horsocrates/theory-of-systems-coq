**3.2.1** · [[03 Sequence Violations|Violations of Sequence (Type 3)]] → [[3.2 Backward Movement|3.2 Reverse Movement]] · subtype: Reasoning from the Conclusion to the Premises

**Formulation.** One starts from a conclusion accepted in advance and looks for any arguments to justify it

**Mechanism of the violation.** The Law of Order is violated by inversion: instead of moving D1→D5, reasoning goes D5→D1. The conclusion precedes the premises; the premises are selected to fit the conclusion.

**Example.** “Since we know that our instrument does not err, our task is to explain why the data show otherwise”

**Diagnostic question.** Was the conclusion formed BEFORE the data were examined? Would the conclusion change with different data?

**Psychological source.** Confirmation bias, cognitive dissonance

**Related fallacies:** 4.1, 2.1.3, [[Circular Reasoning]]
