**3.2.2** · [[03 Sequence Violations|Violations of Sequence (Type 3)]] → [[3.2 Backward Movement|3.2 Reverse Movement]] · subtype: Circular Dependence of Premises and Conclusion

**Formulation.** A is true because of B, and B is true because of A; or the conclusion is contained in the premises

**Mechanism of the violation.** A violation of sequence: reasoning returns to its starting point. The conclusion is already presupposed rather than following from independent premises. The movement D1→D5 is closed into a loop.

**Example.** “Witches are the most serious spiritual threat. Why? Because witches threaten our salvation”

**Diagnostic question.** Does my premise prove the conclusion, or does it itself presuppose the conclusion?

**Psychological source.** The illusion of justification

**Related fallacies:** [[A Priori Argument]], 2.2.4
