**2.1.3.1** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]] → [[2.1 Domain 1 Recognition|2.1 Recognition]] · subtype: Data Filtering

**Formulation.** Disproportionate attention to information that is readily available, ignoring broader data

**Mechanism of the violation.** Filtering at the input: which data we “see” is determined by their availability in memory, not by their relevance. The first or the last information received gets disproportionate weight.

**Example.** “We know from experience that this doesn’t work”—where “experience” means the latest local attempt, ignoring successful experience elsewhere

**Diagnostic question.** Am I basing my conclusion on what is easier to recall or on the full picture?

**Psychological source.** Availability heuristic, anchoring

**Related fallacies:** 2.1.3, 2.4.4
