**Group 2.3** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]]

A mismatch between the framework of evaluation and the object of inquiry.

**Principle:** the Principle of Objectivity.

<!-- PROSE:BEGIN -->
## Why the Domain Is Vulnerable

The choice of a framework most often remains invisible: we look *through* the framework, not *at* it, as through glasses we have forgotten to take off. “I’m just looking at the facts” is already a framework, only an unconscious one; the very concepts of “fact” and “the obvious” presuppose a certain way of seeing. Thomas Kuhn showed this for science: scientists working in different paradigms literally see different things in the same data (*The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*, 1962). Invisibility is exactly what makes the framework vulnerable: it cannot be contested without being noticed; one cannot choose otherwise without knowing that one is choosing. And it is acquired early—absorbed with language and culture before we are capable of reflection—and it holds the more firmly the more pleasant it is: a framework in which I am the hero is more comfortable than one in which I am to blame. The Principle of Objectivity does not demand a “view from nowhere”—there is no such thing; it demands what is possible: that the framework correspond to the nature of the phenomenon and to the goal, and that the choice be conscious.

## The Unity of the Fallacies of Framework Selection

The fallacies of the third domain are different ways of choosing the framework wrongly: to apply it to the wrong category, to transfer it on the strength of a superficial similarity, to confuse fact with norm in one direction or the other, to make an irrelevant feature the criterion, to pick an optic to suit what is desired. One thing unites them: a framework has been chosen—but without awareness that a choice was made, without a check of its fit, without readiness to revise it. The questions of the Principle of Objectivity—why do I look at it in just this way? does the framework fit the object? did I choose it because it will give what I want?—do not guarantee the right choice, but they make the choice *visible*, and that is the first condition for its being open to revision.

The work of the domain, an analysis of frequent fallacies (the category mistake, false analogy, the naturalistic and moralistic fallacies, appeals to tradition/novelty/nature/popularity, confirmation bias), and the cases “Champollion” and “Lobotomy” are on the domain page [[3 Framework Selection|Framework Selection]].
