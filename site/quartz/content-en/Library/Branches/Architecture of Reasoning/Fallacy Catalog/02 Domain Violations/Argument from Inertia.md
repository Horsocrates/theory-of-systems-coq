**2.6.3.1** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]] → [[2.6 Domain 6 Reflection|2.6 Reflection]] · subtype: Past Investment Influence

**Formulation.** One must continue a mistaken course, because to admit the error would be to devalue all the sacrifices

**Mechanism of the violation.** Reflection is blocked by sunk cost: past investments (effort, sacrifice, blood) are used as an argument against revising the decision. “We’ve already invested so much” is not an argument that the course is right.

**Example.** “We can’t shut down the project—otherwise two years of work will have been in vain”

**Diagnostic question.** Is a past investment an argument for continuing? If I were starting now, would I choose the same course?

**Psychological source.** Sunk cost fallacy, avoidance of cognitive dissonance

**Related fallacies:** 2.6.3, [[Appeal to Tradition]], [[Argument from Consequences]]
