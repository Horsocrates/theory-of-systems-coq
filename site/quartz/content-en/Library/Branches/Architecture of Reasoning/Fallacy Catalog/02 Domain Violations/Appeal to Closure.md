**2.6.1.1** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]] → [[2.6 Domain 6 Reflection|2.6 Reflection]] · subtype: Illusion of Completion

**Formulation.** A demand to accept a conclusion in order to “close the question”; a refusal to admit that some questions may remain open

**Mechanism of the violation.** Reflection must acknowledge limits and open questions. The demand for “closure” shuts reflection down by force: the conclusion is declared final not because it is grounded but because “certainty is needed.”

**Example.** “We need a final decision—people are tired of the issue”: closure instead of an analysis on the merits

**Diagnostic question.** Is the “need for closure” a logical ground for a conclusion? Can the question remain open?

**Psychological source.** Intolerance of uncertainty

**Related fallacies:** 2.6.1
