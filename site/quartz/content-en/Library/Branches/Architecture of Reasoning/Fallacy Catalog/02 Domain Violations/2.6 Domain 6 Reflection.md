**Group 2.6** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]]

Refusal to acknowledge the limits of a conclusion.

**Principle:** the Principle of Limitation.

<!-- PROSE:BEGIN -->
## Why the Domain Is Vulnerable

Reflection is difficult and therefore often fails to do its work. What is usually visible is the result; one’s own course of thought stays in the shadow until one turns to it, and that is a separate effort which the mind avoids, hurrying on to the next task. In addition, a conclusion is *my* conclusion, tied to my competence and self-esteem; whoever mistakes their records about themselves for themselves sees in an error of the conclusion an error in themselves and defends the record—the justification of their position—rather than absolute truth ([[Metaphysics#The Ego Zone|“The Ego Zone”]]). The more tightly a conclusion is interwoven with identity—professional, political, personal—the fiercer the defense; precisely where reflection is needed most, it meets the greatest resistance.

## The Unity of the Fallacies of Reflection

The fallacies of the sixth domain are different ways of keeping reflection from taking place: to declare a conclusion final, to assess oneself with an unfit instrument, to defend a conclusion for the sake of what has been invested in it, to check asymmetrically, to refuse to revise in spite of the grounds. One thing unites them: the limits are not drawn, the errors are not noticed, the revision does not happen. Without reflection, conclusions ossify into dogmas, and knowledge turns into belief; with reflection it stays alive—it is refined, corrected, and grows.

The work of the domain, an analysis of frequent fallacies (the illusion of completion, the Dunning–Kruger effect, sunk costs, confirmation bias in reflection, refusal to revise), and the cases “The Surgical Checklist,” “Lord Kelvin,” and “Semmelweis” are on the domain page [[6 Reflection|Reflection]].
