**Group 2.4** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]]

A defective application of the framework to the material.

**Principle:** the nodal domain: all four laws at once.

<!-- PROSE:BEGIN -->
## Why the Domain Is Vulnerable

The discipline of comparison is simple and old: Aristotle already required, in the *Topics*, that one compare in one and the same respect, by one and the same criterion, between comparable things. The rules are simple—following them is hard, because comparison seems obvious: put two objects side by side, and the differences are visible. But only the differences that catch the eye are visible; those that require attention stay in the shadow, and the result is easily adjusted to what is desired—unnoticed even by the one who reasons.

## The Unity of the Fallacies of Comparison

The fallacies of the fourth domain are different ways of breaking comparison: to equate falsely what is different, to juxtapose the incomparable, to shift the criterion, to leave it incomplete, to put a nonexistent ideal in place of the real. The Aristotelian requirements—one respect, one criterion, comparable objects—are violated in one way or another, and comparison, while remaining convincing in appearance, misses what is essential. Comparison is the nodal domain: the whole path to the conclusion holds in it, and therefore its defect is especially costly. The discipline is one: to compare what is there—honestly, fully, by stable criteria; not what is convenient for the chart, and not what the bosses expect.

The work of the domain, an analysis of frequent fallacies (false equivalence, comparing the incomparable, moving the goalposts, incomplete comparison, the utopia fallacy), and the cases “The Judgment of Paris” and “*Challenger*” are on the domain page [[4 Comparison|Comparison]].
