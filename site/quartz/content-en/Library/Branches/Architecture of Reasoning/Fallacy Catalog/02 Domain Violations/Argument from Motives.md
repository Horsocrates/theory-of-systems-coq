**2.1.2.2** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]] → [[2.1 Domain 1 Recognition|2.1 Recognition]] · subtype: Object Substitution (A→B)

**Formulation.** An argument is declared invalid because of the motives of the one who advances it

**Mechanism of the violation.** Object substitution: instead of the argument (A), the motives of the speaker (B) are considered. But even evil people sometimes tell the truth, and good people make mistakes. A motive does not determine truth-value.

**Example.** “Our competitor wants us to leave the market—so we stay”—a decision driven by someone else’s wish, not by calculation

**Diagnostic question.** Am I evaluating the argument on its merits or by whom it benefits?

**Psychological source.** Suspicion, tribalism

**Related fallacies:** [[Ad Hominem Argument]], 1.B.6
