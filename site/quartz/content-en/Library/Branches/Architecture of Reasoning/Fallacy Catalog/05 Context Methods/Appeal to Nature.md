**2.3.5.3** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]] → [[2.3 Domain 3 Framework Selection|2.3 Framework Selection]] · subtype: Irrelevant Criterion

**Formulation.** “Natural” = good, “unnatural” = bad. Naturalness as a criterion of quality or morality.

**Mechanism of the violation.** The framework of evaluation is chosen by an irrelevant criterion: “naturalness” has no connection with usefulness, with safety, or with morality. Arsenic is natural; antibiotics are not.

**Example.** “Our tea is made from 100% organic poison ivy leaves—no additives, nothing but nature!”

**Diagnostic question.** Is “naturalness” a relevant criterion for this evaluation? Is everything natural good?

**Psychological source.** The romanticization of nature, the heuristic “natural = safe”

**Related fallacies:** 2.3.3, 2.3.4


## As a Method (Type 5)

**5.2.2 · [[5.2 Traditional Cognition\|5.2 Traditional Cognition]].** The method is valid under its conditions; the task is discernment, and only outside them would it be ruled out.

**When it is valid.** Natural systems have passed through evolutionary selection; the body has wisdom (homeostasis)

**When it is a fallacy.** The automatic “natural = good” (arsenic is natural too)

**Mechanism of discernment.** Valid: taking evolutionary adaptation into account. Fallacy: identifying the “natural” with the “good.”

**Valid:** Breastfeeding is evolutionarily optimized for the infant

**Fallacious:** “Homosexuality is unnatural”—the naturalistic fallacy in ethics
