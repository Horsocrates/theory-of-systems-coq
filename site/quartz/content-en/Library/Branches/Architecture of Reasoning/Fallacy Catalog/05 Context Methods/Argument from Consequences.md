**2.5.4.1** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]] → [[2.5 Domain 5 Inference|2.5 Inference]] · subtype: Scale Error / Exceeding the Grounds

**Formulation.** A claim cannot be true because, if it were, the consequences would be unacceptable

**Mechanism of the violation.** A conclusion about truth-value is drawn from the undesirability of the consequences. But the desirability/undesirability of the consequences does not affect the truth-value of the premises. “I don’t like the conclusion” ≠ “the premise is false.”

**Example.** “The diagnosis can’t be right—otherwise I would have to rebuild my whole life”

**Diagnostic question.** Does the undesirability of the consequences affect the truth-value of the claim?

**Psychological source.** Cognitive dissonance, anxiety avoidance

**Related fallacies:** 4.2


## As a Method (Type 5)

**5.3.2 · [[5.3 Indirect Cognition\|5.3 Indirect Cognition]].** The method is valid under its conditions; the task is discernment, and only outside them would it be ruled out.

**When it is valid.** In ethics consequences are relevant (consequentialism); pragmatism: truth = what works

**When it is a fallacy.** Denying what is true because of unpleasant consequences; wishful thinking

**Mechanism of discernment.** Valid: “a tree is known by its fruit” (the evaluation of practices). Fallacy: “This cannot be true, because it is terrible.”

**Valid:** Evaluating a policy by its results is a legitimate approach

**Fallacious:** “Evolution cannot be true—otherwise life is meaningless”
