**2.5.4.3** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]] → [[2.5 Domain 5 Inference|2.5 Inference]] · subtype: Scale Error / Exceeding the Grounds

**Formulation.** The silence of sources or the absence of data in itself proves that a claim is true

**Mechanism of the violation.** The conclusion exceeds its ground: a positive conclusion is drawn from the absence of information. “The sources are silent about X” ≠ “X is true/false.” Silence is not proof.

**Example.** “He can’t say where he was that evening. So he’s the one who smashed the shop window!”—silence read as evidence

**Diagnostic question.** Is silence/the absence of data proof of anything?

**Psychological source.** Intolerance of uncertainty, confirmation bias

**Related fallacies:** [[Argument from Ignorance]]


## As a Method (Type 5)

**5.3.1 · [[5.3 Indirect Cognition\|5.3 Indirect Cognition]].** The method is valid under its conditions; the task is discernment, and only outside them would it be ruled out.

**When it is valid.** When silence is expected and significant in the context (history, law, science)

**When it is a fallacy.** Arbitrary interpretation of silence; “not refuted = proved”

**Mechanism of discernment.** Valid: the absence of mention in sources where it was expected. Fallacy: silence = consent.

**Valid:** The absence of any mention of an event by contemporaries is an argument against its historicity

**Fallacious:** “You didn’t object—so you agree”
