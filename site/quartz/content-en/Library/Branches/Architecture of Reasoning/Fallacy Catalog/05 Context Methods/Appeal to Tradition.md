**2.3.5.4** · [[02 Domain Violations|Domain Violations (Type 2)]] → [[2.3 Domain 3 Framework Selection|2.3 Framework Selection]] · subtype: Irrelevant Criterion

**Formulation.** “It has always been this way” as an argument for the rightness of a position or practice

**Mechanism of the violation.** The framework of evaluation: “antiquity/traditionality = rightness.” The duration of a practice has no connection with its truth or its moral justification. Slavery, too, was a tradition.

**Example.** “We have always kept the records in a notebook—let’s not break a long-standing tradition”

**Diagnostic question.** Is “it has always been so” a relevant ground for “it ought to be so”?

**Psychological source.** Status quo bias, nostalgia

**Related fallacies:** [[Appeal to Nature]], [[Argument from Inertia]]


## As a Method (Type 5)

**5.2.1 · [[5.2 Traditional Cognition\|5.2 Traditional Cognition]].** The method is valid under its conditions; the task is discernment, and only outside them would it be ruled out.

**When it is valid.** Tradition as a filter of generations (the Lindy effect), Chesterton’s fence

**When it is a fallacy.** Blind following without understanding the reasons; tradition as the only argument

**Mechanism of discernment.** Valid: “This has worked for centuries—it is worth understanding why.” Fallacy: “It has always been done this way—so it is right.”

**Valid:** The traditional diet of a region is optimized for local conditions

**Fallacious:** “Women have always stayed at home—and so it should be”
