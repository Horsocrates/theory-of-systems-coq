**1.A.7** · [[01 Violations of Conditions|Violations of Conditions (Type 1)]] → [[1.A Question Framing Errors|1.A Errors of Posing]] · subtype: Demand for an Impossible Answer

**Formulation.** The goal of the question—what will count as an answer—outlines the impossible: the completed corpus of an unbounded process or a contradictory category; the answer is excluded by the question itself

**Mechanism of the violation.** A corruption of the goal: the rule for recognizing the answer demands an object from the layer “cannot be”—a completed totality where only a process exists. The ground and the direction are intact; the question looks correct, but it cannot be closed in principle. The anatomy of the question (goal · direction · ground) predicted it as the third kind of corruption of the goal—and it was found.

**Example.** “Name all the prime numbers”—the primes are given by a process of generation; there is no corpus-object; the correct form is “give the process of generation.” “Give a complete list of everything you do not know”

**Diagnostic question.** Can what the goal demands exist—at least potentially? Does the question demand a completed corpus where there is only a process? Remedy: a processual reformulation.

**Psychological source.** Substituting a completed object for a process

**Related fallacies:** [[Taboo|Taboo / Dogmatism]], [[Venue Fallacy]]
