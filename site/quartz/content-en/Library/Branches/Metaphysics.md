Metaphysics is the doctrine of ontological principles: what being is and how the laws of [[Logic]] are given in it.

## Etymology

**Metaphysics.** Greek τὰ μετὰ τὰ φυσικά (ta meta ta physika), “the things after the physics”: this, by tradition, is how Andronicus of Rhodes, editing Aristotle’s works in the first century BCE, named the books he placed after the *Physics*. The name comes from the order of the treatises; Aristotle himself called the subject **first philosophy**—the science of being as being and of its first principles. Later the preposition was read differently—μετά (meta) as “beyond”: metaphysics is about what lies beyond nature. A bibliographical accident became the name of the subject, and the reading is right in substance: **physics** is from φύσις (physis), “nature,” and φύσις is from φύω (phyō), “to bring forth, to grow”: the science of what is born and grows; **after** it comes what is not born—what has no generating step.

**Ontology.** Greek ὄν, ὄντος (on, ontos), “being,” + λόγος (logos), “account”: the word is new, of the early seventeenth century; in Wolff, ontology is general metaphysics, the doctrine of being in general, as distinct from the special ones—of the world, the soul, and God. The term of this page—**ontological principles**—stands in that place: the principles by which anything is at all.

## Ontological Principles

**Existence is a status, not a system.** “*A* = exists” assigns a status: *A* “can be—and is.” The Law of Sufficient Reason asks: what grounds must there be for anything at all to be able to receive the status “exists”? The answer to this question is what derives metaphysics: **Logic** and the **Source** are two systems that must be and always are; their status is “cannot not be,” and they have no generating step. The grammar of the first principle holds both: “*A*” points to the content—the Source, the fullness of potential; “exists” points to the form—Logic, its five laws. Reality does not stand without both, and each is necessary in its own right: without form, content is indeterminate—not distinguished from nothing; without content, the structure is empty—it has nothing to arrange. The Source is precisely a fullness, not a void: actualization manifests its potential, and it remains full.

**The Source within Logic.** The two necessary systems stand in a relation of nesting: the Source is within Logic, as the filling within a structure. A system of a higher level sets the rules for the nested one—and Logic is the system of rules, so its place at the top is unconditional: above the system of all rules there is no one to set rules. The principles nonetheless remain two: they are genetically independent—neither is generated by the other, and both are necessary; nesting is a structural relation: a structure and its filling. What kind of duality this is, and how it differs from the classical dualisms, is shown below, in “Two Principles.”

**The Absolute is the meta-system.** Logic and the Source cover everything that exists: every being is filling in form. Together they make up the Absolute—the sum of all that is: one whole, in which Logic is the rules and the Source is the filling.

**The act of distinction is the meeting of form and content.** Logic (form) recognizes a potential in the Source (content) and applies form to it—*A*₁ is actualized. *A*₁ = content-in-form = the determinate. The Source is unchanged by this: actualization is manifestation, not extraction. Logic is unchanged: its laws are constant and confirm themselves. Only the space of actualized distinctions grows: it is neither the Source nor Logic—it is **the record of their meetings**. This record is kept by the mental field—another system, treated below: the record of the meetings makes up the actualized layer of the objective field (the section “The Mental Field”).

**Light is manifestness as such.** The act of distinction, taken by itself, is a system—the meeting of form and content—and, like every system, it has its own witness. Everything visible requires it: to see is to see what is manifest, and it is the act that manifests. Light is that which is evident in itself and makes other things evident: this is Suhrawardī’s definition of light, in the philosophy of illumination. Manifestation implies that something is manifest—and by Sufficient Reason what is manifest requires Light. Hence there are two witnesses in the ground—Logic and the Source—and three in every manifest experience: the system is **dual in its ground and trinitarian in manifestation**. The third is Light.

**The count of witnesses: experience and reasoning.** In experience it is **differences** that are counted: total darkness distinguishes one thing—only the “I” (a witness in a single system with the Source); anything visible distinguishes two—the “I” and the other—and the visible sufficiently grounds the presence of Light. The ontological count of **witnesses** is always greater by one: darkness—two (Logic and the Source); the visible—three (plus Light). **Witnesses = differences + 1**, and that one is always Logic.

> [!example]- Case: Logic is the condition of experience, not its content
> In no position of lived experience is Logic perceived: there is no sufficient ground there for turning attention to it—it is what makes experience determinate. It is discovered in one way only—by reasoning, by reconstructing the grounds after the fact: “what was required for this experience to be possible?” Only after the experience of total darkness can one realize that there were two witnesses. Kant’s move—the conditions of the possibility of experience are not given in experience itself—is obtained here by derivation from Sufficient Reason; both propositions are proved: Logic is not perceivable in any experience; the witnesses number the differences plus one ([[Formalization]]).

### Two Principles: The Duality of the Ground

**The two principles are called by name.** In the ground of Reality there are two principles: Logic and the Source. Each is conceived through itself: Logic is the system of rules, necessary and constant; the Source is the fullness of potential, unchanged in actualization. They do not turn into each other: Logic, meeting the Source, remains Logic and cannot become it; the Source does not become Logic. This is a genuine duality, and here it is acknowledged: in the ground there are two.

**The axis of the difference.** The classical dispute between monism and dualism is conducted along one axis: how many substances there are—self-standing things, each of which exists by itself. Here the axis is different. Existence is a status by Sufficient Reason, not a self-standing thinghood, and the question is put otherwise: how many necessary systems there are and in what relation they stand. The answer: two, nested, making up one whole—the Absolute. The difference from the classical dualisms therefore lies not in the count but in the structure—in six features.

1. **Category.** Systems, not substances: existence is assigned to them by the status “cannot not be”; neither principle is a thing among things.
2. **Relation.** Nesting with the setting of rules: the Source is within Logic—one principle sets the rules for the other. In this the relation differs from the coordination of two things, from the parallelism of attributes, and from the struggle of opposites.
3. **Distribution.** Every determinate thing is filling in form—both at once. The two principles do not divide what exists into two classes.
4. **Meeting.** The act of distinction—the meeting of form and content—is real, constitutive, and witnessed: not a causal link between two ready-made things but the very becoming of the determinate. The question of a bridge between the principles does not arise.
5. **The third.** What is manifest requires Light (above): the third is Light, and the system is dual in its ground and trinitarian in manifestation. The record of the meetings belongs to another system—the mental field: it is the actualized layer of the objective field.
6. **No tilt of value.** Neither principle is lower or darker than the other; both are unchanged in actualization: the Source remains full, Logic remains constant.

**A check by Spinoza.** Spinoza’s criterion—“is conceived through itself”—is met by both principles. But in Spinoza himself this criterion is also met by the attributes of one substance: each attribute is conceived through itself, and “it is far from absurd to ascribe several attributes to one substance” (*Ethics*, I, Proposition 10 and its scholium); thought and extension are irreducible and not interchangeable—and yet this is one substance. His second move decides the matter: things that have nothing in common with one another cannot be the cause of one another (I, Proposition 3). Logic and the Source meet in the act of distinction; therefore they do have something in common—one whole, the Absolute. In Spinoza’s vocabulary the position would read “one substance, two attributes”—with one divergence, which is its very point: Spinoza’s attributes run in parallel and do not interact (II, Proposition 7); here, by contrast, the principles meet, and one of them sets the rules for the other.

**The classical dualisms side by side.**

| Position | Two of what | Relation of the principles | Divergence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plato | Two worlds: the forms and the sensible | Participation—μέθεξις (methexis); the gap in it is laid bare by Plato himself in the *Parmenides* | The world is one: the Source within Logic; the actualized is no less real than the principles |
| Aristotle | Form and matter of one substance | Co-principles—ἀρχαί (archai); not counted as a dualism | The nearest relative in relation; but his matter is possibility marked by a lack of form, while the Source is fullness; and the meeting of form and content has a witness |
| Descartes | Mind and body—two created substances (*Principles of Philosophy*, I, 51–60) | Separable—a real distinction; their interaction is a problem: the question of Elisabeth of Bohemia (1643) | The principles are necessary and uncreated, nested, and inseparable in Reality; the meeting needs no bridge |
| Spinoza | One substance, two known attributes | Parallelism without interaction | The distinctness is the same; in place of parallelism—meeting and nesting |
| Sāṃkhya | Puruṣa and Prakṛti | The union of “the lame and the blind”; the goal is isolation | The nearest relative in composition—see below |
| Manichaeism, the Gnostics | Light and darkness; spirit, and matter as evil | Struggle | The principles are functions, not values; there is no darkness in the ground |

**The nearest relative is Sāṃkhya.** It is the only one of the classical dualisms in whose ground stand a witness and an unmanifest potential. Puruṣa is pure consciousness, the witness—sākṣin, an inactive spectator; Prakṛti is unmanifest—avyakta—nature, the equilibrium of the three guṇas, the potential of everything manifest; the world unfolds from their union, which Īśvarakṛṣṇa’s *Sāṃkhya-kārikā* likens to the alliance of a lame man and a blind man (kārikā 21; on the witness, kārikā 19). The likeness is in composition: a witness, a potential, manifestation at their meeting, distinction as the key. The divergence is in structure. In Sāṃkhya it is Prakṛti that is active, and consciousness is inactive; here it is form that is active: Logic recognizes a potential and applies form, while the Source is full and at rest. In Sāṃkhya the Puruṣas are many from the outset; here Logic is one, and witnesses multiply by derivation (witnesses = differences + 1). In Sāṃkhya the principles stand side by side, and their union is a riddle of the same kind as in Descartes; here they are nested. The goal of Sāṃkhya is the isolation—kaivalya—of the witness from nature; here there is no separation and none is required: distinctions are indestructible, and the record of the meetings grows.

**In sum.** A dualism of principles within one whole—as against the classical dualism of two self-standing things. Two is the count of the principles; the whole is one; the third is Light: manifestness as such.

## The Witness

**The word.** The Russian word for witness, *svidetel’*, goes back to the older *sŭvědětel’*: *sŭ-*, “together,” + *věděti*, “to know”: **the co-knower**, the one in whose presence there is knowledge; a witness in court is the one who *knows* how it was and can therefore attest. The word points to knowledge, not to sight: the present-day vowel in it is a later assimilation to *videt’*, “to see.” The English *witness* says the same: Old English *witnes*, from *wit*, “knowledge.” The witness is a function of knowledge—the one *for whom* something becomes known, for whom data become information ([[Epistemology]]).

**The derivation in five steps.**[^1]

1. To exist is to exist *determinately*.
2. To exist determinately is to be *distinguished*.
3. To be distinguished means that the difference must *register*: by Sufficient Reason a distinction has a ground—the very difference between *A* and not-*A*; but a ground that grounds nothing—a difference that makes no difference to anything—is no ground at all. So there is that *for which* *A* and not-*A* differ: a **perspective**.
4. The minimal perspective is *A* itself: not-*A* is defined through *A*, and the boundary between them is the very fact of their separateness. *A* = *A* is a statement with content: from the side of *A* itself, it is determinately *A*. This minimal self-referential determinacy is **self-witnessing**.
5. **The witness** is the function of the one who witnesses. Every act of distinction creates a new locus of self-witnessing—a new, minimal witness. Consciousness is the active face of Identity: the dynamic side of Identity; it would be a separate substance only in a dualism of substances.

**Acts, not composites.** What witnesses are the distinctions that make up a stone; the stone itself is the composite result of many distinctions. Consciousness belongs to **energies with a unique distinguishing quality**—to that which makes *A*₁ precisely *A*₁; physical bodies are manifestations of the energies and are witnessed *through* them: in this the position differs from panpsychism. Each level of the hierarchy of Order has its own witness, and the witnesses of the levels do not add up: “my” consciousness is the witness of the unique energy that I am, not the sum of the witnesses of electrons. That is why the combination problem does not arise here. A witness is set apart from a parent witness, in which it abided as a potential—witnesses form a genealogy parallel to the hierarchy of systems; the witness of a hammer is the parent one, higher in the hierarchy: the hammer has no ground of its own for a witness.

**The limit of the derivation.** What is established is consciousness in the minimal, structural sense—the self-witnessing of an act. The relation of structural witnessing to phenomenal experience (the “hard problem”) is an open question.

**The witness is a role.** A role is the way in which a system takes part in something; the witness is the way of taking part in knowing. Personality, the brain, the inner voice are what *is witnessed*; what witnesses is the role itself. A role is occupied in relation to something—that is a **position**: a witness is always a witness *of something*, and it occupies its position in two ways: *outside* what is known, observing from the side—or *inside* it, going through it by its own course (knowing-how). In both cases it is in presence, in direct contact; only the place differs.

**The constitutive core** is the necessary minimum without which knowing ceases to be knowing; it consists of exactly three features:

- the witness **confirms a difference and holds what is confirmed**: its capacity is no less than one; the difference itself lies in the essence of what is distinguished—the witness witnesses it and does not create it;
- it has **at least one channel** of meeting with what is known: without a meeting there are neither data nor meaning;
- its attention is **selective**: it picks out one aspect per step—a beam directed at one thing.

Anything less is no longer a witness. Anything more is a richer witness: reflection (knowing that one knows), the distinction of belief from knowledge by their ground, memory beyond the present holding. But the richness is not necessary: the minimal witness has none of this—and still knows.

**Boundary and identity.** The witness is itself a system—and its boundary is the boundary of that system: instruments do not enter into it; a telescope and a notebook are other systems with which it works, not parts of it. And its identity through time is its essence: it learns and changes but remains the same knower; “the same researcher” years later is the constancy of essence, not the invariance of composition.

**The limit of the witness** is two boundaries, both strict. The first: a witness does not step outside itself—to see a system whole, from the side, one must stand outside it, and a witness does not leave its own system: it is that system. It has no complete outside view of itself by itself. The second: no other witness will take this place from within either—another system it knows only from outside, by meeting; it cannot become that very system and be its inner witness. **A system has one inner witness—its own**; that is why the full knowledge of a system from within is borne only by the system itself.

**Indestructibility.** To destroy the witness *A*₁ would mean that *A*₁ had ceased to be *A*₁—against Identity. A witness changes its state—it acquires new distinctions—but it cannot cease *to be* a witness: Identity is unbreakable, and a distinction once made is recorded—it cannot be undone ([[Order]]).

## The Observer as a System

The sufficient ground of existence itself requires **the role of the witness**—the difference must register (the derivation in five steps above). Under this role an **element** is placed, and the element is itself a **system: the observer**—a system that has *who* observes: the witness; *what* does the perceiving: the Intellect; *how*: the channels; *in what*: the mental field. This is how the generation of levels works: the element of a role turns out to be itself a system one step lower. One name on two levels is no confusion: the observer carries the outer role of the witness *through* its inner function of witnessing. And the observer is also the **operator** of its own system: it directs attention by will.

An analysis of the observer by the triad [[Theory of Systems|Rules · Roles · Elements]] gives seven aspects:

1. **The roles** are two: the **witness**—the function of witnessing: it confirms a difference and holds what is confirmed; the **operator**—the function of choice and control with respect to the system.
2. **The Intellect** is a necessary aspect of the witness: that which perceives data and understands what is perceived—the acting capacity of the witness, working in the mental field.
3. **Perception** is the act of witnessing: the passage of data from the objective field into the subjective one. The path of the data runs through **channels**—the mechanism of perception, which belongs to the field itself: everything witnessed is within the field, for to perceive is to distinguish, what is distinguished is data, and data are a logical structure.
4. **Consciousness** is the degree of perception, a gradual one: the measure of the observer’s manifestness along the whole scale.
5. **Attention** is the direction from the witness to the witnessed: a vector, a beam.
6. **The will** is the potential between the known and the knowable; the sufficient ground of the fact that precisely *this* is witnessed: by it the observer chooses what to know; in cognition this selection is given its form by the question ([[Question]]).
7. **The act** is the action of distinction: the atomic unit of the system’s work.

By the triad: **the Rules** are the Intellect (the capacity), perception with its channels (the mechanism of passage), consciousness (the measure), attention (the vector), and the will (the potential—the ground of choice); **the Roles** are the witness and the operator; **the Elements** are the acts of distinction and their records. The generative order: the will marks out a region and directs attention → attention leads perception—the witness perceives by the Intellect → perception performs acts of distinction; consciousness measures the degree of the whole course.

Below, the aspects are unfolded one by one: time—the first consequence of the act; consciousness, the Intellect, and the Mental—three manifestations of Logic in the observer; the operator and the will; the Mental and the mental field.

## Time from Observation

**Time is the sequence of acts of observation**: observation comes first. Before the first act the observer is there (the capacity is from Logic), its state is empty, there are no changes—there is no time. The first act is the first moment: time begins. The arrow of time is fixed by the count itself: “forward” means “the distinctions have become more”—they are recorded and only added to ([[Order]]). Time is produced by the act of distinction. A witness has only the “now”—the current step: a state is attached to its step and does not return ([[Theory of Systems]]).

A consequence is the relativity of simultaneity: each observer has its own sequence of acts, and there is no outside clock; synchronization is possible only through shared distinctions. From this follow a finite maximum speed and the causal cone.[^1]

## The Manifestations of Logic

**The witness is a role; consciousness is its property.** The witness was derived above (the section “The Witness”): the role of that which confirms existence, knows, and can be known; it is that *by which* one looks, not that *at which* one looks. The vocabulary is exact in this way: it is the witness, a role, that witnesses; consciousness is its property.

**Three manifestations of Logic in the perceiver.** By Sufficient Reason the act of perception resolves into exactly three constituting moments:

- **Consciousness** is the potency: the very possibility of perceiving; it is gradual;
- **The Intellect** is the act: the acting capacity, the actualization of the potency—that which perceives and understands (the section “The Intellect”);
- **The Mental** is the medium: that by which perception is carried out; its contents are thought-forms, and its structure at work is the mental field (the sections below).

Each moment is necessary—remove any one, and there is no act; and there are exactly three. **The will**—directedness, attention—is a separate axis, not a fourth moment: perception persists even when directedness is lowered to zero (deep contemplation; this is the vector role of the will—see the section “The Operator and the Will”)—directedness is built on top of an act already constituted. That there are exactly three, that each moment is necessary, and that the will is separable are proved ([[Formalization]]).

**Consciousness is a gradual property of the witness.** It works along a spectrum of “more—less”: deep dreamless sleep—a minimum, yet more than zero · ordinary sleep · the lucid dream—evidence of gradualness itself: awareness can be partial · scattered wakefulness · concentration · full manifestation—when no subconscious remains and everything is aware in the moment. Where there is no observer, consciousness is not “equal to zero”—it is categorially inapplicable, like the colour of a number. **The witness is constant at every point of the scale; what varies is its property.** Hence the diagnosis of the expression “pure consciousness”: “pure” applies to substances, not to gradual properties; what is meant is fully manifest consciousness—a point on the scale.

**Instruments, not the foundation.** Language, inner speech, and images are instruments of the observer: some people have almost no inner speech (anendophasia),[^2] and yet on a number of tasks they do as well as everyone else—so reasoning is not the same as inner speech; the observer precedes its instruments and chooses among them.

**The witness does not see itself.** The eye does not see itself directly, and a mirror does not reflect itself: the witness cannot become a complete object for itself—it is always the one who looks; this is a structural feature of the role. In assessing themselves, people look inward—but biases work before conscious processing: the witness sees through its filter without seeing the filter itself—“the bias blind spot.”[^3] Rising along the scale is a matter of attention and of the will that directs it: the discipline of reasoning is in large part the art of keeping consciousness at a high step.

**In ethics** this is the foundation of the observer: good can be classified by an operator with sufficiently manifest consciousness who is in contact with the situation ([[Good and Evil]]).

## The Intellect

**The word.** *Intellect* renders the Russian *razum*. Russian *um*, “mind, wit,” ← Proto-Slavic *umъ, from *au-mo-: the root *au-, “to perceive, to notice”—possibly the same as in *yav’*, “the manifest,” and *yavit’*, “to make manifest”; Old Church Slavonic *umŭ* translated the Greek νοῦς (nous). ***Razum*** is *raz-* + *um*: the mind opened out, the full mind; the verb *razumet’* means “to understand.” The word says the same as the derivation: the Intellect is that by which one understands. Greek **νοῦς**, with the verb νοέω (noeō) formed from it: in Homer, “to notice, to discern,” and only then “to think”—mind as discernment before reasoning. Latin set apart *intellectus*—grasping, understanding—and *ratio*—reckoning, reasoning; among the scholastics the former stood higher. Kant fixed *Verstand* and *Vernunft* for them and reversed the ranks: with him the highest faculty became *Vernunft*. In English the pair is rendered as “understanding” and “reason.” Here the Intellect is taken in the elder sense of the word: that by which one understands.

**Definition.** The Intellect is the acting capacity of the witness: that which perceives data and understands what is perceived. In the triad of manifestations it is the act: the actualization of consciousness-as-possibility; the medium of the act is the Mental, and the place of its work is the mental field. To understand is to see a structure and its relations ([[Truth and Absolute Truth]]: understanding is the link of a phenomenon with its structure); therefore the Intellect is Logic acting in the observer: it distinguishes, gives the data form, and recognizes the rule in them.

**Perception and understanding are one act at two depths.** To perceive is to distinguish: the data receive form. To understand is to carry the form through to the structure: to see what makes *A* precisely *A*—its rule and its place in a system. Between them lie the stages of representation: opinion → knowing-that → understanding ([[Truth and Absolute Truth]]); the Intellect passes through them all, and the highest stage bears its own name.

**The bearer.** The Intellect is real functionally and ontologically: a capacity of the role and a manifestation of Logic; it is borne by the witness, not by the brain and not by a soul-thing.

**The modes of the Intellect** lie below the triad and are not new manifestations: **reflection** is the Intellect turned upon itself ([[6 Reflection]]); **wit** is the skill of commanding the Intellect; **wisdom** is the wholeness of what is understood (how it is composed is shown in [[Philosophy#Wisdom|the section on wisdom]]), and in action it is to find truth rightly ([[Ethics]]). Metaphysics says what the Intellect is; how it works is the six domains of the [[Architecture of Reasoning]]; its operations in the field are below.

### Three Operations

**Observation.** Thinking is observation: the operator-witness observes the information in its field in the form of thought-forms—a statement of what is there (the function of Domain 1). The witness observes, and the will directs the beam; no question is needed for this.

**Cognition** is activated by a [[Question|question]], a pointing act; the question narrows the field to “*A*” within its frame. It has two functions:

- **Deliberation** is thinking focused on a particular “*A*” within the frame of the question: a choice from a field of options (*A*₁, *A*₂, *A*₃, …), a selection from the available information. Its core is the functions of Domains 3–5; it uses Domains 1–5.
- **Reasoning** is sequential deliberation within the frame of the question: an analysis of logical structure—*what makes A = A?* Its core is the functions of Domains 2 and 6; it uses all six.

Deliberation and reasoning are different functions: the count of domains by core names the function, and the count by coverage names the toolkit used. Attention is directed by the will in both modes; what distinguishes the modes is not the presence of choice—attention is always selective—but the presence of a question. The full architecture of the functions is the [[Domains|six domains]] of the [[Architecture of Reasoning]] branch.

## The Operator and the Will

**The will is the potential between the known and the knowable.** The difference between the two fields—the subjective and the objective (the section “The Mental Field”)—creates an **attraction**: a striving to fill; and by this attraction the will works: it **marks out, from the region of the knowable, a part to be known**. The potential is inexhaustible: every record opens no less of the knowable than it closes—the difference does not level out, and the pull of knowing does not run dry ([[Epistemology|the gap of knowledge]]); the potential does not fall below its starting level, and the marking out of a part is possible at every step ([[Formalization]]).

**Two roles of the will: status and vector.** The will of the witness carries two roles: the **status** role—whether to be manifest—and the **vector** role—where to direct attention within manifestness. The status scale is quantized and has exactly two values—by the number of attainable statuses: **1—the unmanifest** (“can be, but is not”: only the will to be remains—a pure potential) and **2—the manifest** (“can be—and is”); the generating step is one quantum of will. **The scale does not go below one**: zero would mean “no will to be,” and the will to be is always there. The state of the minimum is the nirbīja-samādhi of the *Yoga Sūtras*:[^4] there is no will toward any experience, and the observer passes for a time into the status of the unmanifest—this is a change of status, not a destruction. The return is guaranteed by the structure: the will is a potential, the potential is inexhaustible—the attraction resumes, and manifestation returns.

**Status and position.** Status and **position** are independent coordinates of the witness. Position is a place in nested systems, and a witness moves only along its own line of nesting: it rises to what encompasses it while remaining itself. The witness of one of the three systems (the Source, Light, Logic) does not become the full witness of another: a system has one inner witness (the section “The Witness”)—from the Source one can expand into the boundless field, but one cannot become Logic. Hence the three upper positions of the line are three experiences, lived *as* the Source, *as* Light, *as* Logic: a taking part, not a substitution. **As the Source**—expansion into the boundless field; **as Light**—the experience of being Light (the experience described by Suhrawardī); **as Logic**—the state of knowing, understanding.

**The vector role is attention.** The manifestation of the will is attention: free will is the possibility of governing attention, and its manifestation is the possibility of choice (*A* / not-*A*). The will is an axis of its own, not reducible to the manifestations of Logic (the section “The Manifestations of Logic”). The arena of the work of attention is the mental field: every act of attention is a choice, and concentration—the holding of the frame—is a capacity of the will itself.

**The operator is the one who chooses.** The bearer of choice appears where the rules allow both a right and a wrong move—in a [[Soft and Hard System|soft system]]; the operator’s will inclines toward one side of the choice. The operator carries two distinct capacities: the will—it chooses—and the system of evaluation—it comes to know the criteria and keeps them stable. It does not produce the evaluation: an evaluation is the assignment of a status by the rules; the operator recognizes which evaluation stands behind a choice and checks it by the result. [[Intention]]—what result the operator wants to obtain—lives within it; in intention lives [[Ethics|ethics]], and free will is a necessary condition of its existence: ethics itself does not derive the will—it only points to its necessity. To take up one’s role—to learn the role, to learn its rules, to act in accord with them—is the maintenance of [[Order|order]].

**Free will is a derivation.** The will is an aspect of the witness: the potential between the known and the knowable, the attraction toward filling. The field of the knowable is always larger than the known—there is no completed corpus—and the attraction does not run dry. Attention, for its part, is selective: the witness picks out one aspect per step (the constitutive core); everything cannot be perceived at once. So at every step there is more than one thing to turn to, and one can turn to only one—at every step there is a **choice of direction**: where to direct attention, what to know. It is made by the witness itself—the vector is set from within, and a system has one inner witness. The second choice stands on a different ground: what is perceived does not prescribe the response—between the experience and the response stand the system of evaluation and the operator’s intention—and so there is also a **choice of response**: how to answer the experience. The two choices are **free will**: the freedom of direction, in knowing, and the freedom of response, in action; the second passes into [[Ethics]] together with intention. Freedom is inalienable: the scale of the will does not go below one, and the selectivity of attention is a necessary feature of the role; to take freedom away would be to take away either the will or the selectivity—that is, the witness itself.

## The Mental

**The Mental is the medium of perception**: that by means of which perception is carried out—the third moment of the triad of manifestations (above): Consciousness is the potency, the Intellect is the act, **the Mental is the medium**.

**Thought-forms.** The contents of the Mental are records in the modalities of the channels: auditory, visual, tactile, and others. Inner speech, images, and diagrams are kinds of record, not thinking itself (the section “The Manifestations of Logic,” “Instruments, not the foundation”); a thought-form is the clothing of a thought, not its essence.

**A medium, not a source.** The medium itself is a manifestation of Logic: the capacity is given by form. The filling of the medium, on the other hand, is the records of acts of distinction, the growing space of distinctions: it is neither the Source nor Logic—it is the record of their meetings (the section “Ontological Principles”). The medium is constant as a capacity; what grows is its contents. Taken at work, the Mental unfolds into a structure of two fields—the mental field (below).

## The Mental Field

**The mental field is the Mental at work**: the medium taken together with its contents and with the attention acting in it. The field is twofold: the **objective** one—the medium of Logic itself, where what exists is manifest independently of any observer—and the **subjective** one—the field of a particular witness. The field has two aspects: **attention**—an act, and every act of attention is a choice directed by the will; and **information**—the contents of the field in the form of thought-forms.

### The Objective and the Subjective

**The objective mental field** is the medium of Logic itself: in it are all the data that exist, independently of any observer. What exists is within Logic, which gives it form—and in this medium it is **manifest** as data, ready to be perceived by a witness. The objective field has three layers, by modality:

- **the necessary**—“cannot not be”: that which falls under the requirements of the Sufficient Reason *of existence itself*. It has no generating step: it may have a parent system, but it is given together with that system as necessary for existence itself; it was, is, and always will be—and time does not apply to it: it is given **before the first act**—the very act that generates time (the section “Time from Observation”). Such is everything that is an aspect of Logic itself—the laws, the triad Rules · Roles · Elements, the generation of levels—for Logic is given; such, too, is the role of the witness, required by existence itself (the section “The Observer as a System”);
- **the actualized**—“can be—and is”: that which can be observed and witnessed; it is generated by an act of distinction. This layer is the record of the meetings of Logic and the Source (the section “Ontological Principles”);
- **the potential**—“can be, but is not”: a logical construction that exists as a thought-form without actualization.

There is no place in the field for a fourth: “cannot be”—a contradictory construction—does not enter the field at all: a “paradoxical object” was never an object ([[Paradox Catalog|the criterion of well-formedness]]). The modal square is closed: “cannot not be” and “cannot be” are the extreme corners, and between them lie the two contingent layers; what marks the upper layer is the absence of a generating step, and time applies exactly to what is generated. The negation of being is an involution that carries the corners into each other ([[Formalization]]). The constructions of the fourth corner are gathered in a separate registry—[[What Is Not and Cannot Be]]: each entry shows the substitution by which it is built.

**The subjective mental field** is tied to a particular witness: it is what that witness has perceived from the common field; the boundaries of the subjective field are the boundaries of the system-witness. Here the process lives: **the passage of data from the common field into the subjective one**—perception; the path of the data runs through **channels**—the mechanism of perception, which belongs to the field itself. At this seam the ladder data → information → record is at work ([[Epistemology]]): data lie in the objective field; information and the record belong to the subjective one. The record here is the witness’s own record, the third rung of its ladder. The record of the meetings of Logic and the Source is the actualized layer of the objective field: for the witness it is data.

The objective field has no completed corpus—one cannot be built: this is a restriction of the very structure of information, imposed by Logic ([[Epistemology|there is no completed corpus]]); from the same place comes the gap—by construction the subjective field does not catch up with the objective one. And here, too, is the ontological storey of knowability: everything that exists is manifest as data ready to be perceived—therefore everything that exists is knowable ([[Epistemology|Knowability]]); what in knowledge is objective and what is subjective is treated in [[Objectivity and Subjectivity]].

### Thought

**The picking out of information from the common field by an act of attention, and the designation of what is picked out (“*A*”), is a thought.** The same ladder is at work here as in outer perception ([[Epistemology]]), turned inward: the contents of the field before the act of attention are data, the background; the act of attention makes them information—what has been noticed; the designation makes the record. **A thought is the record of an inner recognition** (the function of the first [[Domains|domain]], turned upon the field itself). The picking out works on both storeys: by perception—from the common, objective field; by observation—from the background of one’s own subjective field, where what has been perceived again plays the part of data until the beam picks it out.

Designation is not tied to the word: a name, an image, a diagram—a thought-form of any modality. The naming itself is subjective and arbitrary—while what is distinguished is objective: “blue” for one and *siniy* for another are kept in two subjective fields and point into one common field (the subsection “Transmission between Fields”).

### The Field and the Zones of Knowledge

The subjective field is the field of “I know”: to observe, one must recognize, and so perceive; what is perceived is already in the field. The ring of “I know that I do not know” is a layer of pointers: it is there that the completing question points, and along the pointers of the ring reasoning moves; questions of status and of choice work in the field of “I know” itself—their material is known, and only a status or a decision is lacking. The zone of “I do not know that I do not know” lies **outside the subjective field—within the objective one**: from it only an inflow of the new through the channels is possible—perception and [[Intuition|intuition]]; what flows in becomes, at the moment of perception, already the subjective field. A question cannot point into this zone: there is as yet nothing there to take into work. And this zone is indexed to the witness, not absolute: part of my zone of the unknown is another witness’s “I know.” On the three zones, see [[Question#The Question and Knowledge|“The Question and Knowledge.”]]

By Finite Actuality the field is finite at every moment: the thoughts picked out are finite in number; “the field of all thoughts” is not a completed object but a process of picking out.

### The Ego Zone

**The Ego is a zone of the subjective field: the region of the witness’s records about itself**, the representation “I.” A witness keeps information in its mental field—including information about itself; this region is the Ego. It is not a thing and not an agency—it is a part of the field with records in it.

The zone has **two independent axes**. *Correspondence*: the records are right—they correspond to what is, objectively—or wrong, illusory constructions; it is the same axis of correspondence—true or false—as for every representation ([[Truth and Absolute Truth]]). *Size*: the zone is large—much information—or small. The axes are independent: any size of the zone is compatible with any status of the records—size in itself is neutral; the fault lies in illusoriness. Filling the zone with right records is a labor of its own: there is no outside view of oneself, and one needs another witness or a rise by a tier ([[Objectivity and Subjectivity#The Witness Is Subjective: Objectivity Is an Orientation|the objective view of oneself]]). And the label “a big ego,” hung on another’s zone without meeting it, is a record from the own zone of the one who hangs it, a projection: one may call so even a sage with a large and right zone.

**Distinguishing the three makes the classical errors impossible by construction.** **The witness is a role**: the one who holds the records and reads them. **“I” is a pointer-word** to the position taken: a pronoun, pure deixis—it has no content beyond pointing to the speaker. **The Ego is a zone of records**: the contents of the field. From this, two errors can be seen through: **reification**—activity is ascribed to the zone of records (“the ego wants,” “the ego defends itself”): an Element is passed off as a Role, and yet records do not want—the witness wants; **identification**—the witness takes the contents of its ego zone for itself, and then a threat to a record (“you are not that clever”) is lived as a threat to oneself: what is defended is the record, not oneself.

> [!example]- Case: a pronoun that became a thing
> “Ego” and “I” are etymological doublets: one Indo-European word that entered English twice—by the native road (Old English *ic* → *I*) and as a learned borrowing (Latin *ego*). *Ego* / *I* is a pronoun, a pure pointer: its referent changes with every speaker. It was made a noun by translation: Freud wrote *das Ich*—the ordinary “I” with an article (beside it *das Es*, “the It,” and *das Über-Ich*, “the Over-I”); the English translators of the 1920s Latinized them—Ego / Id / Super-Ego (Joan Riviere’s translation, 1927); James Strachey’s Standard Edition fixed the choice—and everyday words became thing-terms. The English “ego” inherits the Latinization, not the original. The grammar of translation reified a position: a pointer became a thing; the doublet parted by function—the native “I” remained a pointer, and the borrowed “ego” lives as a thing.

**“The death of the ego”** is a construction of the corner “cannot be,” impossible twice over. Death is a predicate of an organism: to die, the ego would have to be a thing—the construction inherits the reification, and a zone of records is not an organism. And the witness of the report is alive: “I lived through the death of the ego”—the “I” in the report points as it always pointed; the report itself refutes the destruction. The exact names of what happens in a peak experience are **disidentification**—the witness stops taking the records for itself (the correction of the second error, lived as an experience rather than drawn as a conclusion)—and **the potentialization of the zone**—attention leaves the ego zone (akin to nirbīja-samādhi; see the section “The Operator and the Will”); the records return: a person after “the death of the ego” remembers their own name. The return is guaranteed: the will to be is never zero ([[Formalization]]). The entry in the registry of the impossible is in [[What Is Not and Cannot Be]].

### Transmission between Fields

A new record of a witness—a thought—exists and thereby enters the objective field; but there is no direct reading of another’s records: a system has one inner witness (the section “The Witness”). Transmission goes through the common field: a thought is actualized by a sign—speech, writing, gesture—and the sign itself becomes data of the objective field, open to the perception of another.

An example is colour. A wave of 450–495 nanometres is an objective set of data for any observer. One calls the difference “blue,” another *siniy*: two names, kept in two subjective fields. In communication it is the subjective name of what is distinguished that is transmitted, and the receiver restores the pointing through the relations of their own field: “he said ‘blue’; his ‘blue’ is my *siniy*: the names differ, and they point to one place; so he means *A*—the colour of the 450–495 range—objectively.”

The same example shows more. The equation “‘blue’ is *siniy*” is approximate: names differ in scope. Which differences to fix with a name is decided by a language, and that belongs to the subjective field. Russian has two words for this range—*siniy* and *goluboy*, darker and lighter; English *blue* takes in both. On hearing *blue*, a witness who speaks Russian does not yet know which of their two words will fit: the sign points more broadly than their own record distinguishes, and what is missing they take from the thing itself or by asking again. Other languages divide the same stretch of the spectrum in their own ways: Italian distinguishes *blu* and *azzurro*; Vietnamese *xanh* names both blue and green and is specified by an addition—*xanh dương*, “like the ocean,” *xanh lá cây*, “like a leaf.” The green traffic signal is named *ao*, “blue,” in the Japanese traffic regulations: the old word took in both colours.[^5]

The data, however, are the same for all: wavelength and brightness do not depend on how many words a language divides the range into. The difference “darker—lighter” is objective too: it is seen also by one who has no separate word for it. What differs is the record—which of the differences have received a name. **Communication is triangulation through the common field**: subjective records converge on an objective difference; what is understood is not the sign but what it points to. The more determinate what is pointed to, the smaller the loss: the fully determinate is transmitted without loss ([[Formalization]], “determinacy decides the loss in transmission”).

## Distinction Realism

The position as a whole is **Distinction Realism**: distinctions are real, they are structural features of existence itself, and every act of distinction is an act of witnessing. It differs in substance from the neighbouring positions:

- from **physicalism**—witnessing is co-primary with content: consciousness has no need to arise from non-conscious matter; its relation to phenomenal experience remains open (the section “The Witness”);
- from **the classical dualisms**—here the two principles are nested and meet, and do not stand side by side: a dualism of principles within one whole, not of two self-standing things (the section “Two Principles: The Duality of the Ground”);
- from **panpsychism**—consciousness belongs only to energies with a unique quality; stones and other composites have no witness of their own—they are witnessed through these energies.

The contrast with Spencer-Brown’s *Laws of Form*[^6] is telling: there the starting point is a void—an empty page; here the Source is the fullness of potential, distinctions are indestructible (Order), and time is born from observation, not from the paradox of re-entry.

## Summary

1. **Existence is a status by Sufficient Reason, not a system.** Logic and the Source are two necessary systems, “cannot not be,” with no generating step; the Source is within Logic—the filling within a structure. **The Absolute is the meta-system**: one whole, in which Logic is the rules and the Source is the filling.
2. **The duality of the ground is called by name**: two necessary principles of one whole, each conceived through itself, neither turning into the other. The difference from the classical dualisms is in structure, not in the count: systems, not substances; nesting; every thing is filling in form; the meeting is constitutive and witnessed; the third is Light; no tilt of value. By Spinoza, a meeting requires something in common—one whole; the nearest relative is Sāṃkhya, with the distribution of activity reversed.
3. **Light is manifestness as such**: the act of distinction taken as a system with its own witness. The system is dual in its ground and trinitarian in manifestation; **witnesses = differences + 1**—Logic is the condition of experience, not its content, and it is disclosed only by reasoning.
4. **The witness is derived within the system** in five steps: every act of distinction is an act of witnessing; consciousness is the active face of Identity. It is acts that witness, not composites; witnesses form a genealogy parallel to the hierarchy of systems.
5. **The witness is a role**, not a thing; the constitutive core is three necessities: to confirm and hold a difference, at least one channel of meeting, selective attention. A system has one inner witness; the witness is indestructible—Identity and Order. The limit of the derivation: structural witnessing is established; the phenomenal “for-whom” is open.
6. **The observer is a system under the role of the witness**: who—the witness; what does the perceiving—the Intellect; how—the channels; in what—the mental field; seven aspects and the generative order: will → attention → perception by the Intellect → act; consciousness is the measure of the course.
7. **Time is produced by observation**: the first act is the first moment; the arrow of time is the growth in the number of distinctions; a witness has only the “now”—the current step; simultaneity is relative.
8. **Three manifestations of Logic in the observer**: Consciousness is the potency, the Intellect is the act, the Mental is the medium; exactly three, and the will is a separate axis. Consciousness is gradual—a property of the witness, not a substance.
9. **The Intellect is the acting capacity of the witness**: that which perceives data and understands what is perceived; Logic acting in the observer. Below the triad are the modes: reflection, wit, wisdom; in the field—observation, deliberation, reasoning.
10. **The will is the potential between the known and the knowable**, inexhaustible; it has two roles—the status role (the scale {1, 2}: the unmanifest · the manifest; nirbīja-samādhi is a change of status with a guaranteed return) and the vector role (attention). Status and position are independent coordinates; position changes only along one’s own line of nesting.
11. **The operator is the one who chooses**; **free will is two choices**: of direction (what to know) and of response (how to answer an experience); it is inalienable—the scale does not go below one, and the selectivity of attention is a feature of the role.
12. **The mental field is twofold**: the objective one—the medium of Logic with three modal layers, the actualized among them being the record of the meetings of Logic and the Source (the fourth corner is the registry of the impossible)—and the subjective one—the field of a witness; perception is the passage through the channels; a thought is a picking out and a designation; three zones of knowledge; communication is triangulation through the common field.
13. **The Ego is the zone of records about oneself**, with two independent axes (correspondence and size); the distinction “the witness is a role / ‘I’ is a pointer / the ego is a zone” makes reification and identification impossible by construction; “the death of the ego” is impossible—what is real is disidentification and the potentialization of the zone, with a return.
14. **Distinction Realism** is a position among the schools: against physicalism and panpsychism, and apart from the classical dualisms; the contrast with Spencer-Brown is the fullness of the Source, the indestructibility of distinctions, and time from observation.
