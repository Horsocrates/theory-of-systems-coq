## Where the Chapter Stands

The boundary is drawn, the verdict delivered, the map charted. What remains is that without which the synthesis would be not a summary but self-praise—an *honest accounting*: what of all that has been said in the volume is **proved** by machine, what is an **observation** on top of the proved, and what is an open **horizon**, not yet reached.

*The flagship: three registers of honesty. **Proved**—the machine core of the volume ([[Formalization]]): not a single open gap, on two supporting axioms. **Observation**—the syntheses (the boundary, the atlas, the seed, “everything is a process”): backed by instances, but their universality is a matter of interpretation, not a theorem. **Horizon**—the honestly named open edges: the analytic content of zeta, full transfer, Navier–Stokes regularity.*

This chapter hides nothing. The strength of the volume lies precisely in keeping the three registers apart: not passing off an observation as a theorem, and not keeping silent about the horizon.

## Three Registers of Honesty

Every key statement of our final registry stands in exactly one of the three registers, and they must not be mixed.

**Proved** (by machine). The statement is checked by the compiler: the goal is closed, and the axioms used are visible on checking. This is the solid floor—what one can rely on without reservations.

**Observation** (synthesis). A form recurring across many machine-checked instances but not derived by one metatheorem. Each instance is solid; the *universality* of the form is a matter of interpretation. The honest word here is “observation over cases,” not “theorem.”

**Horizon** (open). Content that the volume *names* but does not reach by machine: either it requires means beyond the process core, or it has not yet been built. The horizon is not a defect but an honestly marked edge of the map.

## What Is Proved: The Machine Core

The solid floor of the volume is its machine core ([[Formalization]]): *not a single* open gap (0 `Admitted`), and all of it on a surprisingly narrow axiom picture[^1].

**Where the classical layer appears.** The Law of Excluded Middle (L3) is used above all in talk of the completed side—of the continuum, of uncountability, of a perfect subset. The witness parameter (connected with P4) turns proved existence into a presentable object. The Element side—that of terminating processes—does not require it: it is *zero-axiom*, and this is checkable for each of its summary theorems.

**One honest conditionality.** There is a single place where the volume rests on a domain estimate: in the Navier–Stokes problem regularity stands *conditionally*—on the bound on the triadic interaction, which is honestly named load-bearing[^2]. Elsewhere additional dependencies are either absent or explicitly brought out and checkable.

## What Is an Observation: The Syntheses of the Volume

Above the solid floor stand the syntheses—and their status must be named exactly. Each is backed by machine instances, but the *universality* of each is an observation, not a derived theorem.

- **The finitization boundary $=$ the boundary of constructivity** (H1). The Element side is zero-axiom, the continuum side requires L3—checked on concrete arenas (rotations, spectrum, roots)[^3]; the unity is an observation.
- **The reduction atlas** (H78): dozens of domain facts are read off one $2\times2$ matrix[^4].
- **One seed** (the P1 shadow): the far side of the boundary is generated by the anti-fixedness of $\mathrm{negb}$[^5].
- **Everything is a process** (Chapter 5): every object of the volume is relocated onto a process—an organizing ontology, checked in instances, one by observation.

*These four are not theorems about the world but **observations** on top of the proved: forms that are solid by machine in each case and one by interpretation. To call them theorems would be an overstatement; to call them empty would be unjust to the checked instances.*

## What Is a Horizon: The Open Edges

And finally, the honestly named horizons—content that the volume *names* but does not reach by machine.

**The analytic zeta.** The Riemann hypothesis is read *structurally*—as the symmetry of the reflection of the critical strip; the *analytic* content of the zeros of zeta—the proof of statements about the location of the nontrivial zeros—remains beyond the horizon and is honestly marked as unbuilt.

**Full transfer and Loeb measure.** Nonstandard analysis gave an Element core (germs, the shadow, the derivative, the integral) and an honestly marked role-limit packaging (the ultrafilter); the *full* Łoś transfer and Loeb measure in general form are the horizon.

**Navier–Stokes regularity.** It stands conditionally—on the load-bearing estimate (§ “What Is Proved: The Machine Core”); removing the condition is an open edge, not a proved result.

**Choice-requiring functional analysis.** Hahn–Banach, Tychonoff, and Baire have real Element cores (separable, countable, sequential cases), but in general form they are not built here.

*The horizon is neither a wall nor a failure. It is the edge of the constructed, named honestly: mathematically, as the uncompleted, not as the hushed-up.*

## E/R/R Analysis and the Final Word

The accounting itself is a *system*; let us analyze it briefly and close the volume.

**Rules (L5).** The holding rule is the sorting by reliability: proved / observation / horizon. It stands above the statements themselves and does not let one register pass for another.

**Roles (L4).** Three roles of every statement: the solid floor, the synthesis-observation, the open edge.
**Elements (L1 + P4).** The bearers are the summary theorems and theorems of the volume themselves, checkable by the compiler; under P4 only the checked is actual, and the horizon is precisely what has not yet been actualized.

**Well-formedness check.** Each statement is in one register; there is no mixing, and the self-assessment of the accounting is honest: this chapter itself stands in the register “observation and horizon,” not passing itself off as a theorem.

And—the final word. The volume has gone from number-as-process to the concluding boundary and gathered all of mathematics under one principle: P4—there is no completed infinity; only finite stages are actual. Under it a number became a position-role, the continuum a Cauchy process, the manifold charts, the structural layer of the zeros of zeta a fixed point of a reflection, computation finitization, a function observation, the infinitesimal a germ. Not a multitude of ready things but one ontology of process, refracted by domains; one boundary separating the constructed from the named; one root of the far side. The volume does not claim to have solved more than it has solved: the solid floor is machine-checked, the syntheses are named observations, the horizons horizons. But in this honesty lies its thesis: *mathematics is a process, not a warehouse of completed objects; and what can be constructed is separable from what can only be named*.
