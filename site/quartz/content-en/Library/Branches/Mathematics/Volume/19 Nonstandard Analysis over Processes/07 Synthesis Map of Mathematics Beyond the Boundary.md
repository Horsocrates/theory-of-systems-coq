## Where the Chapter Stands

Chapters 1–6 went through the mathematics “beyond the boundary” piece by piece: the infinitesimal as a process, the calculus as one shadow operation, the ring-not-a-field, the ultrafilter as a role-limit engine, the boundary as invertibility with the single seed $\mathrm{negb}$, the registry of the illusory. It remains to bring everything into *one map* and to state the thesis of the part as a whole.

*The flagship of the part: the **calculational content** of this block is processual: the Element core plus *removable* role-limit scaffolding (by conservativity); the boundary runs at exactly one point—**the maximization of the Fréchet filter to omniscience**; and the ZFC phantoms (in the ontology of ToS) stand *to the side* of Element mathematics, not *above* it. The machine heart of the map is the single root: one undecided distinction $\mathrm{undecided} S$ generates all three algebraic forms of the role-limit gap.*

And an honest frame at once (it is also the content of § “What We Do Not Claim: The Horizon”): we do **not** claim that ToS “does all of nonstandard analysis.” We claim exactly three things—the Element core is formalized, the role-limit is honestly marked (and conservativity is cited), the illusory is named.

## The Single Root: One Distinction—Three Forms

The whole role-limit side of the part grows from one place. The *undecided distinction* $\mathrm{undecided} S$ is given by a subset $S\subseteqℕ$ for which both $S$ and its complement hold *infinitely often*: the Fréchet filter (the cofinite one) does not decide “which of them is large.” This is a **positive** property (both occur arbitrarily far out—but not “cofinitely”), not a double negation.

This one property gives, by machine, **three** forms of one gap[^1]:

- **($\times$) zero divisor:** $\mathrm{ind} S\cdot\mathrm{ind} \neg S\sim 0$ (pointwise zero), but neither factor is $\sim 0$—both take the value $1$ infinitely often;
- **($+$) oscillator without a shadow:** $\mathrm{osc} S$ (values $\pm1$) has no constant shadow—the limit does not exist;
- **(ring) non-invertibility:** $\mathrm{ind} S$ has no inverse—it would vanish on the cofinal complement.

The canonical $\mathrm{undecided}$ set is the evens, $\mathrm{evens}$. This is the structural reason why the germ ring of processes is *a ring, not a field* (Chapter 3): the gap is not an algebraic defect but *one* root in three garbs.

*Honestly about the status. Constructivity here is no accident: all three forms are derived **without** the axiom L3 (excluded middle) precisely because $\mathrm{undecided}$ is given positively (both arbitrarily far out: $S$ and $\neg S$ are cofinal, not cofinite), and not as $\neg(\text{cofinite})$—the latter would require the elimination of a double negation. The genuine content is the **unification** (one root $\to$ three forms) by machine, not a new theorem of nonstandard analysis.*

The same root is also visible algebro-logically (Chapter 5): the anti-fixed point $\mathrm{negb} b\neq b$—Lawvere’s seed—generates the zero divisor, the prime resolution of the ultrafilter measure, uncountability ($ℕ\to 2^ℕ$), and Russell; and on the ToS core this is the shadow of P1 (no self-membership). One seed—many refractions.

## The Map by Layers

Now the whole part is in one table. The rows are the layers of nonstandard analysis; the columns are the three statuses of the verdict.

| **Layer** | **Element** (process, $0$ ax) | **role-limit** (formwork) | **Illusory** (phantom) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\infty$-small | the germ ring $ℚ^{ℕ}/$ Fréchet, $\delta=1/(n+1)$, the derivative | the field $^{*}ℝ$ (ultrafilter) | — |
| transfer | Fréchet: the cofinite filter / eventual equality | the full Łoś transfer | — |
| measure, integral | hyperfinite $=$ Riemann; Loeb as the limit of finite ones | standardization | the non-measurable (Vitali) |
| filters | cofinite (Fréchet) | ultrafilter-ROLE ($\mathrm{LPO}_\omega$) | ultrafilter-object without a witness; $\betaℕ\setminusℕ$ |
| pathology | — | — | Hamel, Banach–Tarski, a well-ordering of $ℝ$ |

The map reads as one sentence: the left and middle columns are one and the same process mathematics (Element plus *removable* formwork by conservativity), the right one holds artifacts of the chosen set theory.

And the boundary between the left and the middle runs at *one* point—the maximization of the cofinite filter to a nonprincipal ultrafilter, that is, the omniscience $\mathrm{LPO}_\omega$ (a coherent decision of “is $S$ large” for *all* subsets at once). In the foundation of the project this place is marked by the ladder of principles of omniscience $\mathrm{LLPO}\sqsubset\mathrm{WLPO}\sqsubset\mathrm{LPO}$[^2], and in the registry of set-theoretic assumptions the power set and choice are assigned to the role-limit side[^3]. This is the same finitization boundary as in thesis H1 of the whole volume: *the finitization boundary $=$ the boundary of constructivity*.

## What We Do Not Claim: The Horizon

The map would be dishonest without an explicit boundary of scope. ToS does **not** reformalize all of nonstandard analysis.

**Claimed (and backed by machine).**
(a) The Element core is built: the germ ring over processes, $\delta$, the shadow $\mathrm{st}$, the derivative and the integral—0 axioms (Chapters 1–3).
(b) The role-limit is honestly marked (ultrafilter $=$ omniscience, Chapter 4), and *conservativity*—what makes the scaffolding removable—is *cited* (Nelson, in the corresponding metatheoretical sense and for standard statements of the language under consideration: a standard consequence obtained through NSA scaffolding has a standard proof), not reproved.
(c) The illusory is named, with one anchor theorem (Banach–Tarski, Chapter 6).

**The horizon (mathematically, not as unfinished work).** The full Łoś transfer, Loeb measure in general form, saturation, choice-requiring functional analysis (Hahn–Banach, Tychonoff, Baire)—remain outside this part. This is an honest horizon: their Element cores (separable, countable, sequential cases) are real and processual, but are not built here.

*And about the unification—honestly. The single root (§ “The Single Root: One Distinction—Three Forms”), the boundary as invertibility, and $\mathrm{negb}=$ the shadow of P1 (Chapter 5) are a **synthesis**: the components are classical (Lawvere, Cantor, Russell, Stone’s two-valued measure, $\det\pm1$). What is genuine is their reduction to one root, by machine, not new theorems.*

## E/R/R Analysis: The Part as One System

Let us analyze *the whole part* by E/R/R in the generating order Rules $\to$ Roles $\to$ Elements.

**Rules (L5).** The holding rule of the part is the three-valued verdict {Element / role-limit / illusory}, bound together by the single root: $\mathrm{undecided} S$ generates the zero divisor, the oscillator, and non-invertibility, and its *resolution* is exactly the ultrafilter (a role-limit). The recurring rule of the boundary is the invertibility of the germ ring.

**Roles (L4).** Infinitesimal / finite / infinitely large are the roles of the dynamic boundary; the shadow is a role-limit; divisor / oscillator / unit are three roles of one gap; the verdict-status is the role of every construction “beyond the boundary”; needs-AC, the witness, and removability are role-diagnostics.

**Elements (L1 + P4).** The bearers are processes $ℕ\toℚ$, actual at every finite stage. $\delta=1/(n+1)$ *is* (a process, actualizable); a concrete nonprincipal ultrafilter as an object *is not*—exactly what P4 does not actualize.

**Well-formedness check.** The rule is the verdict and the single root; the roles are the statuses, the forms of the gap, the diagnostics; the elements are processes $ℕ\toℚ$ and the cofinite Fréchet rules (the ultrafilter measure is a role-limit, not an Element); there are no overlaps. The whole part as a system: the constructions “beyond the boundary” (Elements) receive a status (Roles) by the rule-verdict (Rules), and the only role-limit point is the resolution of the undecided distinction.

**What the analysis of the whole part has yielded.** It has separated the real (the Element core, expressible by processes) from removable scaffolding (role-limit, conservativity) and from phantoms (the illusory, honestly named)—and has reduced the role-limit side to *one* root. Nonstandard analysis has turned out to be not a separate universe of “ideal” numbers but process mathematics in role-limit garb.

With this the part on nonstandard analysis is complete. Its thesis in one line: the calculational content of this block is processual, the boundary runs along the maximization of the filter to omniscience, and the ZFC phantoms (in the ontology of ToS, where “illusory” is a technical term—no Element referent, not a denial of existence in ZFC) stand to the side of Element mathematics—they are formal objects of the chosen set theory, but not senior to the process content. Bringing the *whole* process program into a single summary is a separate task.
