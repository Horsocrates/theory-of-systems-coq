Epistemology is the doctrine of knowledge: what it is, how it is arranged and obtained, where its limits lie. Its starting position: everything that exists is knowable. *Why* what exists is knowable and *by whom* it is witnessed is the subject of [[Metaphysics]]; *what* in what exists is known, and *how*, is the subject of Epistemology.

## Etymology

**Epistemology.** Greek ἐπιστήμη (epistēmē), “knowledge,” ← ἐπίσταμαι (epistamai), “I know, I know how”—on the accepted analysis, ἐπί (epi), “at, over,” + ἵσταμαι (histamai), “I stand”: to know is to stand by the thing. The root “to stand” is the same as in σύστημα (systēma; [[Theory of Systems]]). The second part is λόγος (logos), “doctrine.” The word was introduced into philosophy by James Ferrier in 1854;[^1] in the Russian tradition “gnoseology”—from γνῶσις (gnōsis)—stands beside it. The analysis of the word “to know” itself is below, in the section “Recognition: Knowledge.”

## Knowability

**Something exists**—a fact of a special kind: it presents itself, confirmed in every act of thought, perception, doubt. If something exists, it exists as something *determinate*: it is what it is and is not something else—*A* is *A*, distinct from not-*A*. Since *A* is determinately distinct from not-*A*, one can say exactly what is *A* and what is not-*A*; and to say determinately what something is and what it is not is **to know** it, at least in principle. So from the laws of [[Logic|logic]] a conclusion is read off, with which all that follows opens:[^2]

**Everything that exists is knowable.**

This is a direct consequence of the laws of logic. To think “there is something unknowable,” that something has to be distinguished, that is, recognized as determinate—and the determinate is knowable.

**Knowable does not mean known.** Knowability is a characteristic of the logical structure of what exists: its determinacy (*A* = *A*), given together with existence;[^3] being known is a record, already made, of a particular knower. Between the first and the second lies the whole path of knowing, and all the analysis that follows is a movement from the first to the second. Exactly one thing is impossible—**“something unknowable in principle”**: it is a contradiction in the very definition, non-existence passed off as a mystery ([[What Is Not and Cannot Be]]). There can be nothing unknowable; there is only the not-yet-known.

From here, too, comes **the method of the whole theory**. It does not postulate its positions or the boundaries of knowledge; it **reads them off**: knowledge is taken as a [[Theory of Systems|system]]—rules, roles, elements—together with the conditions of its obtaining: the step is finite, the field grows, a witness is needed, a meeting is needed. Where the logic of the building and the conditions of obtaining speak, there is a conclusion; where they are silent, the theory stops and hands the question on, to Metaphysics or to a particular science.

And one last reading from the structure—with it the arrangement of knowledge opens. What does it mean at all—*to know* something? At the root, **to settle a distinction**: to distinguish what is from what it is not, and to fix that side. Distinction is not one feature of knowledge among others but its root: the build of knowledge inherits the build of what is distinguished. As the thing is, so in form is the knowledge of it.

## Recognition: Knowledge

The English *know* and the Russian *znat’* are one word: Proto-Slavic \*znati, Old Indic *jānāti*, “knows,” Greek γιγνώσκω (gignōskō), Latin *(g)nōscere*, English *know*—all of them parted across the languages from one Indo-European root. That root meant **to recognize, to know again**.

This matters. To recognize is to tell apart from what is other: at the bottom of the word “knowledge” lies distinction—deeper than “possessing information” or “keeping facts in memory.” To know something, in the original sense, is to be able to pick it out among other things, to recognize it. In English the root sounds in *recognize*, *acknowledge*, *cognition*, *connoisseur*.

And this coincides with what knowability led to. To exist is to be distinguishable; distinguishability is knowability. Now the word itself says the same: to know is to distinguish. The root of knowledge and the root of being meet at one point—in difference. On this the whole analysis stands: knowledge itself is, at its base, distinction.

How does knowledge grow out of the act of distinction? To draw a difference is to separate *this* from *that*, to pick something out against the background of what is other. When a difference has been drawn and fixed, a trace remains of it—a fixed “this, not that.” Such is the actualized layer of the objective field—the record of the meetings of Logic and the Source ([[Metaphysics#The Mental Field|Metaphysics]]); for a witness this trace is **data**: a unit of difference that carries only the difference itself and nothing beyond. A minimal sense is already there in data—the “this, not that” itself; but what this difference turns into and what it means, the data themselves do not say.

Meaning comes with the witness. Taking up a difference, the witness relates it to something and makes sense of what it is for the witness—and a difference that has become significant for a witness is **information**: **a unit of sense**. The boundary between data and information is the watershed of the whole theory, and it is seen in simple cases. One and the same light, one and the same wave, enters the eye of a colour-blind person and the eye of one who distinguishes colours: the data are identical, the wave has not changed—but the colour-blind person will draw from it a more modest set of differences, less information. Or speech: the sounds of which a word is made are heard the same by all—these are data; but each unpacks the sense from them in their own way, according to their own store of what is understood. Data are objective and presented to all; information is always someone’s—what in the data will become sense depends on the witness.

So from the act of distinction a road opens to knowledge: a difference is fixed as data, taken up by a witness and made sense of as information, then retained. Knowledge is distinction that has travelled this road to the end.

## The Ladder and Its Ground

The path on which data become information, and information becomes knowledge, goes by rungs.

- **Data**—differences presented by the knowable, before any reading.

- **Reading**—attention turns to the data: one set per step and to the accessible depth; reading is finite and selective. On depth, see below; the channel of reading is [[1 Recognition|perception]].

- **Information**—a difference read and made sense of: data that have become sense for a witness.

- **Record**—information retained: the record of a witness. A true record is knowledge ([[Truth and Absolute Truth|the stages of representation]]).

Every transition stands on [[Sufficient Reason]]: what is presented gives something to read, what is read gives something to become sense, what is made sense of gives something to retain. And the order of the rungs is one: data before information, information before the record. This is the horizontal of the [[Order|Law of Order]], at work within knowing itself.

Hence **the rule of the building** of knowledge: a record arises only through witnessing, unfolded as the ladder. This rule is primary—the ladder is the very order that binds the rest—and by it the roles and elements of the system are read: **the roles**—that which presents (the knowable), that which reads and retains (the knower); **the elements**—data, information, the record. From the one rule of building the whole composition is read off.

Knowledge itself exists too—and since it exists, it is itself knowable: a record about records is possible, and this page is the sign of such a record.

## Field, Slice, Boundary

Everything knowable is bound into one: an object stands among objects, tier rests on tier, and their relations are themselves knowable. Taken with all these bonds, the knowable is a single **field**: a hierarchy of systems, their elements and relations, open to knowledge in principle—the objective mental field ([[Metaphysics#The Mental Field|Metaphysics]]). What belongs to the knower at each moment is a **slice**—the knower’s subjective field: the part that has already passed the ladder up to the record.

The slice has a **boundary**; relative to the slice the field divides into three zones:

- **I know**—the record is made: this is the slice itself;

- **I know that I do not know**—the gap itself is recorded: the object is distinguished, a question is possible, but the content is not there;

- **I do not know that I do not know**—neither a record nor a record of the gap; from inside the slice this zone is not seen at all.

The boundary sets a limit to the very method of knowing. One can come to know only that to which there is a bond from what is already recorded: a new record is built into the earlier ones, understanding is built on what is understood. Hence directed movement goes through the middle zone—“I know that I do not know,” the only one where not-knowing is already distinguished and the bond is already there; the third zone opens otherwise—by the growth of the slice: a new record brings new gaps—and by inflow through the channels: perception and insight ([[Intuition]]). How exactly this movement goes is the page [[Question]].

## Types of Knowledge: Knowledge-About, Knowledge-How

What records are possible? In content, knowledge of an object is one—**knowledge-about**: the whole corpus of information about the object, its facts, properties, states, everything that can be learned of it. The ground of knowledge-about is the completed distinction of a fact: the fact is complete, and so knowledge-about is able to be full by its very build. The content is one; only the **sources** from which it is taken differ.

At first hand, knowledge-about is given by **presence**—the direct contact of a witness with the object; and in the contact the witness stands in two ways. **From outside**—facing the object, observing it from the side: knowledge-about is read from this meeting by the channels of observation already at hand, and no retelling can replace the meeting itself. **From inside**—this is **knowledge-how**: the witness finds itself inside the object and goes through its process itself, by its own course; here knowledge-about is given by taking part, by the course of the process itself. The ground of presence is the co-presence of the knower and the knowable, and one can go through only what is there for one. Both positions are one presence, and both give one knowledge-about; they differ only in place, outside or inside.

And the same knowledge-about can be taken at second hand as well—by **transmission**, ready-made from another witness. By this path what arrives is only a **distillate** of the meeting and of the going-through—they themselves stay with the one who went through; on it, see the section “Knowledge and Understanding” below. This is why, wherever the going-through itself matters, humanity builds apprenticeship rather than libraries.

So knowledge-about is one content, and it stands at the centre; it is obtained in two ways: by **presence**—direct contact at first hand, from outside or from inside—or by **transmission**, ready-made from another, at second hand.[^4]

Knowledge-how is arranged more peculiarly, and in it one sees best why it is a process. The witness here is inside the object: it goes through its process itself, stage by stage, by its own course. Chess can be taken from outside, by observing: watching the game, reading knowledge-about—what it is, with all its properties. Or it can be taken from inside, by going through: *being* a chess player, going the player’s way—and then the knowledge-about opens that is accessible only from inside. Knowledge accumulates here by the going-through itself: at every stage the witness is the one who goes through it.

Hence the going-through rests on the whole course. Knowledge-about taken from the side grasps a value: what opened at this stage, at this place in the process. The going-through itself is the whole course—all the stages in their order. A value is not yet the course: one can know about each stage separately and still remain outside—positions taken from without do not add up to the movement. Hence a corpus of knowledge-about, taken from without or transmitted, does not replace the going-through: the inner course is gone through only from inside.

Why, then, is knowledge-how necessarily a process? Because such is its Sufficient Reason—that by which it holds. **The ground of knowledge-how is the going-through.** And the going-through is by its very nature extended: the stages go in their own order and their own time, one after another, and the ground forms as they are gone through. Knowledge-how is there exactly to the extent that it has been gone through; it cannot be taken ready-made, in a single moment.

More than that—in an unbounded process knowledge-how does not reach its limit at all, and this is seen from the count. While the process goes on, behind every stage gone through stands the next: for every step *n* there is a step *n+1*. And since after every step there is a next, **there is no step at which everything has been gone through**. **The fullness of knowledge-how is not an attainable quantity but a receding limit**: it embraces the whole process only in its course. In the [[Theory of Systems#The Boundary of the Theory: The Role-Limit|Theory of Systems]] such a limit is called a role-limit—a role that no step occupies. And here for the first time a trait shows through on which all that follows rests: **there is no actual infinity**—this is the principle of Finite Actuality: every system is finite at every moment. There is the infinity of process—a process with no last step, open to continuation; a completed infinity, gathered at once into a ready “everything,” there is not. On this distinction process mathematics also stands—a branch of the Theory of Systems ([[Mathematics]]).

From this comes a strict constraint on how this knowledge is taken. Since its only ground is the going-through, the path to knowledge-how is one: to go through. The one who watches meets the process and reads about it; the one who goes becomes it and goes through. Both are at first hand, both are processes; they are set apart by the position of the witness—outside the knowable or inside it. To know-how is to have gone through, and there is no other way.

## Depth

**Depth is a hierarchy of nested systems.** It is the triple of the arrangement of any system—Rules · Roles · Elements—continued along the vertical: downward, every element itself unfolds into a system—with its own rules, roles, and elements; upward, the system itself enters as an element into a larger one ([[Theory of Systems#Levels and Hierarchy|levels]]). The tiers of depth are the levels of this hierarchy. A clock—the hands; beneath them the mechanism; beneath it the metal; beneath it the atoms—tier after tier of distinctions. The vertical goes upward too: above every system stand those that embrace it—cell, organ, organism, biosphere. The word “depth” covers both sides, and the threshold of the witness fails in both: one may fail to see the gears behind the hands—and one may fail to see the forest for the trees; the habit of calling “deep” only the way down is a skew. The ways of knowing are related to this vertical: [[Intuition|insight]] reads upward—to the embracing whole; discursive thought—the step-by-step passage from record to record—goes along a tier; the going-through goes in time.

The vertical marks every record: knowledge-about is full by its build—but on the tier that was read; a deeper tier opens new facts in the same object. Presence is tiered too, and so is every “how”: the hands move, the gears mesh, the spring unwinds—three processes on three tiers.

How many tiers a witness will take is limited three times over:

- **The accessible depth of the object**—how many tiers it presents at all: at each moment a finite number;

- **The depth of the channel**—the meeting cuts its own layer out of what is accessible: to the eye a clock presents the hands, to a microscope the grain of the metal, to an X-ray the mechanism;

- **The threshold of the witness**—capacity: what is above the threshold remains data and does not become sense.

The rule that binds the three: **the effective depth of knowledge is the minimum of the three**—one cannot know deeper than the object has presented, than the channel has let through, and than the threshold has taken in; it is useless to raise one limiter where the minimum is held by another.

Each of the three grows in its own way: **the object deepens by distinction** (a new tier is not generated but actualized—so matter went the way from bodies to atoms and further), **the channel by the instrument** (a microscope does not know about the cell—it presents its layer and holds the imprint for the witness), **the threshold by practice, and only in order**: understanding is built on what is understood, tiers are not leapt over—the ladder of the [[Order|Law of Order]], set vertically.

To the three limiters answer three organized labours of humanity: **science** raises the accessible depth and builds channels, **education** raises thresholds, **specialization** answers to the fact that the field is deepened by the labour of all, while a threshold is raised by the labour of one.

Depth is measured in tiers—along one vertical. The tiers of two different objects are bound only by the common node that embraces both branches; there is no single numerical measure across verticals. The descent has a bottom at each moment—the lowest tier distinguished—and it is movable: distinction lowers it deeper; whether the descent has a final, immovable bottom, the structure leaves open. Beneath every tier, behind every record, there is a gap: the undistinguished, already surrounding the distinguished. The next step into it has to be chosen—this is the pointing act, the [[Question|question]].

## The Gap and Incompletability

Presence is a process: a meeting with an unclosed object does not give fullness, just as the going-through does not. Both are knowledge at first hand, taken in a course; the gap is their common limit.

There is closed knowledge as well. A system by itself, in isolation, is finite: the knowledge-about of it is closed, and such a fact is taken whole—a bounded field is overtaken by the record. Knowledge becomes a process where the system stands among others: an object interacts with objects, and every interaction opens something new to know—new bonds, new composite systems. The field of these bonds grows ahead of the record without bound.

The record, for its part, goes by a finite step, one object at a time: such are the limitations of the knower. Hence the mechanism of the race: every record, closing one gap, opens at least one new one—what is learned about a system pulls in its bonds with others—and the field grows no slower than the slice fills it.

What follows from this is proved by **the theorem of the nondecreasing gap** ([[Formalization]]). It follows two quantities through the steps of knowing: the **field** *F(n)*—how much about a system is accessible to knowledge by step *n*—and the **slice** *S(n)*—how much has been recorded by the same step; their difference is the **gap**. Three conditions: the slice stays within the field (*S(n) ≤ F(n)*); the slice only grows—what is recorded stays recorded; the field grows by at least as much as the slice (*F(n+1) − F(n) ≥ S(n+1) − S(n)*). The conclusion: **at every step the gap is preserved or grows**—once opened, it no longer narrows. The conclusion does not depend on the speed of recording: speed the knower up, and the slice grows faster—and by the same condition the field grows faster.

The gap is the engine of knowing: while it is there, knowledge has somewhere to go; and in a dynamic whole it is always there. And this gap is **twofold**: **in breadth**—the field of the knowable is greater than what is recorded (the potential to learn more of the “what”); **in depth**—beneath an already known “what” lies a “why” not yet understood (the potential to bring knowledge to depth—to understanding). The gap is a **potential** that gives birth to the pull of the will toward its realization: potency → act, and the will is the engine of the transition (the full mechanics of the will is in [[Metaphysics#The Operator and the Will|Metaphysics]]).

The same gap gives the exact measure of all-embracingness. All-embracingness *in the course* there is: for everything that exists a step will be found at which it is taken. A ready-made corpus there is not: a single step at which everything has been taken at once does not exist. Hence “omniscience” in the sense of a finished object of possession is a **category mistake**: the turning of a process into an object. Knowledge embraces everything—in the course; **a completed corpus it cannot be**: a completed corpus is the same role-limit ([[Theory of Systems#The Boundary of the Theory: The Role-Limit|the boundary of the theory]]).

## The Conditions of Knowledge and Its Limits

To know is **to hold absolute truth**: to hold how exactly things are ([[Truth and Absolute Truth]]). It is held in three ways, and these are three different things: **presence**—the position of direct contact with what is; **knowledge-about**—accumulated content (and what is held here is richer than what is transmitted: the distillate is only what can be transmitted, and it is less than what one has); **knowledge-how**—a position toward the very course of a system, the skill of standing rightly toward its movement. Two of the three are positions of the witness: presence—by the object, knowledge-how—in its course; the third is content. Understanding stands to them in depth—it is a descent to the structure, the “why”; the triple of the knower—knowledge, understanding, skill—is gathered below, in the section “Knowledge and Understanding.”

The conditions of knowledge have a reverse side—the limits, and they are ontological. The foundation of grounds is [[Logic]] itself: the chain of grounds does not run into a bad regress; it stands on the laws, and the laws stand by themselves. So the limits of knowledge do not come from a lack of ground. An isolated system can be known to the end—a bounded field is overtaken by the record. The limits come from **dynamics**: interaction, change, the formation of new systems keep the field open—and omniscience, full transmission, full understanding are impossible precisely for a dynamic whole. This is the same gap, seen from the side of the conditions.

## Knowledge and Understanding

The division is sharp: **knowledge is to know that “*A* is *A*”** (to distinguish *A*; to hold that it is); **understanding is the *why* of “*A* is *A*”** (to grasp the structure that gives this identity). The roots themselves hold this division: *to know* is to recognize (hence the Russian *znak*, “sign”—that by which one recognizes); *to understand* is, in Russian, *ponyat’*, from *po-* + Old Russian *yati*, “to take”: to take hold of, to grasp (the same metaphor in *comprehendere*, *begreifen*, *grasp*; the English *understand* speaks of standing, like ἐπίσταμαι). Knowledge holds; understanding grasps. Understanding is more than knowledge: knowledge requires only holding information—remembering; understanding requires holding the structure itself as well: why it is so. It is deep knowledge.

The order is lawful: first to distinguish what is—then to deliberate. Understanding comes after knowledge and is taken not by a separate kind of knowledge but by **reasoning**—consecutive deliberation ([[Metaphysics#Three Operations|three operations]]): by the application of logic, by determining the structure and the bonds beneath the observed elements. Knowledge observes; understanding grasps the structure. To understand *A* is **to study *A* as a system**: to analyse it by the triad Rules · Roles · Elements—by the method of the whole [[Theory of Systems]]; understanding reaches the very ground—why *A* is *A* at all, down to the Law of Sufficient Reason. How exactly reasoning goes—its domains, laws, and failures—is the business of the branch [[Architecture of Reasoning]].

**Making sense is the resolving act between the meeting and the record.** The content of knowledge-about comes from the meeting with the object, from presence: the proposition “water boils at a hundred degrees” is taken from experience. But contact is not yet knowledge: what is met has to be **resolved** into sense, and what settles into the record is what has been resolved. Making sense is this resolving act; knowledge-about is its sediment. The sediment is **thinner** than the meeting: making sense resolves the contact only to the present depth of understanding—to the threshold of the witness (the section “Depth”): what lies within it settles into knowledge-about; what lies deeper is met and stays in the meeting; and the liveliness of the meeting itself, the feeling, stays outside the proposition. Hence knowledge-about is strictly thinner than the meeting; and the **distillate**—that part of the sediment which goes into transmission—is thinner still. The deeper the understanding, the fuller the sediment; the shallower the witness, the poorer.

**Four distillations.** From full contact to what reaches another witness there are four steps, and making sense works at both ends—in the one who gives and in the one who receives: **attention**—the channels carry more than the witness is able to take: already at the entrance a part is taken; **making sense**—the heart of the chain: here what is met is resolved into knowledge-about; **compression for transmission**—what goes to another is the dry residue, not the whole work of making sense; **unpacking**—the receiver makes sense of the distillate anew, to the depth of their own understanding, according to their own store of what is understood: what is received is made sense of anew, not what was sent. Whether the unpacking is whole is decided by the **determinacy** of what is transmitted: what is fully determinate—logic, and mathematics as a system within it—is unpacked by all in the same way; “2+2=4” arrives without loss, for otherwise it cannot be read at all; what is indeterminate is lost the more, the wider the range of possible readings. The distillations eat away precisely the indeterminate; the determinate core passes whole.

**Making sense is irreversible.** The meeting cannot be restored from the sediment, still less from the distillate: that is how the thing is arranged. A difference below the depth of understanding stays in the meeting, and two unlike meetings leave one proposition: the way goes in one direction—from the meeting to the proposition; taste is known by tasting. The going-through is taken by going through: the very application of propositions is a going-through, and the attempt to reduce runs into a regress—Ryle noticed it.[^5] One learns to swim in water. Hence old matters fall into place: presence is irreplaceable, skill is acquired by going through, transmission hands over the distillate.

The summit of the pair is **wisdom: the wholeness of what is understood**. It lies in connectedness, not in “knowing everything”: the “whys” of different things link into a common structure, and knowledge (*what*), understanding (*why*), and skill (*how*) converge into one living whole. Wisdom is the wholeness *of what is understood*: a connected grasp of what has been attained, growing together with the corpus. Wholeness has a degree, completeness a limit: toward it only a path and love are possible ([[Philosophy#Wisdom|the section on wisdom]]).

## What Is Known

There remains the object itself. **Object** ← Latin *objectum*, “thrown before” (*ob-*, “before,” + *iacere*, “to throw”; the Russian *predmet* is its calque)—that which stands before the knower. In it **essence** and **manifestation** are distinguished, and the roots themselves hold the axis: *essence* ← Latin *esse*, “to be” (what there is to the thing), *manifestation* ← Latin *manifestus*, “plainly shown” (how it shows itself). The Russian pair says the same: *sut’*, from “to be”—possibly akin to *istina*, “absolute truth”—and *proyavlenie*, from “to show.”

**Essence is the invariant: that which makes *A* = *A*.** It is always whole: only the manifestation changes—*A* can show itself in different ways and remain itself as long as the essence is the same. There is no “change of essence” as a change of *A*: if the essence is different, this is not the same *A* become other, but simply another *A*. The identity *A* = *A* ([[Identity]]) is guaranteed by the wholeness of the essence.

The triple of questions stands on both sides of knowing:

| Question | In the object *A* | In the knower |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | Essence: the constitution (makes *A* = *A*) | Knowledge (“*A* is *A*”) |
| **How** | The course: the rules in action (how *A* works) | Skill, knowledge-how (a position toward the course) |
| **Why** | Ground: the goal (why *A* is *A*) | Understanding |

Essence is the constitution: the rules of the build and the necessary roles ([[Theory of Systems#Constitution and the Identity of a System|Theory of Systems]]); the “how” is the course, the rules in action; the ground is the goal from which the constitution is composed.

Epistemically, an exact distribution of limits follows: essence is knowable to the end (the invariant can be completed—*A* is recognized through any manifestations), while manifestations are open and variable, especially in interaction—the dynamic limit lies on them, not on essence. And essence together with the unfolding manifestations means: **the object of knowing is itself a process**.

## Summary

1. **Everything that exists is knowable**: knowability is the determinacy of what exists, given together with it; being known is a record of a knower. One thing is impossible—“something unknowable in principle.”
2. **To know is to distinguish.** The ladder leads from data through reading and information to the record; the order of the rungs is from Order, every transition stands on Sufficient Reason; a true record is knowledge.
3. **Field and slice**: the objective field is open to knowledge in principle; what belongs to the knower is a slice—the knower’s subjective field—and along the boundary of the slice the field divides into three zones.
4. **Knowledge-about is one content**: it is taken by presence—from outside or from inside—or by transmission, as a distillate. Knowledge-how is taken only by going through, and in an unbounded process its fullness is a receding limit: there is no actual infinity.
5. **Depth is a hierarchy of nested systems**; the effective depth of knowledge is the minimum of three: object, channel, threshold.
6. **The gap between field and slice is preserved or grows**—and it is not a lack but a potential that pulls the will toward knowing; a completed corpus is a role-limit; the limits of knowledge come from dynamics.
7. **To know is to hold absolute truth**: how exactly things are. Understanding is deeper than knowledge—it is deep knowledge: it holds the structure as well, the “why”; it is taken by reasoning; making sense is the resolving act between the meeting and the record; wisdom is the wholeness of what is understood.
8. **The object holds by its essence and unfolds in manifestations**: essence is the constitution, and it is always whole; the object of knowing is itself a process, known by a process.

The one for whom the knowable becomes knowledge is the witness: its role, constitutive core, and limit are analysed in [[Metaphysics#The Witness|Metaphysics]]. Next: what knowledge answers to—[[Truth and Absolute Truth]].

Existence, knowability, and a witness (at least a parent one) are given together, not in turn—so one cannot say “before any witness.” The derivation of the witness is in [[Metaphysics#The Witness|Metaphysics]].

[^4]: The division has two-membered predecessors. Bertrand Russell distinguished knowledge by acquaintance and knowledge by description: Russell, B. “Knowledge by Acquaintance and Knowledge by Description,” *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 11 (1910–1911): 108–128. Gilbert Ryle distinguished knowing *that* and knowing *how*: Ryle, G. *The Concept of Mind* (London: Hutchinson, 1949), ch. 2. Here the division is not assembled from two dichotomies but read off from the build of knowledge itself: one content—knowledge-about—and its sources, distinguished by the position of the witness.

[^5]: Ryle, G. *The Concept of Mind*, ch. 2: knowing how is not reducible to knowing that—the application of a rule is itself a skill, and the attempt to derive skill from rules runs into a regress.
