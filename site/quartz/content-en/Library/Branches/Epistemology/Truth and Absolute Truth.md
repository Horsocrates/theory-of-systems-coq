Truth is how it is; absolute truth is objective truth: how exactly things are; honesty is the orientation of consciousness toward truth: it excludes the lie, while absolute truth is reached by a path.

## Etymology

Russian has two words where English has one: *pravda* and *istina*. The English *truth* covers both; on this page they are kept apart as **truth** (*pravda*) and **absolute truth** (*istina*). The third word of the theme is *chestnost’*, honesty. The roots of the three Russian words carry the theme.

**Absolute truth: *istina*.** Proto-Slavic \*istina ← \*istъ, “that very one, genuine, real.” Etymologists connect the root with the verb “to be” (PIE \*h₁es-); the connection is debated, the core of the meaning is stable: **exactly that**—the genuine, what is. Cognates: *istyi*, “veritable”; *istovyi*, “earnest”; *voistinu*, “verily.” The word carries the “exactly”: absolute truth is how exactly things are.

**Truth: *pravda*.** Proto-Slavic \*pravьda ← \*pravъ, “straight, right, correct.” The nest: *pravyi*, “right”; *pravilo*, “rule”; *pravit’*, “to govern, to correct”; *pravil’nyi*, “correct”; *spravedlivost’*, “justice”; *opravdat’*, “to justify.” In Old Russian *Pravda* is law, right, court: the *Russkaya Pravda* is a code of laws. The word carries straightness and order: truth is how it is, the right order of things. Rightness, *pravota*, is of the same root and the same thing: truth is rightness; the one who is right stands on truth—sees and says how it is.

**Honesty: *chestnost’*.** *Chest’*, “honor” ← Proto-Slavic \*čьstь, from the verb \*čisti, “to honor, to count, to read”—one word: *chtu*, “I honor”; *chtenie*, “reading”; *schyot*, “count”; *pochyot*, “esteem.” *Chestnyi*, “honest,” is originally “honored, esteemed,” and then “one who keeps honor.” The English word has the same story: *honesty* ← Latin *honestas* ← *honos*, “honor.” The word carries honoring, a holding in esteem: honesty is the orientation that holds truth at the center of attention.

**Greek, Latin, English, and German make do with one word:**

- Greek **ἀλήθεια** (alētheia)—ἀ- (a-, “not”) + the root λαθ- (lath-): λανθάνω (lanthanō), “to be hidden, to escape notice”; λήθη (lēthē), “forgetting”; hence **unhiddenness**, the disclosure of what is;
- Latin **veritas** ← *verus*, “true”—of one root with German *wahr* and Slavic \*věra, “faith”: the root carries **fidelity**, reliability;
- English **truth** ← Old English *trēowþ*, “faithfulness” (of one root with *true*, *trust*);
- German **Wahrheit** ← *wahr* (akin to *verus*): the true, the trustworthy.

The Greek word names the “what is” itself—the disclosed; the Latin–Germanic line (*verus* · *true* · *wahr*) names **fidelity**, a holding to the true; the Russian *vernyi* keeps both senses: a *vernoe* decision is a correct one—correspondence; a *vernyi* friend is a faithful one—holding. Russian splits the “how it is” itself into two words—***pravda*** (how it is) and ***istina*** (how exactly things are)—and keeps the orientation toward it in a separate word, ***chestnost’***. This split gives the theme its ground: two names for what is, and one for the orientation toward it.

## Truth and Absolute Truth

**Truth is how it is.** Everything that exists exists determinately: in some way. This “how” is truth. It is always there—together with what is—and it is no one’s: **truth stands by itself**. To say that someone “has” the truth is wrong: truth has its own support—one joins it. The one who is right stands on it—sees and says how it is.

There is a truth of everything that can be: of the necessary, of the actual, and of the potential—what can be but is not is there exactly as a possibility. The fourth corner—“is not, and cannot be”—has no “how it is” at all; therefore untruth is always a construction: it has nothing to be ([[What Is Not and Cannot Be]]).

**Absolute truth is objective truth: how exactly things are.** The ground is taken from [[Logic]]: to exist is to stand in a logical structure, to exist according to rules. This structure precedes knowing: knowing traces it and does not create it—and so there is something to check against. With the witness, absolute truth is the status of objective correspondence with what is: agreement with the structure of what exists, regardless of who looks and from where. True is what agrees with the structure; false is what diverges from it: both words are statuses of content.

**One “how it is,” from two sides.** In what is, truth is absolute truth: a thread of the dress reflects its own wavelength, and exactly that one ([[1 Recognition|the case of the dress]]). The difference appears together with the witness—in how its representation corresponds to what is: subjectively or objectively.

**With the witness, truth is the status of subjective correspondence.** The “how it is” is given to the witness in a meeting—from its position, through its context—and the meeting is real: the witness really sees so. Its representation corresponds to what is as it is given from here—this is truth, and an honest report conveys it. The correspondence here is taken within what is given to the witness; hence truth is slightly narrower than absolute truth. “How *exactly* things are” is not given by one meeting: it is closed by a path. Absolute truth is truth brought to the “exactly”: to the structure that does not depend on the position of the witness; this correspondence is objective—one for all positions. What distinguishes them is **precision**: truth stands at every step of an honest path, absolute truth at its closure. Between them are the witness, its orientation, and its path; this is the subject of the theme.

**The boundary of the theme.** Logic says it briefly: “truth is logic.” Unfolded: logic is that with which things agree; absolute truth is the status of agreement with it. Here this is taken ready and is not proved. Truth as a value—“to live by the truth”—is the subject of [[Ethics]]; here truth is taken as what is, as the status of a witness’s representation, and as that toward which consciousness is oriented.

## The Theme as a System

An analysis by the [[Theory of Systems]]—from the goal to the rules, the roles, and the elements.

- **The goal** is to learn how it is.
- **The rules** say how a status is assigned to a representation. The status goes by correspondence to what is: correspondence within what is given to the witness is subjective—truth; correspondence that is one for all positions is objective—absolute truth; divergence from what is—false. The [[Sufficient Reason|Law of Sufficient Reason]] works twice: as the ground of the *act*—the desire for absolute truth holds the orientation—and as the ground of the *content*—a chain of grounds from what is; a coincidence without a path is not an attainment. The orientation is binary—[[Excluded Middle]]: the center of attention is occupied by truth or by something else. The path is gradual—by precision. The closure is binary—[[Non-Contradiction]]: in agreement with the structure or not.
- **The roles** are these: the object—what is: the representation is about it and is checked against it; the witness—the one who meets, holds, and conveys: its orientation is honesty, its course is the path; the other—the one to whom it is conveyed and who checks directly. The place of the roles is the common field: a statement returns there.
- **The elements** are states of affairs (what is); meetings (perception, reasoning, measurement) with their circumstances—the context; representations: opinion, knowledge-that (in [[Epistemology#Types of Knowledge: Knowledge-About, Knowledge-How|“Epistemology”]], knowledge-about), understanding; statements (signs returned to the common field).

Truth and absolute truth occupy no roles: with the witness they are statuses that the rules assign to its representation.

Everything further is the unfolding of these rules on the roles: the witness meets, holds the orientation, goes the path—or lays a construction over the “how it is.”

## Witness, Meeting, Context

**The witness does not manipulate in the field of what is—it meets it.** What is real is always there; the witness receives it in a meeting and carries out of it a **representation**—a record in its own [[Metaphysics#The Mental Field|mental field]]: a state of affairs taken from its position. The state of affairs is the object; the representation is the witness’s construction about it. Whether the construction agrees with how exactly things are is decided by the closure—it lies ahead; what matters here is what exactly is given in the meeting.

**The circumstances of the meeting are the context.** Context is the relation of what is considered to its surroundings: light and distance, the instrument and its resolution, the witness’s earlier records and initial assumptions. All of this enters the meeting and therefore enters the given: what is given to the witness is “how it is from here”—the state of affairs together with the circumstances in which it opened. Context is visible—one must pay attention to it; most often it is simply not considered. The dispute about the color of the dress (a case of the domain [[1 Recognition|Recognition]]) is incomplete until the lighting is considered in it, and to consider it one must be aware that there is a context and of what it does to perception. This is the work of the first two domains of reasoning: [[1 Recognition|to recognize]] the presence of a context and what in it has influence; [[2 Clarification|to clarify]] what exactly and how.

**Two kinds of meeting.** One meets structure by reasoning; one meets the filling by experience, through the form in which it is manifested and which corresponds to it—from perception to measurement. Within experience there is a scale by the accounting of circumstances: perception carries the context inside the given without distinguishing it; measurement is an organized meeting: the circumstances are set in advance and subtracted from the result. Reasoning has its own circumstances—premises and initial assumptions ([[Architecture of Reasoning]]). Every meeting has a context—what is considered always has surroundings; the only difference is whether the relation to them has been considered.

**What follows from this.** What is given in a meeting is truth: so it is, from this place. What the witness does with the given—holds it as it is or substitutes for it—is decided by its orientation. How to get from “how it is from here” to “how exactly things are” is decided by the path.

## Honesty

**Honesty is the orientation of consciousness toward truth.** The chain of the act: the intention (the desire for absolute truth) motivates the goal (to learn how it is) and holds truth at the center of attention. This holding is the attunement; the orientation that holds it is honesty. The center of attention is always occupied by something, so every act has an orientation, and it is binary by the Excluded Middle: the center is occupied by truth or by something else—for instance, by personal interest. There is no third.

**What honesty guarantees.** To hold and to convey as it is. An honest conveying is faithful to the given: the witness declares what it holds, and in the status in which it holds it—a perception as a perception, a supposition as a supposition, knowledge as knowledge. Hence the criterion: it is honest when what is declared coincides with what is held. An orientation toward something else pulls them apart: what is declared is something the witness does not hold—or in a status that what is held does not have. This is a lie, and it is addressed either to another or to oneself: a lie to oneself is when the center of attention is occupied by something else already in the meeting.

**What honesty does not guarantee.** Absolute truth. The given carries a context, and an honest witness conveys it together with the given, without distinguishing it; the conveying is faithful to the given—and exactly as far from “how exactly things are” as the given itself is. Honesty excludes the lie—and only the lie. With an honest orientation and an unfinished path, the whole scale of honest error remains—from partial understanding to illusion: there is error along the whole scale, only the measure differs. In an honest illusion there is subjective correspondence and no objective one: a truth about the meeting is taken for an absolute truth about the object. Illusion, moreover, is one in appearance and twofold in mechanism: the honest one is born of an unconsidered context—without intent, and it requires no effort; the deliberate one is laid over the “how it is” and is held by effort; on it, see the section “Untruth and the Lie” below.

| Orientation | Path | What is obtained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Honesty | Reached the structure | **Absolute truth** |
| Honesty | Did not complete | **Honest error**—one scale: partial understanding · imprecision · illusion |
| Toward something else | Any | **The lie** |

**Speech is the result of seeing.** Speech has no path of its own to absolute truth: one says what one sees. Conveying goes through the common field, where there are three vertices—the witness, the other, and the thing pointed at; it is held by **the determinateness of what is pointed at**: the other can check against the field directly. Therefore truth is conveyed, not instilled: the smoothness of a phrase does not replace it—smoothness without correspondence is sophistry, a form counterfeited as truth, behind which there is no seeing.

> [!example]- Case: “lied with the truth”—the orientation decides the status
> The intention is something other than absolute truth: it occupies the center of attention; the status of the act is a lie, even when the content happened to coincide with how it is: such a coincidence has no ground.

## Case: Parallax

If one stretches out an arm and looks at the thumb against a wall—with one eye, then with the other—the thumb jumps along the wall. Each eye gives truth: from this place the thumb is seen there. The question “where on the wall is the thumb really?” has no answer: the position against the background is a relation of thumb, wall, and eye: it is a context. But the relation of the two positions to each other is known—the distance between the eyes—and therefore their divergence is a measurement rather than a dispute: from it vision computes depth. Two givens—both of them truth—with a known connection between them give what neither has: an invariant.

**The sky that did not shift.** If the Earth goes around the Sun, the nearer stars must shift against the farther ones over half a year. Tycho Brahe—the best observer of his age—found no shift. His given is truth: at his precision no shift is visible. He considered the context too: the stars would then have to be unimaginably far—and, at the apparent size of their disks, unimaginably large; this he found absurd and concluded that the Earth is at rest. But the apparent disk of a star is also a given of the meeting: it is made by the eye and the air, and in a telescope by the instrument itself; later optics showed this. One circumstance remained unconsidered—the apparent disk—and it tipped the scale: a given of the meeting was passed off as an absolute truth about the world—a substitution of status without intent. The shift was found by Bessel in 1838 with an organized meeting: a heliometer, more than a year of observations of one star—61 Cygni—a parallax of a third of an arcsecond, and from it the distance: about ten light-years.[^1] Absolute truth was not closed by the Copernicans’ dispute with Tycho: it was closed by the circumstances of the meeting—the baseline of observation and the precision of the instrument—being determined and taken into account.

**The lesson.** From each position a representation corresponds to what is as it is given from there—this is truth. When the relation between the positions is determined and taken into account, the correspondence becomes objective—this is absolute truth: how exactly things are, one for all positions. Context, considered as the relation of what is considered to its surroundings, turns from a hindrance into an instrument: the divergence of two givens under a known relation is a measurement. This is [[Objectivity and Subjectivity|objectivity]]—an orientation toward the invariant from an occupied position; and it is also conveying through the common field—triangulation, where the other checks against what is pointed at directly.

## The Path to Absolute Truth

**The path is the course from the given to the structure.** Observation, reasoning, checking: a path is gone farther or less far—it is gradual, and its measure is precision: how far “how it is from here” has been brought to “how exactly things are.” The closure is binary by Non-Contradiction: a representation either agrees with the structure or does not.

**Absolute truth is closed by determining the logic of what is studied.** The chain: what is truth?—what is; what does “is” mean?—exists; to exist is to stand in a logical structure, according to rules; logic is knowable; therefore, by rightly determining the logic of what is studied, one discovers absolute truth—how exactly things are. A meeting is a way of access to the rules and elements of what is studied: reasoning—to the structure, experience and measurement—to the filling. What closes is the determination of the logic of the object, in which the circumstances of the meeting are taken into account; the meeting itself and the witness’s statement—“I see it so”—give truth.

### The Rungs of Representation

By what the witness can show: **opinion** is held in the place of “what is” without a chain, on the belief that it is so; **knowledge-that** is the link of a phenomenon with information about it (water consists of hydrogen and oxygen); **understanding** is the link of a phenomenon with its structure: why and how, in interaction with other systems. This is the axis of the link; the second axis is the status of correspondence: every representation is true or false—it objectively corresponds to what is or diverges from it. An opinion can be true and can be false. Knowledge cannot be false: “false knowledge” is an illusion of knowledge.

Knowledge has levels: knowledge-that and understanding. True knowledge is understanding; understanding is deep knowledge—the link with the structure, rightly determined.

**The Greek marking.** Aristotle, *Posterior Analytics*: knowledge of the “that” (ὅτι, hoti) and knowledge of the “why” (διότι, dioti); to them answer knowledge-that and understanding.[^2] Plato, *Meno* 97e–98a: true opinions (ἀληθεῖς δόξαι, alētheis doxai) become knowledge (ἐπιστήμη, epistēmē) when they are tied down by reasoning about the cause.[^3] On this page opinion and knowledge are different rungs: opinion is held by the belief that it is so; knowledge-that, by the link of a phenomenon with information about it; understanding holds the structure as well. A true opinion has coincided with what is, and remains an opinion as long as it is held by belief.

**Layering.** Even seeing is layered: the whole is grasped by insight before the details are made conscious ([[Intuition]]); honest speech carries a right whole together with details not yet right. This is the mechanism of partial understanding: right as far as one has got, with error in the remainder.

**One can coincide with absolute truth by chance; one can come to it only by a path.** A coincidence has no ground that would connect the representation with how it is ([[Sufficient Reason]]).

> [!example]- Case: the stopped clock—a coincidence without a link
> A stopped clock showed the right time. The witness is honest; the given—the hands—is truth: they stand so; the representation coincided with how exactly things are. But the path—reading a stopped clock—does not lead to absolute truth: a coincidence without a ground. A stopped clock gives information about itself: there is no link here between the time and the record of it—the record is held by the belief that the clock is running. The status is a true opinion (ἀληθὴς δόξα, alēthēs doxa), not knowledge. “Knew by chance” is an error of construction: knowledge stands on a ground, and a coincidence has none.[^4]

> [!example]- Case: the *Meno*—the tie of causal reasoning is the path
> Plato, *Meno* 97e–98a: true opinions do not stay long and run away, like the statues of Daedalus, until they are tied down by reasoning about the cause—then they become knowledge. The tie of the *Meno* is the path—a chain along the logical structure. For Plato a tied opinion becomes knowledge; here what is tied is already knowledge, not opinion with an addition.

**The word “belief.”** The Russian *vera* is both belief and faith. Here it means a status—a holding without a chain, belief *that*. Fidelity as an orientation—faith *in*, the holding of a goal—is another category: it is honesty.

## The Potential and Projection

**There is nothing to meet.** The potential is what can be but is not: the action—the cause that would carry it to “can be—and is”—has not yet been performed ([[Sufficient Reason]]). There is a truth of it—it is there exactly as a possibility. But the filling is known by a meeting with a form—a manifestation that corresponds to the filling; the potential has no manifestation yet, and so there is no meeting. Hence absolute truth about the potential is two-faced.

**As structure** it is open to reasoning. Logic presents itself and is connected; to follow logic is true. Mathematics is the language for describing logical laws: the laws exist, and they are discovered, not invented ([[Mathematics]]). What can be by structure is known before any experience.

**As filling** it requires a meeting with a form that is not yet there. Without such a meeting, representation builds a **projection**: the witness models the unknown from what it knows—and it has no information about what is not yet there. A projection in itself is legitimate: it is a [[Metaphysics#Thought|thought-form]] of the layer “can be, but is not,” and declared in this status it is honest. The substitution begins with the appropriation of a status: the possible is declared to be what is. By orientation it has two faces—without intent an honest error, with intent a lie.

**Possibility, not guarantee.** Work in a true system gives the possibility of a true result; there is no guarantee in it: within a system constructions are also built that agree with its rules and diverge from the real. Non-contradiction within a system is an admission to absolute truth: what decides is correspondence to what is. A theory that does not stand on the observation of what is works with a projection—however well built it may be.

**The genres of honest work with the potential.** A hypothesis is a supposition of absolute truth, declared as a supposition. Whether a hypothesis about what is is true is decided already now—by how exactly things are; the meeting only opens this to the witness. About what only can yet be, there is no assignment until there is a ground. A model of the boundaries studies what can be—what mathematics and physics allow—not what is; it is a genre of its own. Dishonesty is a substitution of genre: a projection is passed off as knowledge, a study of boundaries as a description of what is. The layers are to be kept apart: how it is, and the representation of how it is.

**Three substitutions of status.** In the course of the theme there are three, and the mechanism is one: a given of a position is passed off as absolute truth—subjective correspondence as objective (parallax); a projection as knowledge: a hypothesis as a fact; a model of the boundaries as a description of what is. Each time a content is assigned a status it does not have—and by orientation this is either an honest error or a lie. The list is continued by the registry [[What Is Not and Cannot Be]].

> [!example]- Case: the smooth sophism—non-contradiction is not yet correspondence
> A smooth phrase without correspondence is a substitution of the criterion: smoothness is taken for correspondence. The same principle on the scale of a theory: a construction that agrees with the rules of a system and diverges from the real. The agreement of a construction with itself is only a part of agreement with logic: an admission to absolute truth. What decides is correspondence to what is, and access to it is given by a meeting.

## Untruth and the Lie

**Untruth is a construction.** It has no “how it is” that it could be; it is built—and built over the “how it is.” Distortion, replacement, deliberate illusion, a projection passed off as what is—all carry one sense: a construction is put in the place of what is. What is itself remains untouched—the objective field is not damaged; what is damaged is a record: another witness’s and the builder’s own.

**Two statuses.** Untruth is a status of content: what is not as it is; it is possible with honesty too—as an error: in an honest illusion there is a truth about the meeting and an untruth about the object. The lie is a status of the act: an orientation toward something else, a will directed at untruth. One and the same content carries different statuses of the act—said by mistake and said with intent. The evaluation of the act—guilt, responsibility, evil—is the subject of [[Ethics]]; here, the mechanics of the content and its price.

**Two paths of one untruth.**

- **Distortion**—to learn how it is, and replace it. It requires going the path of learning; the construction comes out sturdier—it is stitched together with parts of absolute truth. By orientation it is two-faced: with intent—a replacement of what is held; without intent—the record of what was learned shifts by itself: through memory, retelling, the pressure of later records.
- **Replacement**—to put a projection in the place of knowledge, without learning. It requires no path, so it is available to everyone and makes up the majority of cases. By orientation it is two-faced: without intent—an illusion of knowledge, an honest error; with an orientation toward something else—when one does not want to learn—a lie.

> [!example]- Case: Frankfurt, *On Bullshit*—the two paths from the other side
> Harry Frankfurt (an essay of 1986, a separate book in 2005): the liar knows the truth and hides it; the bullshitter is indifferent to the truth—speaks without asking how it is.[^5] Frankfurt’s distinction maps onto the two paths: distortion is the double record of one who knows; replacement is a projection with no intention to learn. For Frankfurt the bullshitter is not a liar. Here the lie is a status of the act, and indifference to absolute truth is an orientation toward something else; therefore the bullshitter lies, distorting nothing.

**The effort of maintenance is of two kinds.** A deliberate construction stands only while it is held.

- Distortion holds a double record—what is and what is declared—and damps every meeting of other witnesses with the field: for every point of contact a new patch; the burden grows; the fall comes at the first uncovered meeting.
- Replacement holds one record—the projection stands in the place of “what is”—and the effort here is different: **keeping the question unasked**, a turning of attention away from the meeting, and the defense of the status “I know” under challenge. The fall comes at the first meeting of one’s own; a meeting can be postponed, so a replacement stands for a long time.

**Truth stands by itself.** It needs no effort: it is held by what is. An honest error falls too—at the first meeting that discloses it—and falls without resistance: an honest witness seeks the meeting. A deliberate construction needs the whole series of moments without exception; for truth one meeting is enough. An error is told from a lie by how the check is met; their content may be the same.

## Summary

1. **Truth is how it is; absolute truth is objective truth: how exactly things are.** In what is, they are one; with the witness they are statuses of the correspondence of its representation to what is: subjective—truth, objective—absolute truth. What distinguishes them is precision.
2. **The witness meets and carries out a representation.** The given carries a context—the circumstances of the meeting, the relation of what is considered to its surroundings; context is visible, and it must be considered: recognized and clarified.
3. **Honesty is the orientation of consciousness toward truth.** It is binary by the Excluded Middle; the criterion: what is declared coincides with what is held; it excludes the lie—and only the lie.
4. **With an honest orientation and an unfinished path—the scale of honest error:** partial understanding · imprecision · illusion; there is error along the whole scale, the measure differs.
5. **The path is gradual by precision, the closure is binary.** Absolute truth is closed by determining the logic of what is studied. The rungs of representation: opinion, knowledge-that, understanding; every representation is true or false, and knowledge cannot be false; true knowledge is understanding; a coincidence without a path is not an attainment.
6. **Absolute truth about the potential is two-faced:** the structure is open to reasoning; the filling is known through a form that is not yet there—without a meeting, a projection. Non-contradiction within a system is an admission to absolute truth. Three substitutions of status—one mechanism.
7. **Untruth is a status of content, a construction without a “how it is”; the lie is a status of the act.** Two paths—distortion and replacement, both two-faced by orientation; effort holds only a deliberate construction; truth stands by itself.
8. **From a position truth is given; absolute truth is taken from the relation of positions, once it is accounted for.** The bridge to [[Ethics]] is by orientation; to [[Objectivity and Subjectivity|objectivity]]—by position.

## Toward Objectivity and Subjectivity

From here comes the polarity that the theme [[Objectivity and Subjectivity]] unfolds:

- **Absolute truth is objective correspondence:** how exactly things are, one for all positions; hence the question of what is objective—structure and data;
- **Truth is subjective correspondence:** how it is from here, within what is given to the witness; hence the question of what is positional—slice, access, confidence; from truth to absolute truth lead honesty and the path;
- **The connection:** objectivity is an orientation toward the invariant from an occupied position; parallax is its mechanics: the divergence of positions under a known relation between them is a measurement.

The theme of objectivity does not redefine absolute truth—it takes it from here and analyzes what in knowledge is objective and what is positional.
