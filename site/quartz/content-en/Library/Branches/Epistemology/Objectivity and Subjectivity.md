The objective is what is, regardless of who looks and from where; the subjective is how it is given to a witness from its position; objectivity is the witness’s orientation toward what does not depend on position.

## Etymology

Both words grew from one Latin verb—*iacere*, “to throw”—and parted by their prefixes.

**Object** ← Latin *obiectum*, “thrown before, set opposite” (*ob-*, “before, against” + *iacere*). That which stands before the gaze, opposite the knower. The objective is what stands by itself, independently of the one who looks.

**Subject** ← Latin *subiectum*, “laid under, underlying” (*sub-*, “under” + *iacere*); a calque of the Greek ὑποκείμενον (hypokeimenon), “that which lies at the base.” In Aristotle the ὑποκείμενον is that of which everything else is said, while it is itself no longer said of anything else: the bearer of properties.[^1]

**The change of meanings.** The scholastics of the fourteenth century, beginning with Duns Scotus, said that a thing known is in the mind “objectively” (*obiective*)—it has the being of an object of thought; the *subiectum* was the bearer—the thing itself, in which the properties are found. The word still stands so in Descartes: the “objective reality of an idea” is the reality of a thing as it is represented in the mind.[^2] The pair changed places in the German philosophy of the eighteenth century; Kant put it at the center, and the present meaning settled after him, toward the middle of the nineteenth century.[^3]

> [!example]- Case: the reversal of subject and object—roles instead of owners
> The words exchanged meanings: the “sub-ject,” what is laid under, was first the thing that bears properties, and then the knower. The dispute over who owns the word is closed by a role. The subject is the role of the witness: it is occupied by the one who knows ([[Metaphysics#The Witness|the witness as a role]]). The object is the role of what is known: that which stands before the witness and which its representation is about. A role is assigned in the act of knowing; what occupies it is there without that act as well. Hence one and the same system can be both witness and object—when another looks at it.

**The accepted sense** is the present one: the objective is on the side of the object—of what stands independently of the knower; the subjective is on the side of the subject—the knower. The subject here is the role of the witness; the ancient meaning, “bearer of properties,” is not put into the word.

The etymology suggests the axis of the theme: “set opposite, standing by itself” is what is, independently of the one who looks; “the knower’s side” is how it is given to the knower ([[Truth and Absolute Truth]]).

## Definition

**The objective is what does not depend on the witness:** one for all positions. Objective in this way are both structure—it is universal—and data—they are particular.

**The subjective is how what exists is given to a witness from its position.** A position is the context of the meeting and the witness’s channel and threshold ([[Epistemology#Depth|depth]]). What is given to the witness in a meeting—the given—already carries its position; the data do not depend on position.

**The objective as a view is a view of the build of the system:** of its rules, roles, and elements. The subjective as a view is a view of how the system is given from here. The witness always looks from its own position; what distinguishes the two views is what they are directed at. The first is objectivity—the orientation toward what does not depend on position.

**The subjective is positional, not “unreliable.”** A position is the place from which one begins: what is given from a position is truth; the honest orientation and the path bring it to absolute truth ([[Truth and Absolute Truth]]).

**The objective is two-faced.** The objective field has two faces: **structure**—logic, open to reasoning—and **data**—the differences in which what exists is manifested: they are there before any reading and are presented to experience. The boundary between a particular fact and a universal law runs inside the objective; to its two faces lead the two kinds of meeting—reasoning and experience ([[Truth and Absolute Truth#Witness, Meeting, Context|two kinds of meeting]]).

## The Theme as a System

An analysis by the [[Theory of Systems]]—from the goal to the rules, the roles, and the elements.

- **The goal** is to learn how exactly things are: what does not depend on the position of the one who looks.
- **The rules** are these: the criterion of the objective—independence from position; the order of the ladder—data, reading, information, record ([[Order]]); [[Sufficient Reason]]—a record is led to objective correspondence by a chain of grounds from what is; the orientation of the witness—honesty and taking its own position into account.
- **The roles** are these: the object—what the representation is about and what it is checked against; the witness—the one who reads and records from its position; the other—a witness from another position, whose given is compared with one’s own. The place of the roles is the common field: the objective field, on whose differences the records of different witnesses converge ([[Metaphysics#Transmission between Fields|transmission between fields]]).
- **The elements** are what is: structure and data; the witness’s reading, information, and record; the positions of witnesses and the relations between positions.

The objective and the subjective occupy no roles: these words name the field in which an element lies and the status of correspondence that the rules assign to a representation.

## The Ladder: Field and Correspondence

The support of the theme is the ladder of knowledge: data, reading, information, record ([[Epistemology#The Ladder and Its Ground|the ladder and its ground]]). The rungs show what lies in which field.

1. **Data lie in the objective field.** The source itself carries them: they are there before any reading. The entrance of the ladder is objective.
2. **Reading and information lie in the witness’s subjective field.** The witness takes the data from its position; information is data that have become sense for it. The middle of the ladder is positional, and there is no way around it.
3. **The record, too, lies in the subjective field.** What is retained lives with the witness: its records make up its slice ([[Epistemology#Field, Slice, Boundary|field, slice, boundary]]). The content of a record may correspond to what is—subjectively or objectively—or diverge from it. What leads to objective correspondence is a chain of grounds from what is ([[Sufficient Reason]]). A record without a chain remains an opinion: it can be true by coincidence—there is correspondence, but no attainment. The correspondence of a record is objective not because the record has left the witness, but because its content is held by logic—for everyone.

The words “objective” and “subjective” have two applications. **By field:** data lie in the objective field; reading, information, and record lie in the witness’s subjective field. **By correspondence:** a representation corresponds to what is subjectively—within what is given to the witness: this is truth; or objectively—one for all positions: this is absolute truth. The field of a record is always subjective; what can be objective is its correspondence.

| | The objective | The subjective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **By field** | structure and data | reading, information, record |
| **By correspondence** | absolute truth: one for all positions | truth: within what is given to the witness |

**Confidence is an estimate of probability:** the witness gives it, and it lies in the witness’s field.

Movement along the ladder has a direction: the rungs go in order. What leads to objective correspondence is the same orientation and path as in the theme [[Truth and Absolute Truth]]: one can coincide with absolute truth by chance; one can come to it only by a path.

> [!example]- Case: one light—two channels
> One and the same light enters an eye with ordinary color vision and the eye of a color-blind person. The data are identical: the light is one. The information differs: the second eye takes fewer differences from it. Data and reading in one example: the entrance is objective, the middle is positional. A spectrometer gives a spectrum—but the instrument does not cancel the ladder, it widens the channel: what it shows is again read by a witness, and the objective is determined from the data by a chain of grounds—through the position of the witness.

**Agreement is a sign, not a criterion.** The criterion of the objective is independence from position: whether what has been determined stays the same when the position is changed and the relation of the positions is taken into account. Agreement deceives where those who agree look from one place.

## The Witness Is Subjective: Objectivity Is an Orientation

**The whole witness, as a system, is subjective.** Everything by which it knows—perception, reasoning, records—is its own and lies in its field.

**Objectivity is the witness’s orientation toward what does not depend on its position.** There is no special organ for it; the orientation is twofold:

- with respect to what is—**honesty**: the orientation toward truth ([[Truth and Absolute Truth#Honesty|honesty]]);
- with respect to itself—**taking its own position into account**: the context of the meeting, the channel, and the threshold. The data pass through the witness, and the witness takes this into account.

In reasoning the same orientation is held by the Principle of Objectivity: one always looks through a framework, and one chooses it by the nature of the phenomenon and by the goal—not to fit the desired result ([[3 Framework Selection|Framework Selection]]).

**The witness always looks from its own position.** An objective view is a view of the build of the system: of what does not depend on position. One looks at the build, too, from a position; the witness reaches the build by reasoning, along a chain of grounds: the path to the objective goes through the subjective.

**The mechanics of the orientation is parallax.** Two positions give two givens; when the relation between the positions is known, the divergence of the givens is a measurement, and from it one takes what does not depend on position—the invariant ([[Truth and Absolute Truth#Case: Parallax|the case “Parallax”]]). A second position is obtained by changing one’s own—or is given by another witness.

> [!example]- Case: the “view from nowhere”—the name of a task
> The name was given by Thomas Nagel: *The View from Nowhere* (1986).[^4] For him objectivity is “a method of understanding,” and it has degrees: “a view or form of thought is more objective than another if it relies less on the specifics of the individual’s makeup and position in the world…” Nagel names the limit himself: “reality is not just objective reality,” and an objective picture must include an acknowledgment of its own incompleteness. On this page every view has a position: the “view from nowhere” is a construction from the registry of the impossible ([[What Is Not and Cannot Be|the view from nowhere]]). The series of ever more objective views is real: objectivity is a direction of movement from one’s own place, not a place outside all places.

A special case is the view of oneself: the witness looks at its own build only from within itself—it has no full outside view of itself—and here it needs another witness ([[Metaphysics#The Witness|the limit of the witness]]). An objective view of oneself is a labor of its own, and it begins with acknowledging one’s own subjectivity.

## Two Edges: Naive Objectivism and Relativism

Two symmetrical errors—both about the objective view:

- **Naive objectivism** (“I see it as it is,” “the data speak for themselves”) **appropriates** it: it takes how the system is given from here for the build of the system. The data are indeed objective—but they are not taken “raw”: only through oneself.
- **Relativism** (“everything is subjective,” “everyone has their own truth”) **abolishes** it: as though one could look only at how things are given from here, and there were no build common to all. It declares correspondence within the given to be the limit—and loses absolute truth.

Both errors are one substitution, in opposite directions: objectivism passes off subjective correspondence as objective—a given of a position as absolute truth ([[Truth and Absolute Truth#The Potential and Projection|three substitutions of status]]); relativism reduces objective correspondence to subjective—absolute truth to a given of a position.

> [!example]- Case: “everyone has their own truth”—a formula that is half right
> Truth is how it is; it is no one’s, but in a meeting it is given to each from that one’s position: honest witnesses say how it is from here. Two eyes see the thumb at different places on the wall ([[Truth and Absolute Truth#Case: Parallax|the case “Parallax”]]); two groups honestly named different colors of the dress ([[1 Recognition|the case of the dress]]). Absolute truth is objective truth: how exactly things are; it is one for all positions. The formula is right as a word about position and becomes relativism when it is used to abolish absolute truth. Here, too, belongs “the agreement of the majority”: before Copernicus almost everyone agreed in seeing the Sun go around the Earth—and all of them looked from one Earth. Agreement is a sign of the objective, and a deceptive one.

**Honesty holds both edges at once:** the objective is there, and one can reach it—by a path, from one’s own position; one always begins from a position, and so the objective is attained, not received ready-made.

## Summary

1. **The objective is what does not depend on the witness:** one for all positions. The subjective is how what exists is given to a witness from its position.
2. **The objective as a view is a view of the build of the system;** the subjective as a view is a view of how the system is given from here.
3. **The subjective is positional, not “unreliable”:** a position is the place from which one begins.
4. **The objective is two-faced:** structure is open to reasoning, data are presented to experience.
5. **The words have two applications.** By field: data lie in the objective field; reading, information, and record, in the subjective one. By correspondence: truth is subjective, absolute truth objective. The field of a record is always subjective; what can be objective is its correspondence.
6. **The whole witness is subjective; objectivity is its orientation** toward what does not depend on position: honesty and taking its own position into account. The mechanics of the orientation is parallax.
7. **The criterion of the objective is independence from position;** agreement is a sign, not a criterion.
8. **Two edges:** naive objectivism appropriates the objective view, relativism abolishes it; honesty holds both edges at once.
9. **Confidence is an estimate of probability.**
