The Law of Order: ***≺ , ⊏*** · ***ordo***

**Logic has an inherent sequence and hierarchy—everything stands in its order and on its level.**

Order here is a law: the property of Logic by which what is distinguished stands in sequence and by levels. The law is unbreakable. The existing order of things—an arrangement that is kept or broken—is the subject of [[Ethics|ethics]].

## Etymology

**Order.** The English word comes through French from Latin *ordo*, a weaver’s word: *ordior* means “to lay the warp of a web”; *ordo* is a row of threads. The Russian *poryadok* comes from *ryad*, “a row”: order is what stands in a row. Greek κόσμος (kosmos) meant first “arrangement, order,” then “world”: the world is named after its order. Both meta-laws—Sufficient Reason and Order—are named in weavers’ words: the warp (Russian *osnova*, whence *osnovanie*, “ground”) and *ordo*.

**Hierarchy.** The word was given its present sense by Pseudo-Dionysius: ἱερός (hieros), “sacred,” and ἀρχή (archē), “beginning, principle.” He defined it through order: hierarchy is “a sacred order”—τάξις ἱερά (taxis hiera).[^1]

## Place among the Laws

Among the five laws ([[Logic]]), Order is the **second meta-law**, the law of structuredness itself; its pair is [[Sufficient Reason]]. [[Identity]], [[Non-Contradiction]], and [[Excluded Middle]] say *what* must hold in a distinction; Sufficient Reason says *why* it holds; Order says *how*: in what sequence and on what level the distinguished stands. It is irreducible to the first three: stability, exclusivity, and exhaustiveness are silent about the directionality of distinction and about the dependence of later distinctions on earlier ones. Its property is *ordering*.

The law can be read in distinction itself. The distinguished and the background occupy asymmetrical positions: *A* is what is marked out, not-*A* is what remains; there is a direction—from the undistinguished to the distinguished. And later distinctions depend on earlier ones: the distinction of *B* within *A* presupposes the distinction of *A*.[^2] Hence two principles—two dimensions of one law.

- **The Principle of Sequence** (the horizontal, ≺) sets the direction: the stages form an ordered series, and each creates the conditions for the next; arbitrary reordering is impossible.
- **The Principle of Hierarchy** (the vertical, ⊏) sets the levels: elements, operations on elements, operations on operations. Each level presupposes the one below; the higher determines the conditions for the lower.

Identity makes differentiation possible, Non-Contradiction makes interaction possible, and Order makes **becoming** possible: what is distinguished stands in a sequence of steps and in a hierarchy of levels. Without Order there would be states but no transitions. As without Sufficient Reason nothing is grounded, so without Order nothing is arranged.

## History

Both lines of the law have a long history; here they are brought together into one law.

**The line of hierarchy**—“above what and out of what.” Plato laid out an ascent: from shadows to things, from things to the Forms, from the Forms to the Good; each step is clearer and more real than the one before.[^3] Aristotle ranked the categories by priority: primary substance lies at the base, and everything else is said of it or is in it—“were there no primary substances, nothing else could exist.”[^4] Porphyry set out a ladder of genera and species—from substance down to man; the Middle Ages drew it as the “tree of Porphyry.”[^5] Pseudo-Dionysius gave this line its name. Out of it grew the “great chain of being”—a picture of the world as an unbroken ladder of beings. Linnaeus arranged the living by ranks—class, order (*ordo*), genus, species (1735); Darwin explained why the ranks are nested one within another: “all true classification is genealogical.”[^6]

**The line of sequence**—“after what and through what.” In Heraclitus everything comes to pass “according to the logos,” and the cosmos is “an ever-living fire, kindling in measures and going out in measures.”[^7] In Leibniz every state of a monad follows from the one before: “the present is big with the future.”[^8] In Hegel the categories pass into one another by necessity; the method is “the course of the matter itself.”[^9]

**Order in knowing.** Aristotle required six things of the premises of a demonstration: they must be true, primary, immediate, better known than the conclusion, prior to it, and its causes. Five of the six—all but truth—concern order.[^10] Descartes made order the third rule of his method: “to conduct my thoughts in order, beginning with the objects that are simplest and easiest to know.”[^11] Kant: “All our cognition starts from the senses, goes from there to the understanding, and ends with reason”; to reason he ascribed a law of seeking unity, and the art of constructing systems he called architectonic.[^12] Frege required chains of inference without gaps and sorted functions into levels: a first-level function takes objects, a second-level function takes functions.[^13] Husserl described founding: one content exists only on the basis of another.[^14]

**Hierarchy took rigorous form in the twentieth century.** Russell stated the vicious-circle principle—“Whatever involves *all* of a collection must not be one of the collection”—and built the theory of types.[^15] Tarski showed that truth for a language is defined in a metalanguage, and that the metalanguage must be essentially richer.[^16] Both came to the same result: level confusion yields an error of construction; hierarchy is a condition of consistency. For both it remained a remedy against paradoxes. Here sequence and hierarchy are two dimensions of one law of logic: a meta-law, paired with Sufficient Reason.

## What the Law Makes Possible

In being, the law gives **the relation between what is distinguished**—the influence of one on another, their interaction, their sequence, and their levels—and thereby makes **becoming** possible. From this come three things that are not there without Order. **The link between cause and effect:** the cause is given by [[Sufficient Reason]]; that the cause precedes the effect and acts on it is the work of Order. **Time:** the sequence of acts of distinction; its direction is the direction in which distinctions accumulate ([[Metaphysics]]). **The hierarchy of the whole:** the whole sets the conditions for the parts—organs work as organs only in an organism, words acquire meaning in a sentence.

In knowing, three things stand on this.

**Reasoning.** The conclusion comes after the grounds. Reasoning passes through six [[Domains of Reasoning|domains]]—recognition, clarification, framework selection, comparison, inference, reflection—and each supplies the material for the next. Skipping a domain leaves the later ones without support; reordering puts the conclusion before its grounds.

**The criterion.** The criterion is established before the question of membership: one can ask whether a thing belongs to a system once the system has been defined. This is the principle of Criterion Precedence ([[Logic]]).

**The distinction of levels.** A statement and what it is about stand on different levels; an operation works on an object and is never its own object. Hence, applied to systems, the Theory of Systems’ principle of Hierarchy: a system is never an element of itself. There are levels within the domains as well: recognition moves along a ladder—data → information → qualities → characteristics—and each rung stands on the one before ([[1 Recognition]]).

Order is a property of being, like the other laws. The objection “Identity and Non-Contradiction are about being, Order only about knowing” starts from a division that is removed here: if logic is the structure without which there is no reality, then sequence and hierarchy are as much its features as stability and exclusivity. The domains of reasoning are a structure of knowing that *corresponds* to the structure of being: consciousness knows through order because reality has order. An error of order is therefore a departure from reality.

Order **liberates**. Reasoning chaotically, we serve our prejudices: we find what we sought because we were seeking precisely that; conclusions turn out to be projections. Beginning with recognition, we open ourselves to what *is*. The chess player moves freely within the rules of the game; the reasoner concludes freely within the order of the domains. The discipline of order is a path to absolute truth. An experienced mind *compresses* the order: it passes through the obvious in an instant; errors arise where the compression has thrown out something necessary.

## Order and the Assignment of Statuses

**Order gives the places.** “First,” “last,” “least” have meaning only within an order. Assignment answers to a ground ([[Sufficient Reason]]). When several candidates fit one role, the ground is the place in the order: the status goes to the first that fits. This is the very definition of the status: “the point of maximum” means “the first of the points with the greatest value.” The axiom of choice asserts that a choice exists; Order gives the place, and there is nothing left to choose. This is how the extreme value theorem is proved: on a plateau, where the greatest value is taken at several points, the first of them is assigned as the point of maximum ([[Formalization]]).

## Self-Confirmation

The Law of Order cannot be meaningfully denied without being applied in the very act of denial. To say “there is no order in reasoning,” one must recognize what is at issue, clarify what is being asserted, relate it to a framework, and draw a conclusion—that is, pass through the order. Intuition grasps in an instant, yet it too passes through the order: the dependence here is logical, and the temporal one is its shadow. One cannot evaluate what one has not understood, or understand what one has not recognized. The law was discovered, not invented: what is invented can be repealed.

Laws are not the only things that confirm themselves: the link between cause and effect cannot be rejected without causal links in the argument itself, and time cannot be rejected outside time. That does not make them laws. Time is a manifestation of Order; the link between cause and effect rests on both meta-laws: the cause is given by Sufficient Reason, the relation by Order. The laws differ in status: Identity and Non-Contradiction are primary, neither derivable from the other; Excluded Middle is derived from them; Sufficient Reason and Order are meta-laws.

## The Preservation of Distinctions

Distinctions, once made, are recorded: Order is a **law of preservation**. A distinction cannot be unmade. It would have to be determinate, so that there is something to unmake, and indeterminate, so as to be absent—against [[Non-Contradiction]]; and it would have to cease being itself—against [[Identity]]. Irreversibility follows from three laws together: Identity, Non-Contradiction, and Order. Hence time has an arrow: distinctions are only added.

> [!note]- The preservation of distinctions in physics
> Preservation has three readings, each stronger than the one before.
>
> - **Count**—the *number* of realized distinctions does not decrease. This is already enough for the second law of thermodynamics.
> - **Content**—each *particular* distinction is preserved. From this come the conservation laws, the conservation of information, and the reliability of memory.
> - **Structure**—the *relations* between distinctions are preserved too: the finer remains the finer. From this comes the link with Noether’s theorem and gauge invariance.
>
> The first law of thermodynamics (conservation) and the second (the growth of entropy) turn out to be readings of one law: entropy grows because nothing is lost while new distinctions are added. The same removes the circle discussed by Wolpert, Rovelli, and Scharnhorst: the second law is derived from the reliability of memory, and the reliability of memory from the second law.[^17] Here both are parallel consequences of one law.
>
> Each distinction divides everything exactly in two, that is, it yields **one bit**. *K* independent distinctions yield 2<sup>*K*</sup> microstates, and Boltzmann’s entropy is obtained by derivation: *S* = *k*<sub>B</sub> · *K* · ln 2. For independent distinctions the formula is exact; for correlated ones it gives an upper bound. Landauer’s principle—erasing a bit costs no less than *k*<sub>B</sub>*T* · ln 2 of heat—reads the same way: in “erasure” a distinction is not destroyed but carried over into the environment.[^18] A qubit in superposition is a distinction not yet made; measurement is its actualization.
>
> The status of these consequences is stated in the author’s article: formal verification confirms the inference, not the premises. What is proved is this: *if* distinctions are preserved, then entropy does not decrease, conservation holds, and time has an arrow. That Order operates in physical reality is a philosophical argument.

## In Ethics

The law is unbreakable; what can be broken is the existing order—and it is entrusted to free choice. From this come two propositions of [[Ethics|ethics]]. *The Law of Conformity:* to conform to the rules is to maintain order. *Violation of order:* a wrong choice is a violation of the existing order; the rules themselves are unbreakable. The order of reasoning is therefore a demand of both logic and ethics: to begin with the conclusion and pick grounds to fit it is to pass off untruth as truth.

## In the Theory of Systems

The triad of a system—Rules, Roles, Elements—is derived from the laws: rules correspond to Order, roles to Sufficient Reason, elements to Identity. The rules are the order of the system: the structure that makes a multiplicity a system ([[Theory of Systems]]). Three principles of the Theory of Systems also stand on Order: Hierarchy, Criterion Precedence, and Finite Actuality ([[Logic]]).

## Violations

In reality the law is unbreakable; it is broken in thinking. What is broken is the existing order of reasoning; the law itself stays in force, which is why the violation yields an error. There is one mechanism: correct operations go in the wrong order or on the wrong level. An open contradiction is visible. A violation of Order yields no open contradiction; it yields wandering: people think they are reasoning and go round in circles. That is why the law remained without a name: its violations were seen but called by different names. There are three forms.

*The skipped stage.* A stage is not passed, or not finished, and the next stands without support: the conclusion is drawn before the comparison is complete. Such are [[Overgeneralization|hasty generalization]] and [[Non Sequitur|non sequitur]]; from the side of the ground, non sequitur is called the gap ([[Sufficient Reason]]).

*Reverse movement.* Reasoning runs from the conclusion to the grounds: the conclusion already lies in the premise ([[Circular Reasoning|the vicious circle]]), or the grounds are picked to fit a ready answer ([[A Priori Argument|rationalization]]; from the side of the ground it is treated on the page [[Sufficient Reason]]).

*Level confusion.* An operation takes itself as its object; the organizer is identified with the organized. This is how [[Russell's Paradox]] and the [[Liar Paradox|Liar]] are built. It is an error of construction: in a well-formed statement it is impossible ([[Paradox Catalog]]).

The skipped stage and reverse movement are violations along the horizontal; level confusion is a violation along the vertical. The horizontal ones are treated in the fallacy catalog, which distinguishes five forms: [[03 Sequence Violations|“Sequence Violations”]].

## Summary

1. **The Law of Order: *ordo***—what is distinguished stands in its order and on its level. Its property is ordering; it is the second meta-law, paired with Sufficient Reason.
2. **Two principles—two dimensions of one law:** Sequence sets the direction, Hierarchy the levels.
3. **It makes becoming possible:** it gives the relation between what is distinguished. The cause is given by Sufficient Reason; that the cause precedes the effect and acts on it is the work of Order. Time is the sequence of acts of distinction; the whole sets the conditions for the parts. In knowing, three things stand on it: reasoning, the criterion, and the distinction of levels. Order is a property of being: an error of order is a departure from reality.
4. **Order gives the places:** assignment answers to a ground; where several candidates fit a role, the status goes to the first that fits. In a system, the rules correspond to Order.
5. **The law confirms itself:** order can be denied only by passing through order. Time and the link between cause and effect confirm themselves too; that does not make them laws: time is a manifestation of Order, and the link rests on both meta-laws.
6. **Order is a law of preservation:** distinctions, once made, are recorded.
7. **In reality the law is unbreakable.** What can be broken is the existing order of things, and that is the subject of ethics. In thinking, the existing order of reasoning is broken in three ways: the skipped stage, reverse movement, and level confusion.

Stability, exclusivity, exhaustiveness, self-grounding, and ordering are five aspects of one act of distinction. For the overview of the branch and the link between the five laws, see [[Logic]]. Next, on the one who reasons by these laws: [[Philosophy]].
