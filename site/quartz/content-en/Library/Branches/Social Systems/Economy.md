## Two Goals

The economy has two goals—directly connected, yet distinct: **(a) the creation of systems of joint work** and **(b) the distribution of resources**. The first is responsible for building the social structure of people—in capitalism this is the company, in socialism, associations of workers; the second, for the financial system. These two systems are defined and built **separately**, and the economy is a **derivative**: a system born of their interaction—by the canon of the trunk, the interaction of systems gives birth to a new system in which both are represented ([[Theory of Systems]]).

## The System of Joint Work: The Guilds

**The seed.** The goal of the system of joint work is optimal organization for maximizing efficiency. Who creates? The human being. Therefore maximum efficiency is achieved through the **realization of the potential of each** element-person.

**Two observations.** From the way a human being is arranged come two observations, not rules. The first: **attention per step is limited**—there cannot be many directions of activity: two or three are possible, ten are not; there would not be enough attention to devote to each. Hence **focus**: a definite key direction is the main function; additional ones are possible—with a different relationship of roles. And hence, too, the division of labor—a consequence of the way attention is arranged, not a postulate of practice. The second: one **does not know from the outset** in which occupation one will realize oneself most fully—hence the bridge task to the system of [[Education|education]]: to help determine the key occupation of each person.

**The single field—a derivation.** The task requires that the potential of each be realized—therefore maximal connections between the elements are required—therefore the elements must be **in a single field**: separated only by grouping within the field, not by the partitions of operating systems. A company limits the field of potential interaction—and thereby limits the efficiency of those very companies, the working systems. A working group is a grouping in a single field, not a wall: a group is a construction by a rule of formation, not a separate system.

**The Guild.** The system for building work in a single field we call the **Guild**: the uniting of people of one key occupation-interest into a single system. The Guild directly organizes the process of work and creation; building it is yet another system, and the rules stand on two levels. **The system-forming level**: its elements are the Guilds themselves, and there is no process of selecting elements here—Guilds arise by the rules of building; the architecture of this level is led by the Guild of Philosophers—in its function as the architects of social systems. **The internal level**: a Guild once built has its own elements—members—and its own rules of assignment to roles (how a candidate becomes a member); here there is self-governance.

**The rules of building.** A Guild arises around a direction of creation; **one Guild per direction**—two Guilds of one interest would restore a partition within the field; the division is recursive—a direction divides according to the nature of the work, and by the same rule nested subsystems arise within it, down to working groups. The levels of belonging follow geography: global—continent—country—region and city.

**Self-governance.** The operator of a Guild is its members themselves: one vote per member with the right to delegate, specialized committees by area, decision by vote, and everything is transparent—a hidden operator would be a withholding of absolute truth. The mechanism of voting is legitimate here because it is relative to the system—and the system is defined.

**Connections between Guilds.** Relationships—by the trunk’s canon of interaction. **Belonging**: all Guilds are elements of the system-forming level; they are not directly linked with one another but are represented in the common structure. **Link**: a joint undertaking of two Guilds—an undertaking that requires several directions—gives birth to a new system: a joint project, a working group drawn from different Guilds; the single field allows this directly. **Intersection in a common element**: a person with additional directions is a member of several Guilds; that person’s attention is divided by a queue of presence, and the Guilds intersect without merging. Disputes go to the nearest common encompassing system.

**The relation to the financial system.** Resources—materials, labor, spaces—live and move in the working systems; the financial system does not move resources—it keeps the record: it reads statuses and directs the movement of resources by its records. The financial system’s own analysis is a separate one and lies ahead: the two systems are built separately.

## The Financial System

The task is the distribution of resources: to bring resources to the tasks, so that every undertaking has the means to be carried out. The analysis lies ahead.
