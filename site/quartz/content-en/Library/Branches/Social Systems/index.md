Social systems are systems that are built: in them the connection of rules, roles, and elements changes through the action of an operator. The branch analyzes their arrangement—from the distinction between an open and a closed system to the structure of the Guilds and the flows of distribution; the groundwork is the [[Theory of Systems|triad]] and [[Ethics|the derivation of ethics]], where justice has already been read as order on a scale of many operators.

## Etymology

“Social” is from Latin *socialis* “allied, companionable” ← *socius* “companion, comrade”: the root \*sekʷ- “to follow”—a *socius* is **one who goes with another**. A social system is, literally, a system of companions. The Russian name holds the other side: *obshchestvo* “society,” *obshchina* “community,” *obshchij* “common”—from a Proto-Slavic root meaning “around”: **the common is what encompasses all**. Two names—two sides of one thing: going together—in a common, encompassing field.

## Definition

In the usual reading, a social system is a multitude of people bound by stable relations and rules: a family, a workshop, a city, a state. The reading is correct, but it gives no distinguishing feature: every system has rules and relations. The analysis will give the difference.

## The Open System and Its Operator

A system is held by its rules; but elements come to their roles in two ways. In some systems the selection is given and takes place by itself, by the laws of the encompassing cluster—such are natural, “automatic” systems. In others the connection of rules, roles, and elements changes only through the action of an **[[Metaphysics|operator]]**—such are human, “manual” systems. The difference runs where **[[Metaphysics|will]]** enters the selection: a closed system is determined by law, an open one is led by choice.

Hence two consequences, on which everything further rests. Since social systems are open—people themselves lay down the rules, set up the roles, and make the selection for them—these systems **bear responsibility**: where there is choice, there is accountability. And they **can be put together wrongly**, unlike natural ones, which simply are: a social system is something that is built—and so also something that can be built with an error. Hence its first question is the **[[Theory of Systems|task]]**: what it is for. For an open system the operator chooses the rules to fit the task, and the task is the ground of the whole triad—of rules, roles, and elements.

## The Analysis of the System

The analysis follows the generating order—from the seed of the system.

The **goal** is the seed of the system: what it is for. In a social system the goal is laid down by people themselves—which is why it is open to its first question; the task is the ground of the whole triad. The **rules** come from the goal: they are laid down by the operators themselves to fit the task. The **roles** are specified by the rules: places of service, of work, and of coordination (three kinds of places—[[Charter]], Article 3). The **elements** are the people who fill the roles; and each element here is itself a system with its own goal and will: what comes to a role is a whole operator.

A peculiarity of the triad: **the element and the operator coincide.** Those who stand in the roles themselves lay down the goal and the rules—which is why the selection is conducted by choice and not by the law of the encompassing cluster.

## Our Definition

**A social system is an open system built by its own element-operators: the connection of rules, roles, and elements in it changes by will.** Everything else on this page consists of consequences: responsibility and the possibility of error, the task as the ground of the triad, gardener governance, the Guilds, the flows.

## The Systems

Six systems are analyzed in depth—each on its own page, each by its own task:

- **[[Economy]]**—creation and provision: production and distribution.
- **[[Politics]]**—the coordination of the common field: roles and selection; gardener governance.
- **[[Law]]**—the maintenance of rules and the resolution of disputes: routing along the axes.
- **[[Education]]**—the transmission of knowledge: education free of charge.
- **[[Technologies]]**—the creation and development of the means by which systems carry out their tasks.
- **[[Spaces]]**—the organization of social spaces: the houses of the Guilds; creating spaces for socialization is one of the functions of a Guild.

## Governance as a Gardener

The central one among the social systems is **governance**: its task is to define the roles and to make the selection for them so that the potential of each person unfolds most fully—each in one’s own place, by calling. But potential cannot be measured in advance: self-knowledge has a limit, and outside evaluation is incomplete. What needs to be known is not the potential but the **pull**—what the person is drawn to, within which system the person wants to perform a role. Potential is a process, not a ready-made quantity: it **unfolds** through doing and grows in the direction of the pull.

Hence the nature of governance changes. Since one places oneself, by one’s own pull, and potential unfolds in the work, governance ceases to be an assessor-distributor. Its work is to maintain a rich space of roles, to provide for movement (to try, to follow one’s pull, to change places), and to give feedback. It **cultivates the field rather than placing people**—a gardener, not an allocator. There is one objective yardstick here: analysis gives only a **threshold**, the minimum for entering a role without doing harm; above the threshold the pull chooses, and “to measure” is the outcome of unfolding, not a condition of entry.[^1]

And the realization of each serves the whole as its mechanism; it does not contend with the whole. A system performs its function by each element being in its own role; the [[Order|order]] of a system is precisely fitting elements in fitting roles.[^2] Hence “each by calling” and “the system works” are one requirement taken on two tiers, not two different ones. And since each system has its own task, society has no single overarching goal: it is a living multiplicity of systems, each with its own, not a monolith with one meaning. What they share is form, not goal: whatever the task, it is achieved by fitting elements in the roles.

## The Guild

Since a person is drawn to a kind of activity, systems naturally gather around interests. A **Guild** is an association of people with a common interest, the parent system for everything done in that interest. Its criterion is to serve its interest; one enters it by a pull toward that interest; the roles within are to teach, to practice, to judge by knowledge of the matter, to create; it is filled by the people whom the pull has brought, and by the fruits of their work.

The house of a Guild is its space: creating spaces for socialization is one of the functions of a Guild ([[Spaces]]).

With the Guild a second axis of the arrangement opens. A person is always in two lineages at once: by **competence** (the Guild) and by **place** (house—district—region). These axes are orthogonal, and the division of decisions falls along them: what requires **knowledge of the matter** is decided by interest, in the Guild; what concerns **living together**—the disposal of a local resource, taxes from the house up to the region—remains with the systems of place. Every decision goes to the encompassing system within whose sphere it falls.

## Functions: Education, Law, Creation

**Education free of charge.** Knowledge is absolute truth; to withhold absolute truth for the sake of advantage is to create a field for illusion, and this is a distortion of absolute truth, not service to it—untruth turned against the will of others for one’s own gain.[^3] Hence access to knowledge is free of payment out of necessity, not out of kindness: a paywall on absolute truth is a distortion. Access is free—but labor is not unpaid: the role of the teacher is sustained by the Guild’s network of mutual provision.

**Law on two axes.** One half of it—decisions of knowledge—goes to the Guild, by expertise; the other—decisions of living together—remains with the systems of place. Law here is routing, not a single code from above: each case goes to its competent tier.

**Creation instead of competition.** The economy remains a function of the Guild but changes its structure. The engine of production is the **will to create**, not competition: competition was only an external surrogate for it, at times even stifling the will with the fear of scarcity. Align a person’s inner will, give the opportunity to create—and the engine runs directly. Hence, instead of vertical firms fighting for a share, there is horizontal work within the Guild, where knowledge is common.

Price remains; what changes is its formation: it is **set** by the Guild itself—production cost plus a fair markup, by decision of its members, with the production cost open. The markup does not go to a dividend recipient: it sustains the common functions—the teacher, the network of provision—and flows on.

Here lies the root of the difference. The poison of any system is the **recipient of profit** at its center, not profit itself: then the task of the system is replaced by extraction, and since the recipients compete through their sources, every system is forced to extract or perish, and the tasks are distorted all at once.[^3] Profit remains but changes direction: not extraction into dividends but **distribution** along the nesting—into the common bank of all the Guilds, into the cluster, into the Guild itself, down to passive payouts to members. Passive support is precisely the condition of the will-to-create: remove the fear about earning a living—and the will is free to create.

Investment flows in the same way. Part of the surplus goes into the bank of its level, and from it come new projects, each according to its level and role; a project gathers financing in a cascade: first the local bank, then upward—to larger banks—and outward—to adjacent Guilds with an interest, then to the investments of individual people; if it falls short along the whole chain, it is canceled or revised on the basis of detailed feedback, with which each committee explains its decision. Thus dispersed demand is gathered by a network of assessors rather than by a single price; and the price of capital is given by the common bank, not by the market.

Nor is reward for a contribution rejected. Whoever has brought an underfunded project to success has earned a share; distortion enters through **accumulation for the sake of status**—capital frozen into a sign—not through reward. Flowing capital, going again and again into creation, serves order; capital frozen for the sake of status is parasitic. Hence the capital-reward is held by a separate entity, an investment account with its own rules that keep it in the flow, and by a culture that sees accumulation-for-status as a distortion. The wealthy person remains—but such a person’s wealth is a manifestation of talent and the capacity to direct resources into creation, not a store of status; and such a person’s power is supplementary, bound by the flow, turned toward creation. Equality is rejected here for the sake of the uniqueness of each: what is adjusted is the flow, not the level.

## Governance and Coordination

Within a Guild the operator is its members themselves: one vote per member, with the right to delegate, specialized committees by area, decision by vote. The operator here is a role filled by a multitude, and a replaceable one; delegation is the fluid transfer of one’s share in it; and everything is transparent, for a hidden operator would be a withholding of absolute truth—the same distortion as a paywall on knowledge.

Above the field of Guilds stands the **Guild of Philosophers**—of those who are drawn to the very structure of systems. Philosophy is precisely knowledge of structure; hence the Guild of this interest naturally leads the architecture of the whole—a coordinator that keeps the systems in accord but does not enter into the internal rules of each. It is above the field by arrangement, not by power: above only in architecture, open in membership, replaceable in its operator, transparent in its work—hence a Guild of its own interest, not an estate. So the thought about philosophy comes true in deed: the structure of systems is led by those whose pull is toward structure (the roles and obligations of the philosopher—[[Code of Ethics]]).

A conflict of systems is resolved by the same law of level: any two disputing systems have a nearest common encompassing system, and the dispute goes to it—no higher. Hence the diseases, too, are seen as level confusion: a higher system that has entered into the affairs of a lower one is overreach; the seizure of the encompassing system by a part of it is usurpation; and the protection against it is that the operator is replaceable.

## Synthesis

Everything in this arrangement is routed in the same way: by the level of nesting, with deference to the lower one, by the decision of its own community. This is how decisions and disputes go, how distribution flows, how capital is gathered. The arrangement turns out self-similar, fractal: each tier is a system with its own operator, its own bank, its own projects, deciding and nourishing at its own level. And everything rests on a single ground, not on a scheme invented from above: order is fitting elements in fitting roles, and it is also the right (the rightful).[^2] Hence the social organization here is not capitalism—for the recipient-accumulator has been driven out of the center—and not leveling—for the uniqueness of each is accepted; but a third: a structure in which the will to create is aligned, potential unfolds by calling, and the surplus flows without stagnating.
